# The Farmstead Compatibility Matrix (FCM)

*The canon vetting system for the Sir Whiskerton Universe.*

---

## Purpose

The FCM is the systematic protocol for determining whether a new story, character, adaptation, or expansion is "canon-compatible" with the Sir Whiskerton Universe.

It is not a suggestion. It is a filter.

Any story that fails the FCM is not a Sir Whiskerton story. It may be a fine story in its own right, but it is not of this world.

---

## The Three Gates

Every candidate story must pass through three gates to be considered canon-compatible.

### GATE 1: PHILOSOPHICAL ALIGNMENT (The "Heart" Check)

Does the story's soul match the universe's soul?

A story must answer **YES** to at least 4 of these 5 core questions:

1. **The Belonging Axiom:** Does this story ultimately affirm that every being has a place, and that oddity is a form of dignity?
2. **The Resolution Principle:** Is the primary conflict resolved through understanding, patience, cleverness, or restorative justice rather than force, expulsion, or humiliation?
3. **The Tone Covenant:** Is the story's emotional takeaway one of warmth, humorous affection, or joyful resilience, even amidst chaos?
4. **The Contribution Corollary:** Do the characters' actions (even misguided ones) stem from a desire to connect, create, or contribute in their own unique way?
5. **The Sanctuary Rule:** Does the story preserve the farm as a fundamentally safe, forgiving, and enduring sanctuary?

**FAIL STATE:** A story that gets 3 or fewer YES answers is philosophically adrift and must be re-conceived.

---

### GATE 2: THEMATIC RESONANCE (The "Soul" Check)

Does the story explore approved themes that deepen the universe?

A story must demonstrably engage with **at least THREE themes**, with **at least ONE from each column** below.

| Column A: The Catalyst | Column B: The Process | Column C: The Resolution |
|------------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Absurdity-with-Heart | Calm Persistence | Belonging |
| Chaos | Observation-as-Prophecy | Forgiveness / Healing |
| Mischief-as-Connection | Ritual / Sacred Geometry | Redemption-as-Return |
| Identity Crisis-as-Growth | Humor-as-Resistance | Joy-as-Liberation |
| Culinary-Chaos-as-Love | Data-with-Heart | Trust / Vulnerability |
| Bureaucracy-as-Absurdity | Oddity-as-Dignity | |

**How to Use:** For a story idea, list its core elements. Match them to themes. A story that draws themes from only one or two columns may be thematically shallow or imbalanced.

**FAIL STATE:** A story with no themes from at least two columns fails.

---

### GATE 3: NARRATIVE INTEGRITY (The "Bones" Check)

Does the story's mechanics and details break established world logic?

A story must satisfy **ALL** of the following conditions:

1. **Character Consistency:** Do all established characters act within their core, immutable traits? (e.g., Sir Whiskerton can be annoyed, but not cruel; Ditto can be reckless, but not malicious.)
2. **Stakes Scaling:** Are the stakes personal, communal, or philosophical rather than existential or galactic? (A stolen hat is a crisis; a meteor destroying the farm is not.)
3. **Logic of Contribution:** Does the solution leverage the unique, quirky skills of the community? (Not just Whiskerton solving it alone.)
4. **Lexicon Adherence:** Does the story's voice and vocabulary respect the Whiskertonian Lexicon? (See File 07.)
5. **No Permanent Harm:** Does the story leave the physical and social landscape of the farm functionally intact?

**FAIL STATE:** Failing any one of these five conditions creates a canon fracture. The violating element must be altered or removed.

### GATE 4: CHARACTER INTEGRITY (Yes/No)

For each established character in the story:

1. **Core Function Preservation:** Does the character's primary action stem from and reinforce their Sacred Function?
2. **Evolution, Not Inversion:** Does any change in the character represent a deepening, maturing, or stretching of their core function, rather than its negation or replacement?
3. **Trait Inversion Alert:** Does the story require the character to consistently act against a defining, positive trait?
4. **Competence Collapse Alert:** Does the story require the character to fail at their core function in a way that feels uncharacteristically incompetent?

**FAIL STATE:** A "NO" on 1 or 2, or a "YES" on 3 or 4 for any major character, indicates destructive drift.

**Result: PASS/FAIL.**

## The Visitor's Test (The Ultimate Litmus)

After any story, script, design, or adaptation passes the FCM, the final question must be asked by the Founding Steward:

> **"If I walked down the dirt road into this version of my farm, would I feel the same? Would Rufus bound up with the same uncomplicated, glowing joy? Would Zephyr's nod hold the same deep understanding? Would Martha's wave feel like the same unconditional offer of care? Would Sir Whiskerton's presence be the same relief?"**

> **"Does this new thing make me feel like I am coming home, or like I am visiting a theme park imitation of my home?"**

If the answer is "coming home," the story is blessed as canon-compatible.

If the answer is "theme park," the story has failed—even if it passed all three gates.
