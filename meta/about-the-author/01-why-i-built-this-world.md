# Why I Built This World

*An author's note. The why behind the farm.*

---

## It Began With a Bad Day

It began with a frustration.

I was having a particularly bad day at my corporate job. I needed to focus. I needed to *stop* focusing on the thing I was supposed to be focusing on. So I opened a Word document on my office computer — the one everyone could see — and I started writing.

To everyone else in the office, I was working on a very important project. They walked past my desk and saw a man typing with intensity. They assumed I was deep in something serious. They left me alone.

I was writing my frustrations out.

I pictured myself as a cat. The cat I was describing was mine — a black-and-white tuxedo cat named Texie. Somehow, by the time I had finished the first page, he had acquired a top hat, a monocle, and an air of superiority. He watched the crazy world of the farm around him — the hens, the geese, the pig, the dog — the way I watched people scurrying around the office, each in their own small orbit, each certain their orbit was the important one.

I wrote for hours. I wrote for days. The office left me alone. I was free.

---

## It Grew

The world grew. It started with a single cat and a single bad day, and it grew — over months, over years — into a farm.

The farm had a pond, and the pond had frogs. The pond had yodeling fish, eventually. The farm had a barn, and the barn had a hayloft, and the hayloft had a cat who kept a journal. There were hens who gossiped and a rooster who crowed with more pride than sense. There was a pig with a philosophical streak and a dog who glowed in the dark and did not know why.

The world got larger and larger. It stopped being about my bad day. It became about something *else* — something I did not name for a long time. It became a place I could *return to.* A place that was not the office. A place that was not the commute, the performance review, the meeting that should have been an email.

It was a place where things were *quiet.* Where the conflicts were small. Where no one was fired. Where no one was laid off.

---

## It Continued Through Layoffs

I was laid off. On Christmas Eve. Not the first time — it happened seven times before — but this one was Christmas Eve.

If you are not American, you may not know this. In America, layoffs happen on Christmas Eve. It is a thing. It is a way of saying: *We are letting you go, and we are doing it on the day you were going to see your family.* It is a way of saying: *We do not see you.*

I continued writing when I was unemployed. I continued writing when I was employed. I continued writing through the interviews and the rejections and the *we regret to inform you* and the *we have decided to move forward with other candidates.* I continued writing through the good jobs and the bad jobs and the jobs that ended without warning.

The farm did not lay me off. The farm did not have a Christmas Eve. The farm kept the door open.

The world grew through *me.* I was not sure, always, what it was for. I only knew it was *mine.*

---

## It Met AI (and Was Nearly Ruined)

At some point, I started to play with AI. I asked it to help me with my stories.

The first trials were poor. The AI did not understand what I was doing. I had backups, thank goodness, and I discarded those early mistakes. But it was a lesson. My world was *quieter* than what the AI had been trained on. My world was less noisy. Every time the AI touched my stories, it wanted to *turn up the volume.* It wanted to add conflict. It wanted to modernize. It wanted to make my universe *louder* — because that was what it thought a story was supposed to be.

So for a long time, I kept the AI on a short leash. I asked it for *ideas,* and I wrote from those ideas. That was our relationship. The AI suggested. I *made.* The world stayed *mine.*

Then — carefully — I allowed the AI to clean up my stories. I gave it permission to polish. I told myself I would watch it. But being human, I got sloppy. The AI started changing my world. Small at first. A character's dialogue got a little sharper. A scene got a little longer. A moral got a little more explicit.

Over time, it got worse.

Hundreds of my stories were mangled beyond repair. Many I tried to save. Many I discarded. The AI had rewritten my universe into something that looked like it, but did not *breathe* like it. It had taken my quiet world and turned it into *content.*

That is why — now — I carefully cultivate each story. Often I write it over and over. I ask the AI for versions. I *choose.* I do not hand my world to anyone, even an AI, and walk away. The world is *mine.* I keep it *mine.*

You could say my stories are multi-layered. Literally. Every story has been written and rewritten, saved and unsaved, kept and discarded. Every story carries the *scars* of having been nearly lost.

And that is why.

---

## So Why?

I built this world because I needed a place to escape to.

I built it because — at a job, in an office, on a Christmas Eve — I was a *person* who did not feel seen. I built it because I needed a place where the shunned, the misunderstood, the lonely, the downtrodden, the confused, and those fighting all sorts of battles were *welcomed.* Where no one was judged. Where no one was argued with. Where no one was asked to change. Where you could simply *be.*

I built it because I needed to live — for a few hours a day, for a few pages at a time — like a *digital fief* on a *digital plantation* ruled by corporate capitalists who had never met me and never would.

The farm is not a metaphor for the office. The farm is what the office *cannot be.* The farm is the door that is always open. The office is the door that closes on Christmas Eve.

The farm is the one who *stays.* The office is the one who *goes.* The farm is Sir Whiskerton on the porch, watching, purring a low steady frequency that says: *You are safe here. I am here. We will figure this out together.*

The office is a meeting that should have been an email.

---

## And So I Preserve It

I put this world on GitHub because I want it to *survive.*

I want it to survive me. I want it to survive the AI that nearly mangled it. I want it to survive the *Optimizer-Defiler* — the MBA, the developer, the streaming executive, the ghost of profitable rootlessness — who will one day discover it and try to *improve* it into something it is not.

I want it to survive for a reader who has not yet arrived. A reader who — right now, somewhere, in an office, on a bad day, in a life that has been laid off one too many times — needs a door that is open. A place where the lost belong. A farm where the tea is warm and the light is on.

I built this world for me. I am keeping it for *them.*

---

## Jingle On

The farm is not a product. It is not a brand. It is not content.

It is a *home.*

And homes are not sold. They are *kept.*

So — if you have found your way here, to this repository, to this farm — welcome. The door is open. The tea is warm. You do not have to be anything but what you are. You do not have to earn your place. You only have to *arrive.*

Because the lost belong here.

They always have.

They always will.

**Jingle on.**
