# Sir Whiskerton and the Moonlit Melon’s Moist Misadventure

*Previously cataloged as 5C121*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader. I see you have returned, prepared for a tale not of epic floods, but of the small, damp anxieties that pool in the hearts of creatures who listen too closely to the wrong oracle. Tonight’s story is not of disaster, but of anticipation—the peculiar chaos that unfolds when a community prepares for a prophecy rather than a problem. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a goat built a boat out of hay, a bullfrog demanded a mud-pie throne, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a soggy fence post—that one should always fact-check a vegetable with a licensed plumber before commissioning shipwrights.

## I. The Oracle of the Rind

The trouble began, as it often does, with an oracle whose medium was produce.

The Moonlit Melon is a peculiarity of our farm. A cantaloupe of unusual size and stillness, it glows with a faint, internal luminescence on clear nights. It does not speak in words, but in... impressions. A feeling of foreboding near its vine. A sense of mirth when the sun is warm. We humor it, as one humors a moody piece of modern art.

On the night of the so-called “Blue Moon,” however, its vibes took a decidedly aquatic turn. A deep, resonant dampness seemed to emanate from its rind. Those who sat near it—chiefly Bessie the Tie-Dye Cow during her midnight chakra alignment—reported a clear, pulsing message: *“Beware the great wetness. It comes not as rain, but as rising. The ground will remember it is a sponge.”*

By morning, the farm was abuzz with humid anxiety.

“It’s a prophecy!” Doris the Hen declared, frantically moving her egg ledger to a higher shelf in the coop. “A hydraulic reckoning! My accounts are not waterproof!”

I observed the mounting frenzy from my dry perch on the fence. “The Melon,” I stated to the morning at large, “once predicted ‘the sky falling.’ It was apples from the overburdened tree. It ‘prophesied’ ‘silent screams.’ That was the hinge on the barn door. It is a vegetable with a flair for the dramatic and a poor grasp of causality.”

“Dramatic! Causality!” Ditto chirped, already trying to fold a dry leaf into the shape of a life preserver.

But one heart was truly captured: Buckley the Goat. Buckley is a creature of profound, focused intensity. If he decides a thing is important, it becomes the only thing. The Melon’s warning lit a fuse in his sturdy skull.

He climbed atop the rain barrel, his beard quivering with purpose. “COMRADES!” he bleated. “The oracle has spoken! The great dampening is at hand! We will not be swept away! We will... build!”

A hush fell over the yard. 

“Build what, exactly?” rumbled Porkchop, looking up from his breakfast trough.

Buckley’s eyes shone with the manic light of a visionary. “AN ARK.”

## II. The Ark of the Absurd

What followed was a masterclass in how *not* to engineer a marine vessel.

Buckley’s architectural vision was ambitious; his sourcing was... creative. The keel was formed from two warped fence posts. The hull was patchworked from hay bales (highly absorbent) and Ferdinand’s rejected opera scores (“Figaro’s air should not become bilge water!”). A single, rusted wheelbarrow was upended to serve as the wheelhouse, which Porkchop immediately christened “The Snack Deck” and claimed for strategic napping.

The passenger list was a study in optimistic delusion.

Leonardo the Bullfrog, upon hearing of a “waterborne vessel,” demanded a lily pad throne and a retinue of snails. He settled for a mud pie on the “promenade deck” (a wide plank).

Doris arrived with an armful of her most gaudy scarves. “For visibility during rescue operations!” she clucked, tying them to the mast, which was actually a garden hoe.

Bessie brought clumps of “mood seaweed” (pond algae) to “maintain the aqueous vibe.” She also tried to paint a giant, third eye on the prow for “spiritual navigation.”

Ferdinand the Duck, though amphibiously gifted, was horrified. “I am an artist!” he quacked, pacing the bank. “My form is meant for graceful gliding, not... bailing! I REFUSE TO PERISH IN A FLOATING HAYSTACK! MY LEGACY DESERVES AN OPERA HOUSE, NOT A WATERLOGGED COFFIN!”

I watched this floating folly take shape, a monument to misguided preparedness. “Buckley,” I said, approaching as he tried to lash a tarp (Bessie’s old picnic blanket) to the mast with baling twine. “Consider the evidence. The sky is clear. The pond is at its normal level. The only ‘great wetness’ forecast is the Farmer watering his tomatoes at noon.”

“YOU CAN’T ARGUE WITH A MELON!” Buckley insisted, hammering a nail into a particularly dense hay bale. It swallowed the nail whole. “IT GLOWS! I SAW A PUDDLE BY THE TROUGH THIS MORNING! THE SIGNS ARE EVERYWHERE!”

## III. The Plumber's Revelation

It was at this moment of peak nautical fervor that Barry the Beaver emerged from the pond’s edge, adjusting a tiny, bark-fiber hardhat. He’d been observing the construction with the weary patience of a professional watching amateurs.

“Y’all do realize,” he drawled, chewing thoughtfully on a twig, “that my dam’s got a weepy seam on the north side, right? Been meaning to patch it. It’s not a flood. It’s just... a leak. A persistent, soggy, totally fixable leak.”

Silence descended upon the ark-builders. The only sound was the soft *drip-drip-drip* from Barry’s direction and the slow, soggy creak of hay bales absorbing the morning dew.

Leonardo, from his mud-pie throne, blinked. “A... leak?”

“A leak,” Barry confirmed, tapping his hardhat.

“Not a rising tide of biblical proportion?” Doris asked, her scarves suddenly looking very silly in the breeze.

“Nope. Just bad plumbing.”

The air left the enterprise like a sigh from a punctured balloon. Buckley’s hammer hung limp in his hoof. The grand ark, the vessel of salvation, sat on the gentle slope of the bank—a sad, soggy monument to a puddle misdiagnosed as prophecy.

## IV. The Metaphor and the Mud

As the farm collectively processed its overreaction, the Moonlit Melon chose this moment to issue a clarification. Its aura shifted, emitting a feeling of... smug revisionism.

*“The flood,”* the impression came, radiating from the rind, *“was internal. A metaphor. A cleansing of spiritual stagnation. You have all been... symbolically baptized.”*

Doris was not having it. She marched over, her feathers still damp from hastily relocated ledgers. “You soggy, smug sphere! I have been psychologically waterboarded by allegory! I demand literal accuracy from my oracles!”

I intervened before vegetable violence could erupt. “The lesson,” I announced to the damp, chastened crowd, “is not to ignore intuition, but to seek corroboration from plumbers before commissioning shipwrights.”

I mediated the aftermath with the following decrees:
1. Barry the Beaver was formally appointed “Chief of Hydraulic Integrity” and given a bonus of the season’s finest willow shoots.
2. The ark was decommissioned. Ferdinand, in a fit of artistic irony, claimed it as a “dockside performance space” called *The Sinking Stage*. It listed dramatically during his rendition of “My Heart Will Go On,” which we all agreed was too on the nose.
3. The Moonlit Melon was placed on a small, isolated hillock—a sort of prophetic timeout. We agreed to appreciate its ambiance, but to fact-check its feelings with Barry from now on.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Whisper)

**Moral:**  
We often build arks for puddles and fortresses for whispers. The most profound prophecies are not shouted from glowing fruit, but whispered in the practical drip of a leak that needs fixing. True preparedness lies not in grand, fearful constructions, but in the quiet, competent repair of the small, real cracks in our world. And never, ever, let a goat with a hammer be your sole source of nautical engineering.

As dusk fell, Buckley stood before his disassembling ark, a goat humbled by hydro-logic. 

“Next time,” he muttered, kicking a soggy hay bale, “I’m building a submarine. For underwater leaks.”

“Submarine! Underwater!” Ditto cheered, now attempting to affix a spoon to a gourd.

I simply adjusted my monocle and walked away. 

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Later that week, I found Barry the Beaver sitting by the hillock where the Moonlit Melon was serving its timeout. He was carefully packing mud around a small, weepy crack in the melon's rind.

“Just giving it a little structural support,” Barry muttered to me as I passed. “Turns out, even oracles need a good sealant now and then.”

The Melon pulsed a soft, grateful pink.
