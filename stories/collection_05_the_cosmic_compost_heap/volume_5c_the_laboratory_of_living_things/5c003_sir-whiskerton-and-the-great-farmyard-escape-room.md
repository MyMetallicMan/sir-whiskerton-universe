# Sir Whiskerton and the Great Farmyard Escape Room

*Previously cataloged as 5C85*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader. You have returned, and I see you are prepared for a tale of confinement, confusion, and catastrophic do-it-yourself carpentry. Tonight’s story is not one of external peril, but of a danger far more insidious: enthusiastic incompetence, given form, nails, and a deeply confusing crayon riddle. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a donkey with a hammer built a prison, a stray cat played warden, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a splintered doorframe—that some puzzles are not meant to be solved, but simply broken.

## I. The Calamity of Hank’s Howdy-Do

The calamity began, as so many do, with Handy Hank. 

Hank is the farm’s resident tinkerer, a donkey of boundless optimism and a technical skill set best described as “aspirational.” His projects have a certain… theoretical quality. We have all learned to steer clear of the self-milking machine that milked the fencepost, and the automatic chicken feeder that launched eggs like catapult ammunition.

Which is why the sight of his latest creation, erected overnight in the barn’s lean-to, sent a chill through the morning air.

It was a structure. That much was undeniable. It appeared to be cobbled together from mismatched planks, a stolen garden gate, several yards of baling twine, and what looked like the repurposed skeleton of a patio umbrella. A sign, painted in cheerful, dripping red letters, proclaimed:

**HANK’S HOWDY-DO ESCAPE ROOM!**
*FUN FOR THE WHOLE HERD! SOLVE THE PUZZLES! WIN FREEDOM! (AND A SURPRISE!)*
*Disclaimer: Surprise may be splinters. No refunds on hay or dignity.*

The animals gathered at a safe distance. 

“Y’all ready for some lateral-thinkin’, team-buildin’ excitement?” Hank brayed, gesturing with a hammer that was, alarmingly, still warm. “I been readin’ up on psychology! It’s about the thrill of the solve!”

I adjusted my monocle, surveying the wobbling edifice. “Hank,” I stated, “that ‘puzzle’ on the left appears to be Big Red’s rubber hamburger nailed to a two-by-four.”

“It’s abstract!” Hank insisted. “Symbolizes the meat of the matter!”

## II. The Velvet Hutch of Despair

Before a proper critique could be mounted, a new figure emerged from the shadows. Catnip the Stray, a sleek, perpetually unimpressed tomcat from the neighboring woods, sauntered forward, his tail a metronome of disdain.

“An escape room,” he drawled, his voice like silk over gravel. “How adorably pedestrian. I suppose for the simple-minded, running from one’s own stupidity passes for entertainment.”

Hank’s smile faltered. “It’s about brainpower!”

“Is it?” Catnip purred, slinking around the structure. “I see a locked door. I see simple minds. A hypothesis presents itself.” 

In a fluid motion, he ducked inside the rickety door, tapped something with a claw, and slipped back out, pulling the door shut behind him. A distinct *CLICK* echoed in the sudden silence. 

We all stared at the closed door.

Catnip leaned against the wall, examining his claws. “There. An impromptu experiment. Let’s see if the barnyard brain trust can outwit… a latch. First clue’s inside. Do try not to perish of ennui before I return with snacks.” And with a flick of his tail, he was gone.

A long, heavy pause was broken by Porkchop. “So… are we locked in Hank’s death trap?”

“It appears,” I said, my voice dangerously calm, “that we have been voluntold for a feasibility study in structural integrity and collective problem-solving. Under duress.”

## III. The Clues of Chaos

The interior of Hank’s Escape Room was a monument to well-intentioned chaos. The “clues” were less a trail of logic and more a scavenger hunt designed by a cheerful madman.

**Clue #1** was a sheet of butcher paper taped to the wall, adorned with a childlike drawing of a sun and a hen, under which was scrawled in green crayon: *“What’s sunny, lays, and points the way? It ain't the rooster at break of day!”*

Doris the Hen puffed up immediately. “This is clearly about me! The sun! The laying! The poetic injustice of the rooster getting all the dawn credit! The key must be… my self-worth!”

I sighed. “Doris, it’s a pun. The answer is ‘corn.’ Yellow, ‘lays’ in a field, points in rows. It’s pointing us to that ear of corn nailed to the ceiling.” 

Doris deflated. “That’s speciesist. And reductive.”

**Clue #2** was Bessie’s missing mood ring, duct-taped to a post, its stone swirling a frantic, anxious violet. 

“Whoa,” Bessie lowed, leaning close. “The room’s aura is… panic purple. A trapped, spiraling frequency. We must align our chakras with the locking mechanism.”

“The ring is broken,” I said, prying it loose. “You left it on the fencepost yesterday, and it got rained on. It’s just… stuck on ‘damp existential dread.’” 

We located a small key taped behind it, which fit nothing in the room.

The centerpiece was a large, rusty toolbox with a padlock. A note on top read: *“The answer is in the song of the morning.”*

Doris was livid. “Another avian reference! This is a targeted campaign! The ‘song of the morning’ is my egg-laying soliloquy! The code is… ‘CLUCK’!”

I examined the cheap padlock. “While your outrage is artistically coherent, the solution is more literal.” I plucked a thin, dried fishbone from a forgotten saucer (Hank’s idea of “red herring” decor) and, with two precise twists, popped the lock open. Inside was not a key, but a single, worn-down pencil and a map of the farm drawn on a napkin. 

The map was useless. Big Red had eaten the corner with the barn on it. Speaking of Big Red, the good-natured dog was having the time of his life. He’d found the “snack station” (a bowl of puzzle pieces made of compressed liver) and consumed not only the snacks but several of the actual wooden puzzle pieces meant to form a number code. He now looked pleased, slightly ill, and utterly unhelpful.
