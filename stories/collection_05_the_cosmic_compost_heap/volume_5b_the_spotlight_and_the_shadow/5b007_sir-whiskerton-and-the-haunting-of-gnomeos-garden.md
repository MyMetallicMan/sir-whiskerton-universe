# Sir Whiskerton and the Haunting of Gnomeo’s Garden

*Previously cataloged as 5B141*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, settle into the hayloft of your heart. Let the screen door sigh shut behind you. The air tonight smells of damp soil, crushed garlic, and the faint, unmistakable scent of a prank gone entirely too far. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a garden gnome tried to be spooky, a vampire was deeply offended by a kazoo, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a judgmental piñata—that if you’re going to haunt a garden, you really must commit to the aesthetic.

## I. Gnomeo’s Grand Prank

It all began on a perfectly peaceful, starlit evening—or at least, it would have been peaceful if Gnomeo the garden gnome hadn’t decided that the farm animals were far too relaxed. 

Gnomeo stood in the center of the vegetable patch, rubbing his tiny, painted hands together. “Ah,” he whispered to the night breeze. “What this farm needs is a little… supernatural intrigue.”

Beside him, hanging from the lowest branch of the apple tree, was Bartholomew the Piñata. Bartholomew did not speak. He merely swayed in the wind, radiating a profound, silent judgment.

Using nothing but a stolen bedsheet, a length of fishing line, and a kazoo he had “borrowed” from the Quacking Kazoo Crew, Gnomeo rigged up the ghost of “Sir Spookerton.” He claimed the specter haunted the garden because it was built on an ancient lettuce burial ground.

He waited in the shadows for his first victim. 

Doris the Hen came clucking down the path, her wire-rimmed spectacles glinting in the moonlight. Gnomeo pulled the string. The bedsheet floated down. He blew into the kazoo. *BZZZZ-SQUEEEE.*

“Bewaaaare…” Gnomeo rasped, pitching his voice as low as it could go. “The curse of the… overwatered petunias!”

“Oh, cluck!” Doris shrieked, flapping her wings in a panic. “Harriet! Lillian! We’re doomed!”

“Doomed!” Harriet echoed, tilting her head.

“Doomed!” Lillian added, before fainting dramatically into a nearby wheelbarrow. She would remain there for exactly 17 seconds.

Gnomeo cackled silently. He moved to his next target: Porkchop the Pig, who was happily snacking on a bowl of cheese puffs. 

Gnomeo swooped the sheet down. “Your snacks… are MIIINE!” he wailed.

“Not the cheese puffs!” Porkchop yelled, his eyes widening in sheer terror. “I’ll fight you, ghost!” 

Gnomeo hadn’t anticipated this. He learned very quickly that pigs are surprisingly fast, and remarkably heavy, when their snacks are threatened. He barely managed to reel the sheet back into the tomato vines before Porkchop trampled his hiding spot.

His final victim was Rufus the Dog. Gnomeo swooped down one last time. “The spirit of… the mailman you never caught seeks vengeance!”

Rufus stopped, his chartreuse glow dimming in confusion. “NOOOOO—wait, I don’t even chase the mailman anymore! I made peace with him last Tuesday!”

Gnomeo paused, the kazoo dangling from his lips. “Uh… reverse vengeance?”

“Oh,” Rufus said, his tail giving a single, polite wag. “Okay. Cool.”

From atop the fence, Sir Whiskerton watched the entire debacle. He adjusted his monocle and sighed. “This is the worst haunting I’ve ever seen,” he muttered to Ditto. “And I’ve seen Bartholomew after a stiff breeze.”

## II. Enter: Count Catula

Just as Gnomeo was reveling in his prank’s success, the temperature in the garden suddenly dropped. A thick, theatrical fog rolled in from the compost heap. A shadowy figure emerged, his cape billowing dramatically in a wind that didn't exist.

“Ah, mes amis!” Count Catula declared, flipping his velvet cape over his shoulder. “You have prepared for my arrival, non?”

Gnomeo froze, his bedsheet tangled in a zucchini vine. “Uh… yes? Totally. Welcome, oh… spooky guest?”

Count Catula looked around the garden, his crimson eyes narrowing in profound disgust. “You call zis a gothic welcome? Where are ze candelabras? Ze wailing violins? Ze blood pudding?”

Gnomeo nervously offered him a small, dirt-covered radish. “We’ve got… root vegetables?”

“Mon dieu,” Count Catula gasped, clutching his chest. “Zis is an outrage.”

It turned out, Count Catula had actually been haunting the garden for weeks, napping in the shadows like a sophisticated specter. Gnomeo’s fake ghost had deeply insulted him by being, in his words, “amateur hour undead.”

“A bedsheet?” Count Catula sneered, pointing a pale, clawed finger at the kazoo. “A KAZOO? I am appalled. Do you have any idea how hard it is to maintain a mysterious aura when some garden gnome is squeaking a toy instrument?”

“To be fair,” Sir Whiskerton called out from the fence, “you do sleep in a coffin made of old shoeboxes.”

“Artisanal shoeboxes!” Count Catula corrected sharply. “With velvet lining!”

## III. The Haunting Escalates

Now, the garden had two supernatural nuisances, and neither was willing to back down. 

Gnomeo’s "ghost" kept getting tangled in the tomato vines, squeaking the kazoo every time the wind blew. Count Catula, meanwhile, demanded "proper ambiance"—which meant he kept insisting that everyone wear black and sigh dramatically every time they walked past the pumpkin patch.

“You zere, Piñata!” Count Catula snapped at Bartholomew one afternoon. “Why do you not mourn? Why do you not weep for the fallen leaves?”

Bartholomew said nothing. He merely swayed. *Swish. Swish.* The judgment was palpable.

“Très bien,” Count Catula murmured, nodding respectfully. “A fellow brooder. I respect zis.”

Meanwhile, the animals were entirely over it. 

“I can’t cluck under this much gothic pressure!” Doris complained, adjusting her spectacles. “And the kazoo is giving me a migraine!”

“I miss when the only thing haunting me was hunger,” Porkchop sighed, staring longingly at a bowl of slop.

Rufus trotted up to Count Catula, his tail wagging politely. “Excuse me, Mr. Vampire. Can ghosts fetch? Asking for a friend.”

Count Catula stared at the glowing dog, utterly bewildered. “I am a creature of ze night! I do not… fetch!”

## IV. Sir Whiskerton’s Solution

Realizing this haunting had gone entirely too far, Sir Whiskerton leapt down from the fence and brokered a truce. He gathered Gnomeo, Count Catula, and the silently judging Bartholomew in the center of the garden.

“Enough,” Whiskerton purred, his voice carrying the quiet authority of a thousand napping ancestors. “Gnomeo, your prank is over. You have offended the local undead. Count Catula, your dramatic sighing is disrupting the farm’s sleep schedule. We need a compromise.”

He laid out the terms:

**Step One:** Gnomeo apologized by building Count Catula a "throne" out of discarded garden tools and a velvet cushion Martha had left on the porch. 

**Step Two:** Count Catula agreed to "haunt responsibly"—meaning no dramatic fog before 8 PM, and absolutely no demanding blood pudding from the Farmer.

**Step Three:** Bartholomew the Piñata, who had been swaying silently through the entire negotiation, finally spoke. His voice was dry, papery, and utterly exhausted.

“This was… unnecessary.”

Everyone gasped. 

“GASP. HE SPOKE!” Doris shrieked, clutching her chest. 

“Spoke!” Harriet echoed.

“Unnecessary!” Lillian added, fainting dramatically into the wheelbarrow for the second time that week.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Whisper)

**Moral:**  
Pranks backfire when real drama queens show up. And sometimes, the true haunting is the friends we annoyed along the way. If you’re going to mess with the supernatural, make sure you have the aesthetic to back it up.

As peace returned to the garden (save for Count Catula’s occasional, perfectly timed "woe is me" soliloquies), the animals reflected on the night’s events. 

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Later that night, Count Catula decided to test his new "responsible haunting" limits. He crept up to the Farmer’s porch, draped his cape over his shoulders, and let out a low, mournful wail. 

The Farmer, half-asleep in his rocking chair, looked down at the black cat. “Oh, hey there, funny-looking kitty,” he murmured, reaching down to scratch Count Catula behind the ears. “You want some milk?”

Count Catula froze. The wail died in his throat. He looked at the saucer of milk. He looked at the Farmer’s gentle hand. 

“…Make it warm, please,” the vampire whispered, entirely defeated.
