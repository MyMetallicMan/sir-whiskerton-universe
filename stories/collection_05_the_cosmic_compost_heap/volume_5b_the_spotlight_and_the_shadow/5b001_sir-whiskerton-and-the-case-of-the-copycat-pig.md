# Sir Whiskerton and the Case of the Copycat Pig

*Previously cataloged as 5B140*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, dim the house lights and raise the curtain. Let the screen door sigh shut behind you. The air today smells of stale popcorn, theatrical greasepaint, and the faint, electric static of a pig who has discovered the power of a dramatic pause. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a quiet pig found his inner diva, a cybernetic assassin crossed the county line, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a celery baton—that when life gives you chaos, the only logical response is to put on a show.

## I. The Rise of the Oinkster

It all began on a perfectly ordinary Tuesday—which, as any farm animal will tell you, is never a good sign. The sun was shining, the hay was freshly baled, and Doris the Hen was holding court in the coop, as was her divine right.

“Ladies,” Doris declared, fluffing her feathers and adjusting her wire-rimmed spectacles. “Today, we discuss the scandal of the missing corn kernels.”

“Scandal!” Harriet echoed, clutching her chest.

“Kernels!” Lillian added, before promptly fainting onto a pile of straw. She would remain there for exactly 17 seconds.

And then… it happened.

From the shadows of the barn, a portly figure emerged. A pig. But not just any pig—this pig had ambition. His name was Oinkster. 

Now, Oinkster had always been a quiet, contemplative pig, content to wallow in mud and ponder life’s great mysteries (such as, *Why is slop so delicious?* and *Do acorns dream of being truffles?*). But on this fateful day, something inside him snapped. Perhaps it was the way Doris swooned with such flair. Maybe it was Harriet’s dramatic gasps. Or maybe it was Lillian’s impeccable comedic timing. Whatever the reason, Oinkster felt a calling.

And so, with a snort of determination, he did the unthinkable. He joined them.

“Ladies,” Oinkster declared, fluffing his nonexistent feathers, “today, we discuss the scandal of the missing corn kernels.”

“Scandal!” he echoed, clutching his chest.

“Kernels!” he added, before dramatically collapsing onto the exact same pile of straw—nearly crushing Lillian in the process.

The hens stared. Doris blinked. Harriet gasped (again, for effect). Lillian, still half-buried under a very heavy pig, managed a weak, “Help.”

And thus, the legend of Oinkster, the Copycat Pig, was born.

## II. The Farm Descends into Chaos

What began as a curious imitation quickly spiraled into full-blown pandemonium. Oinkster didn’t just copy the hens—he *became* them. 

When Doris organized a protest against the Farmer’s “unfair” feed distribution, Oinkster was right beside her, holding a hastily painted sign that read: *PIGS DESERVE GOURMET TOO!* 

When Harriet shrieked about a “mysterious shadow” (which turned out to be a slightly aggressive leaf), Oinkster shrieked louder, his voice cracking with theatrical terror. 

When Lillian fainted at the sight of a particularly menacing dandelion, Oinkster also fainted—directly onto the dandelion, thereby solving the problem through sheer, blunt-force trauma.

The farm had never been so… dramatic. 

Even Sir Whiskerton, who prided himself on maintaining order from his rooftop observatory, was at a loss. “This is getting out of hand,” he muttered, watching Oinkster reenact Harriet’s latest swoon with alarming, hoof-flailing accuracy.

“Hand!” Ditto echoed, nodding sagely from the fencepost.

But just when Whiskerton thought things couldn’t get worse… *he* arrived.

## III. Enter… The Pigernator

A shadow fell across the barnyard. A cool, leather-clad shadow. A shadow wearing dark sunglasses.

From the dusty road emerged a figure so intimidating, so unstoppable, that even the chickens paused mid-squawk. 

Leather jacket? Check. 
Muscles rippling beneath the leather? Check. 
Tiny pig hooves crossed over his chest in a pose that screamed, *“I’ll be back… for slop”*? Double check.

This was no ordinary pig. This was The Pigernator.

“I am here for Oinkster,” he grunted, his voice sounding suspiciously like gravel wrapped in bacon. “Bigcat wants him back.”

A hush fell over the barnyard. The wind stopped blowing. The crickets stopped chirping. 

“Back?” Doris whispered.

“Bigcat?” Harriet gasped.

“Oh no,” Lillian said, before fainting.

“No!” Oinkster echoed, fainting directly on top of her.

The Pigernator adjusted his sunglasses, the lenses flashing in the sun. “Resistance is futile.”

## IV. Sir Whiskerton’s Brilliant (and Slightly Ridiculous) Plan

Now, dear reader, you may be wondering: How does one negotiate with a pig who thinks he’s a cybernetic assassin sent by a rival farm boss? 

The answer, as it turns out, is theater.

“Pigernator,” Sir Whiskerton began, stepping forward with his most diplomatic tail flick, his kilt rippling in the breeze. “I propose a… competition.”

“Competition?” The Pigernator’s eyebrow (or where an eyebrow would be, if pigs had them) twitched beneath his shades.

“Yes,” Whiskerton said smoothly. “A test of skill. A battle of wits. A… dramatic showdown. If you win, Oinkster returns with you to Bigcat’s farm. No questions asked.”

“And if you win?” The Pigernator grunted.

Whiskerton smirked, a slow, confident curving of his mustache. “Then you admit that farm life is superior to Bigcat’s tyranny… and you stay for dinner.”

A long, tense pause. Then—

“Accepted.”

And so, the greatest contest the farm had ever seen began.

## V. The Competition: Three Rounds of Absurdity

**Round One: The Dramatic Faint-Off**
*Contestants: Oinkster vs. The Pigernator. Rules: Whoever faints with the most flair wins.*

Oinkster went first. He gasped, clutched his chest, spun once on his hind hooves, and collapsed with a swoon so dramatic, so perfectly timed, that even Doris applauded. 

The Pigernator stepped up. He tried. But alas, cybernetic pigs are not built for theatrics. His faint was more of a controlled, rigid descent, like a redwood tree falling in slow motion. *Thud.* 

*Winner: Oinkster.*

**Round Two: The Overreaction Challenge**
*Contestants: Harriet vs. The Pigernator. Rules: React to a completely normal event as if it were a catastrophe.*

Harriet, upon seeing a single, tiny ant crawl across a leaf, shrieked, “IT’S AN INVASION! WE’RE DOOMED! THE SKY IS FALLING!” She fainted, revived, and fainted again.

The Pigernator stared at the ant. He pulled out a tiny, metallic scanner. “I have analyzed this threat. It is… small. Probability of doom: 0.04%.”

*Winner: Harriet (by a landslide).*

**Round Three: The Ultimate Test… Karaoke**
*Contestants: The Pigernator vs. Ferdinand the Duck. Rules: Sing “I Will Always Love You” with feeling.*

Ferdinand stepped up to the makeshift microphone (a hollowed-out gourd). He belted it out like a feathery, feathered Whitney Houston, his vibrato shaking the leaves off the oak tree. 

The Pigernator took the mic. His rendition sounded like a tractor engine choking on a corn cob, sung entirely in a monotone, robotic growl. 

*Winner: Ferdinand (obviously).*

## VI. The Aftermath: A Pig Reformed

Defeated, The Pigernator slowly removed his dark sunglasses, revealing surprisingly gentle, soulful brown eyes. 

“I… concede,” he admitted, his gravelly voice softening. “Farm life is… illogical.”

“But fun?” Whiskerton prompted gently.

A long pause. The Pigernator looked at Oinkster, who was currently teaching Lillian how to project her voice from her diaphragm. He looked at Ferdinand, who was taking a bow. He looked at the mud puddle, gleaming invitingly in the afternoon sun.

“…Acceptable,” The Pigernator murmured.

And with that, The Pigernator joined the farm, trading his leather jacket for a wide-brimmed sunhat and his mission for a life of leisure. He took up a quiet spot by the pond, occasionally offering stoic, one-word critiques of the ducks' swimming form.

As for Oinkster? He did return to Bigcat’s farm… but not as a prisoner. He went back as a drama teacher. (Bigcat’s hens had been desperately needing help with their monologues.)

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Curtain Call)

**Moral:**  
Life is too short to take too seriously. Whether you’re a pig who copies chickens, a cybernetic agent of chaos, or a cat with a flair for diplomacy, sometimes the best solution is to embrace the absurdity—preferably with a dramatic faint and a well-timed quack. After all, why be normal when you can be memorable?

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Somewhere on Bigcat’s farm, a group of hens are performing Shakespeare in the mud. Oinkster, now their director, wearing a tiny beret, sighs wistfully. 

“Alas, poor Yorick,” Oinkster directs, adjusting a hen’s wings. “I ate him. But with more *pathos*, darling! More pathos!”
