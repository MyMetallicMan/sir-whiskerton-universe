# Sir Whiskerton and the Case of the Cursed Cheese Wheel

*Previously cataloged as 5D96*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader. You have returned, and I see you are braced for a tale of dairy-based doom and the perils of ignoring a rodent in a trench coat. Tonight’s story is not one of malice, but of appetite—a lesson in the delicate etiquette of charcuterie, the heavy burden of hoarding, and the profound truth that a curse is often just a blessing waiting to be shared. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a pig ate a cursed wheel of gouda, a rat spoke in riddles, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a charred piece of wood—that the best things in life are not meant to be consumed alone.

## I. The Curse is Cast

It all began on a quiet afternoon, when the farm was bathed in the golden, honeyed light of the setting sun. The animals were going about their usual routines. Doris the Hen was gossiping with Harriet and Lillian, Rufus the Dog was chasing his tail in a joyful, glowing circle, and Porkchop the Pig was lounging in his favorite mud puddle, contemplating the structural integrity of a particularly good grub.

But the peace was shattered when a loud, resonant *THUD* echoed from the barn.

“What was that?” Porkchop asked, his ears perking up, his snout twitching.

“That!” Ditto echoed, his tiny tail twitching with curiosity.

Sir Whiskerton, ever the vigilant detective, leapt from his sunbeam and made his way to the barn. There, lying in the exact center of the dirt floor, was a large, round cheese wheel. It was golden, glistening, and emitted a faint, ominous hum. Carved deeply into its rind was a strange, swirling symbol.

“Hmm,” Sir Whiskerton mused, narrowing his piercing green eyes. “This is most peculiar. Where did this cheese come from?”

Before anyone could answer, a shadow detached itself from the rafters. It was Ratso the Rat, his trench coat flapping dramatically in the nonexistent breeze, a fedora pulled low over his eyes. 

“It’s cursed,” Ratso said, his voice gravelly and world-weary. “A cursed cheese wheel. Anyone who eats it will be plagued by bad luck.”

Porkchop’s eyes widened, but his mouth watered. “Cursed cheese? That’s the most ridiculous thing I’ve ever heard. Cheese is cheese. How can it be cursed?”

Ratso shook his head, striking a match on his own tooth and letting it burn down to the filter. “You don’t understand. This is the Cheese of Calamity. Legend has it that it was created by an ancient cheesemaker who was betrayed by his apprentice. In his anger, he cursed the cheese, ensuring that anyone who ate it would suffer misfortune until they learned the weight of their own greed.”

Sir Whiskerton flicked his tail. “A cursed cheese wheel. How... cheesy.”

## II. The First Victim

Despite Ratso’s noir monologue, Porkchop couldn’t resist the allure of the golden cheese. “It’s just a silly legend,” he said, his trotter reaching out. “What’s the worst that could happen?”

With that, he took a massive, triumphant bite of the cheese. 

Almost immediately, the universe retaliated. First, his favorite mud puddle dried up into a dusty, cracked disappointment. Then, a swarm of highly agitated bees chased him around the barnyard, stinging his Hawaiian shirt. Finally, he tripped over a perfectly flat rock and landed face-first in a pile of dry, itchy hay.

“See?” Ratso said, crossing his arms. “I told you it was cursed.”

Porkchop groaned, pulling a piece of hay from his ear. “Okay, okay, I believe you. But how do we break the curse?”

Ratso sighed, a long, dramatic exhalation of smoke. “The only way to break the curse is to solve a series of four riddles. Each riddle will lead you to a clue, and the final clue will reveal how to lift the curse.”

Sir Whiskerton nodded. “Very well. Porkchop, it seems we have a mystery to solve. Ditto, take notes.”

## III. The Riddles of the Farm

Ratso handed Sir Whiskerton a piece of parchment with the first riddle written on it: 
*“I am not alive, but I grow; I don’t have lungs, but I need air; I don’t have a mouth, but water kills me. What am I?”*

Sir Whiskerton pondered the riddle for a moment. “Hmm. Not alive, but it grows. Doesn’t have lungs, but needs air. Doesn’t have a mouth, but water kills it.”

“A plant?” Porkchop scratched his head.

“No, plants are alive,” Sir Whiskerton said. 

“Fire!” Ditto piped up, his eyes wide.

Sir Whiskerton’s eyes lit up. “Yes, Ditto! Fire grows, needs air, and is killed by water. Well done.” With the first riddle solved, the trio made their way to the farmhouse fireplace, where they found the next clue: a small, charred piece of wood with the second riddle written on it.

*“I speak without a mouth and hear without ears. I have no body, but I come alive with the wind. What am I?”*

Porkchop frowned. “Something that speaks without a mouth? And comes alive with the wind?”

“An echo!” Ditto tilted his head.

“Exactly, Ditto,” Sir Whiskerton smiled. “An echo speaks without a mouth and hears without ears.” With the second riddle solved, the trio made their way to the pond, where the sound of their voices echoed across the water. There, they found the next clue: a smooth stone with the third riddle written on it.

*“The more you take, the more you leave behind. What am I?”*

Porkchop scratched his head. “The more you take, the more you leave behind? That doesn’t make any sense.”

“Footsteps!” Ditto chirped.

“Yes, Ditto,” Sir Whiskerton nodded. “The more footsteps you take, the more you leave behind.” With the third riddle solved, the trio made their way to the path leading to the barn, where they found the final clue: a small, dusty book with the fourth riddle written on it.

*“I am always in front of you but can never be seen. What am I?”*

Porkchop groaned. “This one’s impossible.”

“The future!” Ditto tilted his head.

“Exactly, Ditto,” Sir Whiskerton smiled. “The future is always in front of you, but it can never be seen.” 

## IV. The Curse is Broken

With the final riddle solved, the trio made their way to the barn, where they found a small, hidden compartment beneath the hay. Inside was a note that read: *“To break the curse, share the cheese with a friend.”*

Sir Whiskerton turned to Porkchop. “Well, it seems the curse can only be broken by sharing the cheese. Are you willing to share?”

Porkchop hesitated, looking at the remaining golden wheel. “Share my cheese? But it’s so delicious!”

Sir Whiskerton raised an eyebrow. “Do you want to keep suffering from bad luck? Do you want your mud puddles to remain dry?”

Porkchop sighed, his shoulders slumping. “Fine. I’ll share.”

With that, Porkchop cut a generous piece of the cheese and offered it to Sir Whiskerton. As soon as Sir Whiskerton took a bite, the strange symbol on the cheese wheel faded away, the ominous hum ceased, and the curse was lifted. Porkchop’s mud puddle instantly refilled with cool, glorious muck.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Whisper)

**Moral:**  
Sometimes, the things we desire most come with unexpected consequences. Whether it’s a cursed cheese wheel or a tempting opportunity, it’s important to consider the potential risks before diving in. And when faced with challenges, sharing and working together can often lead to the best outcomes. True abundance is not found in what we can hoard, but in what we can give away.

As the farm returned to its peaceful state, Porkchop, though initially reluctant to share, learned the value of generosity and friendship. Sir Whiskerton, ever the vigilant detective, resumed his sunbeam vigil, content in the knowledge that he had once again saved the day.

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
As for Ratso, he disappeared into the shadows, his trench coat flapping dramatically in the breeze. “Another case closed,” he muttered, his voice gravelly and world-weary. “But the world is still full of mysteries.” 

He reached into his pocket, pulled out a tiny crumb of the cursed cheese, and took a bite. Immediately, his trench coat caught fire. 

“Curse you, dairy!” he yelped, running toward the pond.
