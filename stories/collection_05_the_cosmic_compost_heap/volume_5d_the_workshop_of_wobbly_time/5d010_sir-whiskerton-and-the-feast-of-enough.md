# Sir Whiskerton and the Feast of Enough

*Previously cataloged as 5D132*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader. I see you have returned, and there is a particular softness to your expectancy. You are not braced for a mystery or a calamity, but for a different kind of revelation. Very well. I, Sir Whiskerton, shall recount a tale not of scarcity or conflict, but of a peculiar, delicious arithmetic discovered one evening when the farm’s table was set not just with food, but with a quiet question. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day we learned that the plate which feels emptiest is often the one you are clutching closest to your chest, and that joy is a dish that refuses to be served solo.

## I. The Metaphor with Mashed Potatoes

The idea was Zephyr’s, of course. The farm’s resident spirit of whimsical disruption arrived not with a bloop of his lava lamp, but with the scent of cinnamon and possibility one twilight.

“Cats and chicks,” he intoned, floating serenely above the picnic table we’d dragged into the yard. “Tonight, we dine on more than vittles. We dine on metaphor. With a side of mashed potatoes.”

He waved a shimmering hand. The table, already laden with Martha’s simple, hearty cooking—roasted roots, fresh bread, a stew that smelled of thyme and comfort—seemed to... deepen. The colors of the food grew more vivid. The steam carried not just heat, but a faint, golden glow. And an unspoken rule settled over the gathering, as clear as if written in the air: *Nothing here could be hoarded. It could only be shared.*

The farm gathered, intrigued. Martha and the Farmer sat at the head, beaming. Porkchop rubbed his trotters together in glee. Bessie hummed a low note of appreciation for the “collective nourishment vibe.” Even Catnip lurked at the edge of the lantern light, drawn by the smell but held back by his own proud perimeter.

The feast began. And the lesson, subtle and profound, unfolded.

## II. The Foundational Law of the Evening

Genghis, our feline kingpin, was the first to test the unspoken rule. His eyes, gleaming with proprietary delight, locked onto a platter of glitter-glazed rolls—Martha’s special recipe, dusted with edible gold sugar. They shone like little, buttery suns.

“Mine,” he murmured, not with malice, but with the simple, unquestioning gravity of a cat who believes the world exists for his convenience. He piled his plate high with them, a glistening, golden mountain. He did not offer. He did not even look up. He simply began to build his fortress of dough.

He took the first bite. A look of sublime pleasure crossed his face. He went for a second.

His fork met empty porcelain.

He blinked. The mountain of rolls was gone. Not eaten—vanished. His plate was clean, save for a few sparkling crumbs that winked out like dying stars. His stomach, which had been anticipating a feast, grumbled in protest. He had had them. He had tasted them. And now they were... un-had.

“A flaw in the catering!” he declared, outraged. But a quiet, dawning suspicion, colder than the empty plate, settled in his gut.

I observed from my perch on the table’s end. “It is not a flaw, Genghis,” I said quietly. “It is the foundational law of the evening. The feast recognizes only one currency: exchange.”

## III. The Abundance of the Margins

Across the table, a different scene played out. Martha, her own plate modestly filled, noticed Catnip eyeing a dish of honey-roasted carrots from his distant shadow. He looked not hungry, but... calculative. Weighing the risk of approach against the desire for the sweet, glazed vegetables.

Without a word, Martha picked up the serving bowl. Not just a spoonful, but the whole, generous dish. She rose, walked to the edge of the light where he stood, and held it out.

“Here,” she said, her voice offering no pressure, only presence. “They’re better warm.”

Catnip froze. The offer was a trap. It was a trick. It was... a bowl of carrots, held by a woman whose eyes held no expectation, only a simple, quiet kindness.

Hesitantly, rebelliously, he took one. Then, as if conceding to a grand defeat, he took the whole bowl from her hands, his claws careful not to scratch her. A grudging, almost imperceptible nod.

As he turned back to the shadows with his prize, something shimmered. Not just around the carrots. On Martha’s now-empty plate, the roasted roots replenished themselves, steaming hot. But more appeared: a small, perfect wedge of pie she hadn’t baked, a sprinkle of candied nuts, a pool of silky gravy.

On Catnip’s stolen bowl, the carrots seemed to multiply. And next to it, on the stump he used as a table, a warm, crusty roll appeared, along with a pat of butter and a cup of spiced cider he’d never asked for.

But the real abundance was not on the plates. It was in the space between them. Martha’s smile deepened. And Catnip, alone with his unexpected bounty, took a bite of carrot... and did not scowl. He simply ate. And for a moment, he looked not like a wary outlaw, but like a creature being fed.

## IV. The Potluck of the Soul

Zephyr floated by, his form weaving through the lantern light. He paused between Genghis’s stunned emptiness and the quiet, burgeoning plenty surrounding Martha and even Catnip.

“See, sugar?” he whispered, his voice like a warm breeze. “Enough ain’t a number you count. It’s a feeling you cook up together. And the recipe’s simple: it only grows when you pass it on.”

The truth, once tasted, spread faster than butter on a hot roll.

Porkchop, seeing this, bellowed with delight. “OH! A POTLUCK OF THE SOUL!” He began enthusiastically shoveling portions of his apple-wood smoked beans onto everyone’s plates within reach. With each generous splat, his own bowl refilled not just with beans, but with crispy fried onions, a cornbread muffin, a surprise pickle.

Doris, initially horrified at the chaos, reluctantly passed a perfectly segmented hard-boiled egg to Ferdinand. In return, her plate bloomed with delicate, herb-infused cheese cubes and a single, perfect strawberry.

Bessie shared her “Zen-rained Kale” and received a splash of glowing, celestial fruit compote.

Even I participated. Observing Ditto looking wistfully at a high-placed dish of cream, I nudged it toward him with a practiced paw. “For your investigative efforts,” I stated. As he purred into the saucer, my own plate, which had held a sensible portion of salmon, found itself graced with a garnish of fresh dill and a sun-warmed cherry tomato, plump with summer.

Genghis watched, his grand isolation now feeling less like a throne and more like a barren island. The laughter was the garnish, the shared “Oh, you must try this!” was the sauce, and the simple act of passing a dish was the sustenance that truly filled the belly. His glittering rolls had been a solitary joy, and the feast had politely declined to acknowledge it.

Finally, with the dignity of a deposed monarch, he picked up his one remaining roll—the one he’d taken a single bite from. He looked across the table at Rufus, who was vibrating with happy, food-driven joy but had missed the platter of rolls entirely.

Genghis sighed, a sound of profound, theatrical surrender. He held out the roll. “Here, you overgrown hearthrug. It’s... acceptable.”

Rufus’s eyes lit up. He gently took the roll. As he did, Genghis’s plate shimmered. The mountain of glitter-glazed rolls did not return. Instead, a small, perfect assortment appeared: a slice of the pie, a few honeyed carrots, a scoop of Porkchop’s beans. A meal. A shared one.

And on Rufus’s plate, next to the gifted roll, appeared a giant, savory bone-biscuit. Genghis looked from his diverse, communal plate to Rufus’s joyful, slobbery gratitude. A slow, unfamiliar sensation—warmer than pride, quieter than victory—spread through him. It was the feeling of being part of the table.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Whisper)

**Moral:**  
We had learned the delicious, simple math of the Feast of Enough: that joy is a dish that refuses to be served solo. It multiplies in the handing-over, deepens in the acceptance, and becomes a true banquet only when whispered, with a full mouth and an open hand, “Here. Try this.”

When the moon was high and the plates were finally, truly empty (because even a magical feast must end), the farm sat in a contented, buzzing silence. The air was thick with the fragrance of shared food and the deeper, sweeter scent of shared experience.

Zephyr gave a final, soft bloop. “The dishes’ll do themselves, darlings. The metaphor, however... that’s yours to keep.”

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Now, if you’ll excuse me, I believe I have a saucer of cream to share with a certain apprentice. The lesson, after all, is in the passing.
