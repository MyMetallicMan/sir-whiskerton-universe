# Sir Whiskerton and the Forbidden Spreadsheet

*Previously cataloged as 5A10*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, settle into the hayloft of your heart. Let the screen door sigh shut behind you. The air today smells of old paper, damp earth, and the faint, electric hum of a laptop struggling to process the impossible. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a bureaucrat tried to quantify a shadow, a kitten accidentally broke the laws of data entry, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a glowing screen—that not everything needs to be put in a cell. Some things are meant to remain delightfully, beautifully uncalculated.

## I. The Vampire Audit

Taxman Ted had a problem. Or rather, he had a resident. 

Count Catula, the farm’s resident gothic aristocrat, was currently draped dramatically over a hay bale in the barn loft, his cape billowing in a breeze that didn't exist. Ted stood before him, clipboard in hand, his glasses reflecting the harsh, unforgiving light of a spreadsheet.

“Count Catula,” Ted said, his voice tight with the strain of a man trying to impose order on chaos. “As a non-living resident of this farm, you are not exempt from financial transparency!”

Catula clutched his cape to his chest, his eyes wide with theatrical outrage. “I am undead, Ted, not unpaid! My coffers are none of your concern! I pay my dues in atmospheric gloom and nocturnal pest control!”

Undeterred, Ted tapped his pen against his clipboard. “Gloom is not a deductible expense. Therefore, I have created the ‘Vampire Hours Log’ to track your nocturnal activities.”

Catula squinted at the paper. “Nocturnal Activity? Dramatic Exits? What is this?”

“It’s a comprehensive audit,” Ted explained, adjusting his glasses. “I need to track your blood consumption, your napping hours, and your dramatic exits.”

“Dramatic exits?” Catula gasped, his voice trembling. “That is persecution! Where is my eternal rest?!”

Ted didn't blink. He simply pointed to the bottom of the page. “Column F, Row 12. Eternal rest scheduled for never.”

## II. Ditto’s Digital Disaster

While Ted and Catula debated the tax implications of being a creature of the night, Ditto the Kitten had wandered into the barn office. He was fascinated by Ted’s laptop, which was open to the master farm spreadsheet. 

The screen glowed with rows and columns of neat, orderly numbers. Ditto’s silver fur crackled with static curiosity. 

“Formulas!” Ditto chirped, his eyes wide. “Formulas!”

He began to echo every function Ted had typed. And because Ditto’s echoes carry a certain, unpredictable magical resonance, the spreadsheet began to change.

First, the “Blood Expenditure” tab flickered. The neat sum of Chef Remy’s donated tomato juice vanished, replaced by a glowing, pulsating text: **SNACKS OWED: ∞**.

Next, Count Catula’s “Coffin Maintenance” budget auto-updated. The cells expanded, the font turned to a jagged, gothic script, and the budget requirement shifted to: **REQUIRES 700% MORE CAPES**.

Finally, the entire file shuddered. The cursor blinked rapidly, and a new cell auto-filled itself at the very top of the sheet in bold, red letters: **THIS SPREADSHEET IS CURSED. -Management.**

Ted stared at the screen, his eye twitching. “Why does it keep adding emojis to the depreciation column?! Why is there a bat next to ‘Taxable Undead Income’?!”

“Emojis!” Ditto echoed happily, batting at the screen. “Bats! Bats!”

## III. The Shadowy Exit

Count Catula, who had been watching this digital meltdown with a mixture of horror and vindication, finally reached his breaking point. He couldn't handle the pivot tables. He couldn't handle the emojis. 

“Audits are for the living!” Catula declared, his voice echoing with dramatic finality. “I refuse to be micromanaged by a haunted Excel file! I TRANSPARENTLY VANISH!”

With a swirl of his cape and a puff of theatrical smoke, the vampire melted into the shadows of the hayloft, leaving nothing behind but his heavy, velvet cloak on the floor.

Ted stood in the silence, blinking. He looked at the empty space where the vampire had been, then looked down at the cloak. Without missing a beat, he pulled out his pen and logged the item on his clipboard. 

“Charitable donation,” Ted muttered to himself. “Tax-deductible.”

## IV. Sir Whiskerton’s Gentle Correction

Sir Whiskerton, who had been observing the entire debacle from the rafters, leapt down gracefully, landing softly on the desk beside the glowing laptop. He looked at the cursed spreadsheet, then at Ted, and finally at the empty pile of velvet.

“Ted, my good man,” Whiskerton purred, his voice a calm, grounding rumble. “You cannot quantify a shadow. And you certainly cannot audit a creature whose primary currency is existential dread.”

Ted sighed, his shoulders slumping. “But the transparency score, Whiskerton. It’s ruined. The file is literally demanding more capes.”

Whiskerton gently reached out with one velvet paw and closed the laptop lid. The glowing green light vanished, plunging the desk back into the soft, dusty shadows of the barn. 

“Some things, Ted, are not meant to be balanced,” Whiskerton said gently. “Catula’s dignity, Ditto’s echoes, the night sky. They don't fit in your columns. And that is perfectly alright. Let the vampire have his shadows. Let the kitten have his chaos. And let yourself have a cup of tea.”

Ted looked at the closed laptop, then at the velvet cloak. He took a deep breath, the tension finally leaving his jaw. “I suppose,” Ted murmured, “a cup of tea would be acceptable. I’ll log it as a ‘Morale Enhancement Expense.’”

Whiskerton smiled. “An excellent choice, Ted.”

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Formula)

**Moral:**  
Even the undead deserve a little chaos. And more importantly, not everything in life needs to be measured, tracked, or put into a cell. True belonging means accepting the parts of our friends that don't fit neatly into our spreadsheets. Sometimes, the most beautiful things are the ones that refuse to be calculated.

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Later that night, the barn was quiet. Ted was asleep in his cot. But on the desk, the laptop screen flickered to life on its own. The cursor blinked in the dark. 

A single cell auto-filled: **FEED ME MORE NUMBERS, KITTEN.**

From the shadows, Ditto’s eyes glowed. “Numbers,” he whispered softly. “Numbers.”
