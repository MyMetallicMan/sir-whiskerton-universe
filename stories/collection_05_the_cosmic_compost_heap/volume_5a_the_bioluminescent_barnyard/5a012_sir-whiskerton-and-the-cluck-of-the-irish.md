# Sir Whiskerton and the Cluck of the Irish

*Previously cataloged as 5A12*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, settle into the hayloft of your heart. Let the screen door sigh shut behind you. The air today smells of turmeric, cheap glitter, and the faint, electric hum of a tiny chipmunk’s Napoleonic ambitions. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day the Valley Chicks discovered cultural appropriation via Pinterest, a chipmunk tried to tax the concept of rainbows, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a four-leaf clover—that real luck doesn't need a hashtag.

## I. The Festival of False Fortune

The Valley Chicks—Tiffany, Brittany, and Madison—had big plans for St. Patrick’s Day. They had spent the previous three days dyeing popcorn with turmeric, weaving clover out of green pipe cleaners, and arguing over the aesthetic merits of leprechaun hats.

“Like, obvi we’re basically Irish,” Tiffany declared, adjusting a tiny, sequined top hat that kept sliding over her left eye. “We love rainbows, and clover is totally a vibe.”

“Our pot of gold is gonna be so Insta-famous!” Brittany added, carefully arranging a mountain of bright yellow, turmeric-stained popcorn into a cauldron made from a hollowed-out pumpkin. 

“Hashtag Cluckin’ Lucky!” Madison chirped, taking a blurry selfie with the popcorn.

Their "Lucky Cluck" festival transformed the barnyard into a neon-green spectacle of questionable taste and unquestionable grift. Elvis the Rooster, ever the willing participant for a good snack, wore a collar of green sequins and manned the "Kiss the Rooster" booth, charging three corn kernels per smooch. Gnomeo the garden gnome, thrilled to be included, sat atop a hay bale wearing a green felt hat, serving as the target for the "Pin the Tail on the Leprechaun" game.

But the true architect of the festival’s chaos was Lucifer the Chipmunk. 

Dressed in a tiny green vest and wielding a bottle-cap crown, Lucifer had set up a "Lucky Licensing Office" beneath the old oak tree. He sat behind a clipboard, his beady eyes gleaming with the thrill of absolute, unregulated authority.

## II. The Scam of the Century

Lucifer’s "taxes" were as absurd as they were aggressively enforced. 

“Ten percent of all egg profits!” he squeaked, tapping his clipboard with a twig. “One 'lucky' acorn per animal! And exclusive rights to all rainbows! This immortal requires tribute! Also, have you heard about our lord and savior tax evasion?”

Doris the Hen nearly fainted when he demanded a cut of her morning yield. Rufus the Dog ate his "lucky" acorn, realized it was just a regular acorn painted green, and demanded a refund. Bessie the Tie-Dye Cow simply stared at him, her mood ring swirling a confused, muddy brown. “That’s, like, nature, man,” she rumbled gently. “You can’t tax a rainbow.”

“You can if you own the sky!” Lucifer squeaked back, though his voice cracked slightly.

Meanwhile, Mr. Ducky, sensing a market inefficiency, waddled up to the Valley Chicks’ turmeric popcorn "pot of gold." With a swift, practiced sleight of wing, he stole the entire cauldron, waddled to the other side of the barn, and sold it back to Tiffany as "Authentic Leprechaun Corn™."

“Limited-time offer!” Mr. Ducky quacked, his eyes shifting nervously. “Also, it’s definitely not yours. But it’s spiritually yours.”

Tiffany bought it immediately. The festival was descending into a beautiful, chaotic anarchy of imaginary taxes and stolen snacks.

## III. The Luck of the Farmer

Just as Lucifer was attempting to charge Rufus a "glow-tax" for his chartreuse fur, the Farmer wandered into the barnyard. 

He was utterly oblivious to the neon-green hysteria. He was wearing his usual straw hat, his single boot unlaced, and he was holding a small, delicate, perfectly green four-leaf clover he had just plucked from the pasture while weeding.

“Huh,” the Farmer murmured, looking at the clover with quiet appreciation. “Would ya look at that.”

The barnyard fell silent. The Valley Chicks gasped in unison. 

“OMG!” Tiffany shrieked, dropping her phone. “We manifested that! We’re the lucky charms! The vibes attracted it!”

Sir Whiskerton, who had been observing the entire debacle from the fencepost, adjusted his monocle with a slow, deliberate sigh. “No, Tiffany,” he purred dryly. “That’s called grass. Sometimes it mutates. It has nothing to do with your turmeric popcorn.”

Lucifer, sensing his empire of grift collapsing in the face of genuine, unmonetized luck, scrambled onto the fencepost. “That clover is holy!” he declared, his tail twitching with desperation. “For five kernels, you may bask in its glory! I am the Keeper of the Clover!”

Before Lucifer could set up a ticket booth, Porkchop the Pig ambled over. He was wearing a Hawaiian shirt patterned with tiny, cheerful shamrocks. He looked at the clover, looked at Lucifer, and then gently took the clover from the Farmer’s hand.

“Tastes like…” Porkchop mumbled, chewing thoughtfully, “…justice.”

He swallowed. The clover was gone. 

Lucifer stared at the pig, his bottle-cap crown slipping over his eyes. The spell of the grift was broken. The Farmer just smiled, patted Porkchop on the head, and wandered back toward the house, entirely unaware that he had just dismantled a tiny, furry dictatorship.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Shenanigans)

**Moral:**  
Luck is great, but a good lawyer is better. Or, at the very least, a pig who eats the evidence. True luck isn't something you can manufacture, monetize, or put in a cauldron. It’s the quiet, unexpected moments—a four-leaf clover in the grass, a friend who eats your taxes, a farmer who just wants to weed his garden. Real luck needs no hashtags, and it certainly doesn't require a licensing fee.

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Later that afternoon, the Valley Chicks sued Lucifer in "Farm Court," which was held on the porch and judged by Sir Whiskerton. 

“The defendant attempted to tax a rainbow,” Tiffany argued, pointing a dramatic wing at the chipmunk. “That’s, like, emotional distress.”

Lucifer crossed his arms, his tiny vest puffed out. “I was stimulating the local economy!”

Sir Whiskerton banged a wooden spoon against a tin bucket. “Order,” he purred. “The verdict is in. Everyone is guilty of aesthetic crimes, especially the duck. Case dismissed.”

Mr. Ducky waddled away, whistling innocently, already planning his next venture: "Authentic Leprechaun Water™."
