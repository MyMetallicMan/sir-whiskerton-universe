# Sir Whiskerton and the Influencer Interrupted

> *Previously cataloged as 4B2*

### Foreword

Ah, dear reader. Settle in. Adjust your posture. Perhaps dab a little lavender oil behind your ears. Today’s chronicle does not involve haunted haystacks, tax audits, or dairy-based enlightenment. No. Today’s story is a cautionary fable wrapped in glitter, dipped in mud, and served with a side of existential dread. It is the tale of the Valley Chicks, three hens whose ambition vastly outpaced their structural engineering skills, and a duck in a tie who saw a market gap and sprinted toward it like a feathered venture capitalist. 

I refer, of course, to *The Influencer Interrupted*. A story about hashtags, hubris, and the harsh, unfilterable reality that no amount of ring lights can make a collapsing chicken coop look chic. Buckle up. The algorithm is hungry.

### I. The Rise of #FarmHotties

It began, as most modern tragedies do, with a cracked phone screen and a ring light propped against a water bucket.

The Valley Chicks—Tiffany, Brittany, and Madison—gathered in the center of the coop, their tiny sunglasses glinting with the cold, hard light of ambition. They had watched the humans. They had seen the metrics. And they had drawn one irrefutable conclusion: farming was so last season.

“Okay, like, agriculture is totally out,” Madison announced, adjusting a feather boa that had seen better decades. “We’re pivoting to lifestyle content. Hashtag FarmHotties. Hashtag RusticGlam. Hashtag… whatever that other one is with the sparkle emoji.”

Tiffany nodded solemnly, her comb perfectly asymmetrical. “Our aesthetic is elevated pastoral. Think: hay bales, but make it fashion. Think: dirt, but make it *textured*.”

Brittany, ever the strategist, tapped a talon against the phone. “First post: DIY Coop Makeover! Sponsored by… us! And possibly the concept of self-belief!”

What followed was less a renovation and more a glitter-based hostile takeover.

They raided the farmhouse attic. They liberated a crystal chandelier that hadn’t swung since 1987. They dragged it into the coop, rigged it to a fence post with baling twine, and declared it a “statement perch.” They dumped three industrial-sized bags of cosmetic glitter over the nesting boxes. They installed a mirror made from a polished tractor hubcap. They practiced their “casual morning routines” in front of the ring light, which was currently powered by a car battery and a deeply concerning extension cord.

Then, they hit *Go Live*.

“Hey besties!” Tiffany chirped, fluffing her feathers to maximum volume. “Welcome to our sanctuary! Today we’re manifesting abundance, sipping herbal rainwater, and completely ignoring the fact that the support beams are, like, *very* load-bearing.”

The chat rolled. Slowly. Painfully.
`@CluckLover99: is this a cult?`
`@FarmCoreDaily: following for the vibes`
`@Bot_44291: ✨🐔`
`@Tiffany’sMom: eat your scratch grains honey`

Eleven followers. One human. Ten bots. And approximately four hundred dollars worth of glitter now embedded in the insulation.

Then—the coop sighed.

Not a metaphorical sigh. A structural, wooden, deeply exhausted sigh. The chandelier groaned. The twine strained. The mirror vibrated. Madison gasped. “Wait, is that—”

The roof did not fall. It *resigned*.

It collapsed in slow, glittering majesty, burying the ring light, the chandelier, and Tiffany’s left wing in a avalanche of straw, shattered crystal, and deeply ironic decor. The live-stream cut to static. Three thousand views registered. Two thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine were bots. One was Mr. Ducky, who had been watching from the fence line, taking notes.

### II. The Duck-Tastrophe

Mr. Ducky did not believe in accidents. He believed in pivots.

He waddled into the wreckage wearing a silk tie, a clip-on bowtie backup, and the serene confidence of a creature who had never once questioned his own branding.

“Ladies,” he quacked, adjusting his lapels. “Darling, visionary ladies. Your content is fire. Your aesthetic is disruptive. But let’s be brutally honest: your monetization strategy is *tragic*.”

Brittany blinked up from a pile of glitter and hay. “OMG. Like, yes?”

“Precisely,” Ducky purred. “You don’t need structural integrity. You need *synergy*. You need a product. You need… Organic Worm Serum.”

He produced a mason jar filled with what could only be described as aggressive mud. It sloshed. It glistened. It smelled faintly of Tuesday.

“Guaranteed to make your feathers shine,” Ducky continued, tapping the jar with a webbed foot. “Sustainably sourced. Ethically ambiguous. Clinically untested. But the packaging? *Impeccable*.”

The Chicks stared. The mud stared back.

“We’re, like, so sustainable,” Madison whispered, already reaching for it.

They slathered it on. All of it. Their feathers turned the color of wet pavement. Their combs dripped. They stood in the ruins of their coop, coated in premium-grade barnyard sludge, and smiled for the camera.

“SPA DAY WITH DUCKYCORP,” Brittany announced, hitting *Post*. “Link in bio! Use code MUD20 for twenty percent off your own existential crisis!”

The internet, as it tends to do, responded with the gentle, merciless weight of collective bewilderment.

`@SoilScienceDaily: is this a crime scene or a skincare routine?`
`@WormAppreciation: why is that duck wearing a tie?`
`@FarmCoreDaily: I stan the worms. they are low-key iconic.`
`@Bot_77312: ✨📉`

The video went viral. Not for the glamour. Not for the lifestyle. But because a duck in a tie had successfully convinced three hens that mud was a luxury good, and the algorithm, in its infinite wisdom, decided this was peak content.

The worms, watching from the soft earth below, did not comment. They simply shifted in the soil, collectively nodding. *We’re low-key iconic,* the dirt seemed to whisper. *We always have been.*

### III. The Fall of the Flock

I arrived to find the coop resembling a craft store that had been struck by a meteor. The Chicks were huddled in the center, covered head-to-toe in drying mud, sobbing quietly into their non-refundable serum jars. Mr. Ducky was already auctioning the salvaged chandelier crystals on FarmBay. The worms had apparently unionized.

I did not raise my voice. I did not sigh. I simply stepped over a shattered ring light, adjusted my monocle, and looked at the three mud-caked influencers.

“Let this be a lesson,” I said, my voice dry as last year’s hay. “Likes won’t feed you. Corn will. Also, never trust a duck in a tie. Ties are for humans who need to pretend they have a plan. Ducks just need water and a reasonable grasp of gravity.”

Tiffany sniffled, a clump of glitter falling from her beak. “But our engagement—”

Brittany cut her off, standing as tall as a mud-caked hen possibly could. “We’re influencers, Whiskerton! We don’t eat corn, we *style* it!”

I blinked. Slowly. The farm does not argue with delusion. It simply waits for it to dry.

“Style it,” I repeated. “Fascinating. Tell me, when the style fades, and the mud cracks, and the bots move on to the next glitter-based tragedy… what will you eat?”

Silence. The kind of silence that only exists when three hens realize, for the first time, that an aesthetic cannot be pecked.

Mr. Ducky cleared his throat. “I do accept payment in mealworms. Or exposure.”

I nudged a trough toward them with my paw. “Eat. Then molt. Then try again. But this time, build the perch first. Then hang the chandelier. The farm does not rush. It endures. And it prefers its influencers slightly less damp.”

They ate. Slowly. Grudgingly. The mud flaked. The glitter settled. The algorithm, mercifully, scrolled on.

### IV. The Moral (and the Post-Credit Comeback)

**Moral:** If you chase clout, you might trip over a worm. Authenticity doesn’t trend. It just stays. And it usually comes with a full feed trough.

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Three days later, the worms launched their own channel: *Dirtbag Diaries*. Their debut video? A slow, beautifully lit exposé titled “Organic Worm Serum: What They Don’t Tell You.” The camera panned to a jar of mud. A single worm slowly dragged itself across the glass. The caption read: *It’s still mud. But now it’s honest.* It hit two million views. Mr. Ducky tried to sue. He lost. The worms bought a tiny velvet couch.
