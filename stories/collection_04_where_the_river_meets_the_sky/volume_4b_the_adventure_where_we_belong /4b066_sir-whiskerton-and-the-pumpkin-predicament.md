# Sir Whiskerton and the Pumpkin Predicament

*Previously cataloged as 4B66*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, settle into the hayloft of your heart. Let the screen door sigh shut behind you. The air today is crisp, carrying the scent of woodsmoke, crushed leaves, and the faint, earthy perfume of a pumpkin so massive it possessed its own gravitational pull. 

Today’s tale is not merely a mystery of a missing gourd. It is a meditation on ambition, the physics of rolling vegetables, and the quiet grace of a farm that solves its problems not with anger, but with a shared slice of pie. 

## I. The Quiet Void

The morning mist clung to the south pasture like a secret too tender to speak aloud. It was the day of the county fair, and the farm was buzzing with the low, golden hum of anticipation. At the center of it all was Big Bertha.

Big Bertha was no ordinary pumpkin. She was a marvel of patience and sun: round as a promise, orange as a sunset held in cupped hands, and heavy with the quiet pride of a season well-tended. For weeks, she had sat in the patch like a throne waiting for its queen.

But when the Farmer arrived at dawn to load her onto the wagon, he found only an imprint in the earth.

He did not shout. He did not curse. He simply stood for a long moment, his straw hat in his hands, watching the mist curl around his boots. Then, softly, almost to himself:

“Huh. Must be Tuesday.”

Somewhere on the fencepost, Sir Whiskerton’s tail gave a single, slow flick. He descended with the grace of a thought unfolding, his monocle catching the morning light, his kilt—a subtle nod to ancestral steadiness—rippling in the breeze.

He found the Farmer kneeling by the empty patch, brushing soil from a stray vine. 

“No harm done,” the Farmer murmured, though his eyes lingered on the space where Bertha had been. “Just… curious.”

Whiskerton sat beside him. “Curious things rarely happen without reason,” he said. “And this farm has never been careless with what it grows.”

## II. Clues in the Dew

By the time Whiskerton reached the pumpkin patch, his trusty companions had gathered. 

Porkchop ambled over, his Hawaiian shirt fluttering (today’s pattern: autumn leaves doing jazz hands). “Found a thing!” he announced, holding up a scrap of orange peel. “Smells like… pie dreams.”

Nearby, Rufus glowed a soft gold-green—content, but alert. His nose twitched toward the orchard. “Whiskerton! You gotta see this!” He pointed a glowing paw at the damp soil.

Perched in the old apple tree, Sedgwick the barn owl blinked slowly, his feathers ruffled by wisdom rather than wind. “The earth speaks in drag marks,” he observed. “Not theft. Transport.”

Whiskerton surveyed the empty spot. “Roll marks,” he said, pointing to the shallow groove in the dew-damp soil. “It looks like Big Bertha was rolled away.”

“Rolled?” Porkchop exclaimed, his snout twitching nervously. “Who would roll a pumpkin that big? The physics alone are a nightmare! It must’ve weighed more than my mud-puddle!”

“Let’s follow the trail,” Sedgwick suggested, flapping down to a nearby fence post. “The culprit may not be far.”

The trail led them to the barnyard, where they found a curious sight: several strands of hay scattered across the ground, along with a faint smell of… molasses?

“Molasses?” Porkchop said, sniffing the air. “Why does it smell like molasses?”

“Odd,” Sedgwick said, tilting his head. “Hay and molasses are often used to lure livestock. Perhaps someone was attempting to bait an animal.”

“Or bribe one,” Whiskerton muttered, narrowing his eyes. 

Suddenly, Doris the Hen arrived, clipboard clutched to her chest, spectacles fogged with urgency. “This is unprecedented! A gourd-based displacement! I’m drafting a manifesto: *‘On the Right of Squash to Self-Determination’!*” She fainted upright for 17 seconds, then revived with a gasp. “The symbolic resonance is overwhelming!”

Whiskerton sighed gently. “Let’s keep following the trail, Doris. The mystery is not in the manifesto.”

## III. The Innocent Grazer

The trail of hay and molasses led them to the goat pen, where Clover the goat was chewing thoughtfully on a bit of orange rind. Her eyes widened—not with guilt, but surprise.

“Oh! Sir Whiskerton,” she said, voice warm as hayloft sun. “I found this near the haystack. Was wondering if anyone lost a snack.”

“You didn’t roll it here?” Whiskerton asked gently.

Clover laughed, a sound like wind chimes in a gentle storm. “That pumpkin weighs more than my entire philosophy of grazing! No, someone else moved it. But… they left molasses behind. Odd, isn’t it?”

“She’s telling the truth,” Sedgwick said, his keen eyes studying Clover closely. “Clover may have found the rind, but she’s not our thief.”

“Then we keep looking,” Whiskerton said, his resolve strengthening. “The trail isn’t cold yet.”

## IV. A Palace of Gourd

The trail led them to the edge of the orchard, where the slope grew steep and the leaves grew thick. And there, they found the culprits.

It was Nutters the squirrel, wearing his signature bottle-cap crown and a dandelion sash, accompanied by his gang: Kuàizhuǎ, Túntún, and Shǎ Lìzi. They were frantically trying to push Big Bertha up a small hill, their tiny paws slipping and sliding as they struggled with the pumpkin’s massive weight.

Shǎ Lìzi was sobbing quietly, having eaten half the molasses bait. Túntún’s cheek pouches were so full of pumpkin seeds he could barely squeak. And Nutters was directing traffic with the frantic energy of a Napoleonic warlord.

“Push, you fools! The Winter Palace of the Acorn must be built before the frost!” Nutters shrieked.

“Well, well,” Whiskerton said, stepping forward. “Caught in the act.”

The squirrels froze. Nutters adjusted his bottle-cap crown, trying to salvage his dignity. “Ah! Sir Whiskerton! We were just… relocating this architectural marvel for the betterment of the woodland!”

“A squirrel house?” Porkchop said, looking both confused and impressed. “That’s… actually kind of clever.”

“But it’s not your pumpkin,” Whiskerton said, his voice firm but devoid of malice. “Big Bertha belongs to the Farmer, and she’s supposed to be entered in the county fair.”

Nutters’ shoulders slumped. The grand illusion of his dairy-based hegemony faded, leaving just a small, tired squirrel in a bottle-cap hat. “We didn’t mean any harm,” he squeaked. “We just wanted a home that was warm. It’s getting cold.”

Rufus’s glow softened to ember-warmth. He stepped forward, tail wagging at a gentle 90 BPM. “You wanted a house,” he said simply. “Not a prize.”

## V. The Resolution (A Shared Harvest)

With the squirrels’ help—and a great deal of Porkchop’s “muscle” (which mostly involved rolling on his back and pushing with his hind legs)—they rolled Big Bertha back to the pumpkin patch, where she was restored to her rightful place.

The Farmer was waiting. He looked at the exhausted animals, the glowing dog, the kilted cat, and the three tiny, trembling squirrels. 

He didn’t scold them. He simply knelt. 

“She’s a fine pumpkin,” he said softly to Nutters. “Strong walls. Good roof.” He paused, then reached into his pocket and pulled out a trowel. “But she’s meant for the fair. However…” He pointed to a patch behind the shed, where dozens of smaller, sweeter pumpkins were growing. “There’s plenty more. Help me dig tomorrow. We’ll plant something new next spring—just for you.”

Nutters’ eyes widened. “Really?”

“Really,” the Farmer said. 

Later that evening, the farm gathered for pie. The Farmer had baked two: a massive one from a spare pumpkin for the animals, and a tiny, perfectly crusted one for the squirrels. 

As they ate, Doris tapped her clipboard. “I must amend my manifesto,” she declared. “*‘On the Interspecies Housing Cooperatives and the Ethical Distribution of Pie.’*”

Whiskerton smiled, taking a sip of his cream. “Well done, team. Once again, order has been restored to the farm.”

“And once again, I didn’t get any credit,” Rufus muttered, though his tail was wagging happily.

“You got a pumpkin seed earlier,” Whiskerton reminded him.

“Fair point,” Rufus admitted, swallowing it whole.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Snack)

**Moral:**  
Sometimes, what looks like a crime is just a cry for shelter. Misunderstandings can lead to mischief, but with teamwork, patience, and a little forgiveness, even the biggest problems can be resolved. And remember: always think before you roll off with someone else’s pumpkin.

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Later that night, Nutters sat on the fencepost, polishing his bottle-cap crown. He looked at the tiny pumpkin seed in his paw, then at the vast, starry sky. 

“Kuàizhu!” he whispered fiercely. “Tomorrow, we steal the Farmer’s watering can! The palace needs a moat!”

From the shadows, Shǎ Lìzi sniffled. “Can the moat be made of molasses?”

Nutters sighed, adjusting his dandelion sash. “Yes, Shǎ Lìzi. The moat can be made of molasses.”
