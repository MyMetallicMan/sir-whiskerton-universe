# Sir Whiskerton and the Great Kitten Gaokao Escape

*Previously cataloged as 4C7*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, sharpen your pencils—or your claws, as the situation demands. Today’s tale is not about education. It is about the profound, hilarious, and deeply necessary collision between the rigid metrics of the outside world and the fluid, chaotic grace of the farm. It is the story of a tiny kitten, a rotten potato, and a glowing dog who proves that sometimes, the smartest thing you can do with a standardized test is eat it. 

Prepare for a lesson in academic absurdity, where the only thing being graded is sheer, unadulterated mirth.

## I. The Exam Begins (Against All Odds)

The barn had been transformed into a high-stakes testing center, or at least, that is what Taxman Ted insisted. Ted, a visiting bureaucrat with a clipboard and a profound misunderstanding of feline biology, had spent the morning arranging three desks in a perfect, joyless triangle. 

“This is a serious examination!” Ted declared, adjusting his spectacles as he ushered Ditto into the center desk. “No cheating. No distractions. And absolutely no napping!”

Ditto sat in the oversized wooden chair, his silver fur crackling with the static of pure, unfiltered anxiety. He blinked his wide, saucer-like eyes. “Napping? Napping?” he echoed, his voice a high, frantic chirp. 

Sir Whiskerton, perched on a nearby hay bale in his kilt, adjusted his monocle with a slow, deliberate sigh. “Ted,” he said, his voice dry as autumn leaves, “he is a kitten. His entire existence is a nap with brief, chaotic intermissions for mischief. To ask him not to nap is to ask the sun not to shine.”

Ted ignored him, tapping his pen against the clipboard. “The Kitten Gaokao is a rigorous assessment of Advanced Mouse-Chasing Theory and Spatial Pounce Dynamics. It will determine his future trajectory.”

Ignoring all logic, Ted locked Ditto inside the testing zone with one exam paper, two sharpened pencils (one of which immediately rolled under a hay bale, fleeing the scene), and zero survival instincts. 

As Ted marched out, the heavy barn doors clicked shut. The silence was absolute. 

Ditto stared at the paper. The words swam. His ears pinned back. His fur stood on end, making him look like a startled dandelion. He didn’t know the answers. He only knew the sound of pressure. And so, softly, he echoed the only word that felt safe: “Test… test… test…”

## II. The Potato-Powered Jailbreak

Outside, Chester the Handyman was oiling his wrench when he heard the frantic, looping chirp. He peered through a knothole in the barn wall and saw the tiny, trembling kitten.

Chester’s heart, a vast and chaotic engine of goodwill, immediately engaged. He didn’t see an exam; he saw a creature in distress. And Chester’s solution to distress was always, invariably, structural.

“The Handyman’s Handbook says nothing about academic integrity!” Chester declared to no one in particular, pulling a comic book from his back pocket (which he firmly believed was the handbook, albeit upside-down). “If the problem is a wall, the solution is a tunnel!”

He scavenged a profoundly rotten potato from the compost heap, strapped it to a rusted fishing reel, and attached the whole contraption to a hand-crank. The machine whirred, sputtered, and emitted a smell so potent, so deeply offensive to the laws of nature, that Bessie the Cow paused mid-chew, her mood ring flashing a distressed, muddy brown.

With a mighty heave and a joyous shout, Chester engaged the crank. 

*FWUMP.*

He burst through the barn wall in a shower of splinters and potato mush, landing in a heroic, albeit fragrant, heap. 

At that exact moment, Rufus the Dog bounded into the barn. He had smelled paper. To Rufus, paper meant love. It meant notes from Martha, letters from the Farmer, grocery lists scented with lavender. Paper was a snack of the soul.

He saw the exam paper. He thought: *Feast.*

“A+ for flavor!” Rufus barked joyfully, his tail wagging at a rhythmic 140 BPM, his chartreuse glow pulsing bright with excitement as he crunched the paper into a soggy, happy pulp.

Ditto, startled, then instantly delighted by the sudden explosion of joy and the smell of rotten potato, stopped trembling. His static fur smoothed. He looked at Rufus, then at the shredded paper, and let out a happy, resonant echo of the only sound that mattered:

“Crunch! Crunch!” 

It was, officially, his final exam response.

## III. The Grading Debacle

When Taxman Ted returned, clipboard raised to record a perfect score, he found not a testing center, but a masterpiece of joyful ruin.

The exam was shredded, soggy, and half-digested. Chester stood proudly in a halo of potato mush, holding a piece of cardboard that read *Mission Accomplished?* in shaky crayon. Rufus lay on his side, contentedly gnawing the eraser off the second pencil, his glow warm as embers. And Ditto? Curled atop Rufus’s belly, fast asleep, one tiny paw resting over the dog’s glowing tag.

Ted’s voice cracked. “This is unacceptable! Where is the academic rigor?! Where is the data?!”

Sir Whiskerton leapt down, landing with the grace of a falling leaf. “It is in Rufus’s stomach,” he said gently. “Along with your dignity.”

Before Ted could protest further, the Farmer appeared in the doorway, straw tucked behind one ear, single boot unlaced. He took in the scene—the potato tunnel, the sleeping kitten, the dog radiating pure, unearned joy—and smiled. 

He walked over, knelt beside Rufus, and placed a hand on Ditto’s back. The kitten stirred, blinked, and whispered one word—not an echo, but his own:

“Okay.”

The Farmer nodded. Then, from his pocket, he pulled a small, hand-stamped diploma made from a repurposed feed bag. On it, in careful script, it read:

> **Ditto the Kitten**  
> For Demonstrating Courage in the Face of Unnecessary Pressure,  
> And for Choosing Joy Over Judgment.  
> — The Farm, 4C7

He handed it to Ditto, who held it gently in his teeth, then tucked it under his paw like a treasure.

“Kid’s got guts,” the Farmer said softly. Then, to Rufus: “And by guts, I mean literally—drop that pencil.”

Rufus obeyed. Then licked the Farmer’s hand.

## The Moral (and the Post-Credit Snack)

**Moral:**  
When life gives you standardized tests, eat the instructions. Not out of defiance, but because some things are not meant to be measured. A kitten’s curiosity, a dog’s loyalty, a handyman’s love expressed through potato-powered tunnels—these are not data points. They are the very fabric of belonging. On this farm, you do not have to pass a test to prove you are smart. You just have to be here.

**Post-Credit Scene:**  
Later that week, Rufus received an honorary degree from “Chew-versity” (a title bestowed by Porkchop during a mud-puddle ceremony). His thesis, presented via enthusiastic tail-wag and a single, perfectly chewed index card, was titled:  
*“Why All Exams Taste Like Desperation (And How Love Tastes Better).”*

He passed with flying colors. Mostly green.
