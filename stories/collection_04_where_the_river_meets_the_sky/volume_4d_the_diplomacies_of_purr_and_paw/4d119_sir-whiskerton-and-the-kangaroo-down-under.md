# Sir Whiskerton and the Kangaroo Down Under

*Previously cataloged as 4D119*

## Foreword

Ah, dear reader, settle into the hayloft of your heart. Let the screen door sigh shut behind you. The air today smells of crisp autumn leaves, crushed clover, and the faint, dusty scent of the Outback. 

Do not be alarmed. This is simply Tuesday on the farm: the day a kangaroo brought a bit of the Land Down Under to the barnyard, a glowing dog tried to box with his tail, and Sir Whiskerton reminded everyone—with one velvet paw on a wombat’s stubby nose—that family is the greatest treasure of all, even when they’re a bit grumpy.

## I. The Arrival of Kanga Ruby

It all began on a crisp autumn morning, just as the sun was peeking over the horizon, casting a golden, honeyed glow over the farm. The animals were going about their usual routines. Doris the Hen was clucking about the socio-political implications of a missing worm, Rufus the Dog was chasing his tail with his usual luminous enthusiasm, and Sir Whiskerton was perched on the barn roof in his kilt, surveying his domain with a satisfied flick of his tail.

Suddenly, a loud, rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* echoed through the air, growing louder with each passing second. The ground actually vibrated. The animals froze, their ears twitching in unison. 

Before anyone could react, a kangaroo bounded into the farmyard. Her powerful legs propelled her in great, soaring leaps. She landed with a final, dramatic *thud* in the center of the barnyard, kicking up a small cloud of dust. Her large, expressive eyes scanned the crowd of bewildered animals.

“G’day, mates!” she called out in a thick, warm Australian accent. “Name’s Ruby—Kanga Ruby, if ya like. I’ve come a long way from the Land Down Under, and I reckon I could use a bit of rest and relaxation.”

The animals stared in awe. None of them had ever seen a kangaroo before. Even Sir Whiskerton, who prided himself on his unflappable composure, was momentarily speechless, his monocle slipping a fraction of an inch.

“A kangaroo?” Doris finally squawked, breaking the silence, her wire-rimmed spectacles sliding down her beak. “What in the name of cluck is a kangaroo doing here?!”

“Cluck!” Harriet echoed, tilting her head.

“Here!” Lillian added, fainting dramatically onto a nearby pile of hay. She would remain there for exactly 17 seconds.

Ruby chuckled, her long ears twitching. “Well, it’s a bit of a long story, but the short version is I’ve been on the run from a mob of angry wombats. Nasty little blighters, they are. Thought I’d hop on over to your lovely farm for a bit of peace and quiet.”

Sir Whiskerton, having regained his composure, leapt down from the barn roof with the grace of a falling leaf. He approached Ruby with his usual air of quiet authority. “Ahem. Welcome to the farm, Miss Ruby. I am Sir Whiskerton, the resident detective and problem solver. If you’re in need of assistance, I’m sure we can accommodate you.”

Ruby grinned, revealing a set of surprisingly sharp teeth. “Cheers, mate! I appreciate the warm welcome. And don’t worry, I won’t be any trouble. In fact, I’d be happy to teach you all a thing or two about boxing—it’s a national pastime back home.”

The animals exchanged excited glances. Boxing? This was something new, and they were eager to learn.

## II. Boxing Lessons with Kanga Ruby

True to her word, Ruby quickly became the star of the farm. She set up a makeshift boxing ring in the barnyard, complete with hay bales for corners and a rope made of twisted vines. The animals lined up to take lessons, each one eager to try their paw, hoof, or wing at the sport.

First up was Rufus the Dog, who bounded into the ring with his usual enthusiasm, his chartreuse glow pulsing at a bright, excited 140 BPM. “Alright, Ruby, show me what you’ve got!” he barked, wagging his tail furiously.

Ruby chuckled and adjusted the tiny boxing gloves she’d fashioned out of old socks. “Alright, mate, let’s start with the basics. Keep your paws up, like this, and always stay light on your feet.”

Rufus mimicked her stance, but his tail kept wagging, throwing off his center of gravity. After a few minutes of stumbling around the ring, he finally managed to land a playful, gentle punch on Ruby’s padded glove. “I did it!” he yelped, jumping up and down.

“Good on ya, Rufus!” Ruby said, giving him a thumbs-up. “You’ve got potential, mate.”

Next was Porkchop the Pig, who waddled into the ring with a determined look on his face, his Hawaiian shirt bright against the dusty ring. “Alright, Ruby, let’s see if I can’t knock you out with my famous left hook,” he said, puffing out his chest.

Ruby laughed and raised her gloves. “Bring it on, Porkchop!”

The two sparred for a few minutes, with Porkchop landing a few solid, enthusiastic punches before Ruby gently tapped him on the snout with a well-placed jab. “Not bad, mate,” she said, grinning. “You’ve got a bit of a swing on you.”

Even Sir Whiskerton couldn’t resist trying his paw at boxing. He stepped into the ring with his usual air of confidence, his monocle glinting in the sunlight. “Very well, Miss Ruby, let’s see if your boxing skills can match my feline agility.”

Ruby raised an eyebrow. “Alright, mate, let’s see what you’ve got.”

The two circled each other, with Sir Whiskerton darting in and out, landing quick, precise, velvet-soft punches. Ruby, however, was no slouch. She countered with powerful jabs and hooks, forcing Sir Whiskerton to stay on his toes. In the end, the match ended in a respectful draw, with both combatants panting and grinning.

“Not bad, Whiskers,” Ruby said, clapping him on the back. “You’ve got some moves.”

Sir Whiskerton adjusted his monocle and smirked. “Of course. I am, after all, the farm’s foremost problem solver.”

## III. The Mystery of the Missing Joey

For a few days, life on the farm was filled with laughter, boxing matches, and the occasional dramatic fainting spell from Lillian the Hen. But then, one morning, Ruby woke up to find her joey, Joey (yes, she named him Joey), missing from her pouch.

“Joey!” Ruby cried, her voice filled with panic, her long ears pinning back. “Where are you, little mate?”

The animals gathered around, their faces filled with concern. “What’s wrong, Ruby?” Doris asked, flapping her wings, her clipboard trembling. “Is it a kidnapping? A foul play?!”

“My joey’s gone!” Ruby said, her eyes wide with fear. “He was in my pouch last night, but now he’s nowhere to be found!”

Sir Whiskerton stepped forward, his tail flicking with quiet determination. “Fear not, Miss Ruby. I, Sir Whiskerton, will solve this mystery and bring your joey back safely.”

Ruby nodded, her eyes filled with gratitude. “Thank you, mate. I don’t know what I’d do without him.”

## IV. The Investigation and the Wombat Connection

Sir Whiskerton wasted no time in gathering clues. He first questioned the animals. Rufus, his glow dimming to a worried amber, scratched his head. “Well, I did hear some rustling near the barn, but I thought it was just the wind.”

Porkchop shook his head. “Nope, nothing. But if I find out who took Joey, they’re gonna get a taste of my left hook!”

Sir Whiskerton then examined the area around Ruby’s sleeping spot. He noticed a few small, heavy footprints leading away from the barn, but they were too faint to identify. “Hmm,” he muttered, his keen eyes scanning the ground. “These prints are unfamiliar. They don’t belong to any of the farm animals.”

“Un-fam-iliar!” Ditto chirped, his silver fur crackling with static. “Un-fam-iliar!”

Sir Whiskerton sighed gently. “Yes, Ditto, unfamiliar. Now, if you’ll excuse me, I need to focus.”

As Sir Whiskerton followed the footprints, he began to piece together the mystery. The prints led to the edge of the farm, where the woods began. There, he found a small, burrow-like hole in the ground, surrounded by freshly dug dirt. 

“A burrow,” Sir Whiskerton mused. “And these claw marks… they belong to a wombat.”

Suddenly, a rustling sound came from the bushes, and a group of small, stocky animals emerged. They had short legs, stubby tails, and a deeply grumpy expression on their faces. 

“Wombats!” Ruby exclaimed, hopping over to join Sir Whiskerton. “What are you lot doing here?”

One of the wombats stepped forward, his beady eyes narrowing. “We’ve come for what’s ours, Ruby. You thought you could escape with our treasure, but we’ve found you.”

Sir Whiskerton raised an eyebrow, stepping slightly in front of Ruby. “Treasure? What treasure?”

The wombat pointed a stubby paw at Ruby. “She stole our golden acorn! It’s a sacred relic, and we want it back.”
