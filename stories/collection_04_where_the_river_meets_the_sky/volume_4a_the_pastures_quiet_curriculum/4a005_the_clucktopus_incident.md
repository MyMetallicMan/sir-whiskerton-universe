# Sir Whiskerton and the Clucktopus Incident

> *Previously cataloged as 4A25*

---

## Act 1: The Beast from the Deep

The morning light spilled across the pasture in long, golden ribbons, and Captain Cluckbeard stood tall upon the deck of his magnificent vessel — a repurposed wheelbarrow with a mop handle for a mast, a dish towel for a sail, and a toilet paper roll taped to his eye like the finest spyglass in all the seven seas (or at least the seven acres).

"Steady as she goes, mates!" Cluckbeard squawked, his tiny pirate hat — actually a bottle cap — wobbling atop his feathered head. "The tides of adventure call us ever onward!"

Squawk, his first mate, huddled behind a bag of chicken feed. "Captain, we're in a pasture. There are no tides."

"THAT'S WHAT THE SEA WANTS YOU TO THINK!" Cluckbeard spun, his beak gleaming with conviction. "The ocean is a trickster, Squawk. She wears many masks. Today, she wears... *grass*."

Pluck, the second mate, was busy trying to untangle his foot from a piece of twine he'd mistaken for rigging. "Captain, I don't think—"

"BEHOLD!" Cluckbeard thrust his toilet-paper telescope toward the horizon — which was really just the fence line — and froze. His beady eyes went wide. His beak fell open.

There, grazing in the middle distance, stood Bessie the Tie-Dye Cow. Her coat was a swirl of pastel purples and blues and pinks, the result of an unfortunate incident with a tie-dye kit that no one had ever fully explained. But it was not her coat that caught Cluckbeard's attention.

It was her udder.

Swollen with morning milk, speckled with spots that seemed to shift in the light, Bessie's udder swayed gently as she shifted her weight. The spots — some dark, some light, some an unsettling shade of lavender — created a pattern that, from a certain angle, in a certain light, with a certain level of sleep deprivation, might have looked like a great, many-tentacled beast from the briny deep.

"By the barnacles," Cluckbeard whispered. "It's... the Clucktopus."

Squawk peeked over the feed bag. "The what?"

"The Clucktopus!" Cluckbeard repeated, louder. "The legendary beast of the deep! Half chicken, half octopus, all terror! It's been spoken of in hushed whispers for generations! And now — NOW — it rises from the depths to claim our souls!"

Squawk squinted at Bessie. "Captain, that's just Bessie. The cow. She makes the milk we put in our tea."

"THAT'S WHAT SHE WANTS YOU TO THINK!" Cluckbeard grabbed the garden rake that served as the ship's harpoon. "She's wearing a disguise, Squawk! A clever, dairy-based disguise! But I see through it! I see the truth! The Clucktopus lives!"

Bessie, meanwhile, had noticed the commotion. She lifted her head, blinked her large, calm eyes, and said, in her slow, dreamy voice: "The universe is speaking to me through my own udder, man."

"IT SPEAKS!" Cluckbeard shrieked. "IT SPEAKS IN RIDDLES! PREPARE THE HARPOON!"

---

## Act 2: The Battle of the Bovine

The wheelbarrow ship lurched forward, its wheels creaking, its dish-towel sail flapping uselessly in the still morning air. Cluckbeard stood at the prow, garden rake held aloft like a trident of destiny.

"Onward, crew! For glory! For honor! For the extermination of the dread Clucktopus!"

Squawk had buried himself so deep in the feed bag that only his tail feathers were visible. "WE'RE ALL GONNA DIE!" he screamed, muffled but emphatic.

Pluck, who had finally freed his foot from the twine, looked at the rake, then at Bessie, then back at the rake. "Captain, I'm not sure this is a sea monster. I'm not sure this is even a *monster*. I'm not sure this is anything other than a cow."

"THAT'S EXACTLY WHAT THE CLUCKTOPUS WANTS YOU TO THINK!" Cluckbeard hurled the rake with all his might.

The rake spun through the air in a graceful arc, glinting in the morning sun, and bounced harmlessly off Bessie's left flank with a soft *thwump*.

Bessie did not flinch. She did not moo. She simply turned her head, looked at the rake lying in the grass, and said: "Everything is connected, man. The rake, the grass, the udder... it's all one."

"IT MOCKS US!" Cluckbeard roared. "IT SPEAKS IN KOANS! WE MUST STRIKE AGAIN!"

But before he could launch another assault, Bessie's udder swayed once more — a slow, hypnotic pendulum of speckled promise — and Cluckbeard found himself... pausing.

The spots. They were mesmerizing. They seemed to pulse with a rhythm that matched his own heartbeat. He felt his knees go weak. His beak hung open. The world seemed to soften at the edges.

"Captain?" Pluck waved a wing in front of Cluckbeard's face. "Captain, are you okay?"

"She's... she's using her powers," Cluckbeard breathed. "The Clucktopus... she's... *hypnotizing* me."

Bessie, in her own world entirely, was humming a tune that no one else could hear. Her udder continued its gentle sway. "The moon and the sun are just reflections of the same dairy-based truth," she murmured.

Pluck looked at the udder. He looked at Cluckbeard. He looked at Squawk, who had emerged from the feed bag just enough to witness the scene. Then he said, very slowly and very carefully: "Captain. Perhaps we should... investigate. Scientifically. For research purposes."

Cluckbeard blinked. "Research?"

"Yes, Captain. We could... *milk* the beast. To determine its nature. To see what it's made of. For science."

There was a long pause. Then Cluckbeard's beak curved into a grin. "For science, you say?"

"For science," Pluck confirmed.

"Then let it be written in the ship's log!" Cluckbeard declared. "Today, we milk the Clucktopus — FOR SCIENCE!"

What followed was a scene of such profound confusion that the grass itself seemed to lean away in bewilderment.

Squawk was ordered to approach from the rear. He did so on trembling legs, his eyes squeezed shut, chanting "I'm not here, I'm not here, I'm not here" under his breath. Pluck stood at the ready with a bucket — a real bucket, borrowed from the barn, because apparently "for science" required proper equipment.

Bessie, for her part, did not resist. She did not even seem to notice. She was too busy having what she would later describe as "a profound spiritual experience involving the interconnectedness of all beings and also a really nice patch of clover."

The milking itself took approximately four minutes. Squawk fainted twice. Pluck got kicked once, but only gently. And at the end of it, they had one bucket of fresh, warm milk — and two deeply traumatized roosters who would never look at a cow the same way again.

"We... we did it," Cluckbeard whispered, staring at the bucket. "We conquered the Clucktopus."

"Captain," Pluck said, "I don't think we conquered anything. I think we just milked a cow."

"NONSENSE!" Cluckbeard dipped a wing into the bucket and held it up, dripping with white. "Behold! The essence of the beast! The Clucktopus Elixir!"

Bessie, drifting back to ordinary consciousness, looked at the bucket and smiled a slow, knowing smile. "I have achieved udder enlightenment," she said.

## Act 3: The Truth Comes Out

The bottling operation was well underway by the time Sir Whiskerton arrived.

Cluckbeard and his crew had set up a small table at the edge of the pasture, using the wheelbarrow as a display stand. On the table sat a row of glass bottles, each one filled with milk and sealed with a cork. Pluck had hand-lettered labels that read "CLUCKTOPUS ELIXIR — For Science!" Squawk was huddled under the table, refusing to come out.

"Avast!" Cluckbeard called as Sir Whiskerton approached. "Have you come to purchase the miraculous essence of the deep, good sir?"

Sir Whiskerton — a cat of considerable dignity, with whiskers that could cut glass and a tail that expressed more emotion than most faces — stopped in his tracks. He looked at the bottles. He looked at Cluckbeard. He looked at the wheelbarrow ship. He looked at Bessie, who was now sitting in the grass with a serene expression, humming to herself.

"What," Sir Whiskerton said, "is happening here."

"We have captured and milked the dread Clucktopus!" Cluckbeard announced proudly. "And we are now selling its elixir to discerning customers at a very reasonable price!"

"THERE IS NO DISCOUNT FOR BULK PURCHASES!" Pluck added. "We checked."

Sir Whiskerton's tail twitched. He walked over to Bessie, who looked up at him with her big, calm eyes.

"Bessie," he said. "Are you alright?"

"I have achieved udder enlightenment," Bessie replied. "The universe is a cow, and the cow is the universe, and also I think someone milked me. It was nice."

Sir Whiskerton closed his eyes. He counted to ten. He opened his eyes again.

"Cluckbeard," he said, in the tone of a cat who has reached the end of his patience but is too polite to show it. "That is a cow. That has always been a cow. There is no such thing as a Clucktopus."

Cluckbeard gasped. "You dare deny the evidence of your own eyes? Look at her spots! Look at her udder! Look at the way she speaks in riddles!"

"She's a tie-dye cow who got into the clover patch," Sir Whiskerton said. "She's not a sea monster. She's not a goddess. She's just... Bessie."

There was a long, heavy silence.

Then Cluckbeard said, quietly: "But... but the adventure..."

"The adventure," Sir Whiskerton said, "was you attacking a cow with a rake, then milking her without permission, then bottling the results and trying to sell them as monster juice."

Cluckbeard's beak opened. Closed. Opened again.

"...So no one's going to buy the elixir?"

"No one is going to buy the elixir."

Sir Whiskerton confiscated the bottles. He poured the milk into a proper container — one that hadn't been labeled with words like "EXISTENTIAL" or "DREAD." He gave Cluckbeard a very long, very quiet look that communicated, without words, the profound disappointment of a cat who had hoped for better.

Then he turned to leave.

And that was when Porkchop arrived.

Porkchop — a pig of simple pleasures and even simpler motivations — had been drawn by the commotion. He sniffed the air. He saw the bucket. He saw the milk.

"Is that milk?" Porkchop asked.

"It was," Sir Whiskerton said. "Now it's evidence."

Porkchop considered this for a moment. Then he walked over to the bucket, stuck his snout in, and drank the entire thing in one long, satisfied gulp.

"Evidence," Porkchop said, licking his lips, "is delicious."

## Act 4: The Aftermath

In the days that followed, several things happened.

**First:** Bessie started a wellness cult.

She didn't mean to. She simply continued saying things like "The udder is a metaphor for the soul" and "We are all just milk in the great bucket of existence," and somehow, a small group of farm animals began to gather around her each morning to listen.

Squawk, despite his trauma, became her most devoted follower. He would sit at her feet (hooves?) and nod along as she spoke about the interconnectedness of all dairy-based life. "She understands me," Squawk said. "She understands the *dread*."

**Second:** Mr. Ducky saw an opportunity.

Mr. Ducky — a duck of questionable ethics and impeccable business sense — had heard about the Clucktopus Elixir. He had heard about the confiscation. He had heard about Porkchop drinking the evidence. And he had realized, with the clarity of a true entrepreneur, that there was still money to be made.

Within a week, a new product appeared at the Barnyard Market. It was a small bottle of milk, hand-labeled with elegant script. The label read:

> **CLUCKTOPUS MILK**
> *Now with 100% More Existential Dread!*
> *As featured in the Great Dairy Incident of Last Tuesday*
> *300% markup — because you're worth it*

Mr. Ducky sold out in an hour.

Sir Whiskerton, upon discovering this, said nothing. He simply walked away, his tail expressing a complex emotion that roughly translated to "I am too tired for this."

**Third:** Squawk developed a phobia of polka dots.

It started when he saw a polka-dot pillow in the barn. He screamed. He ran. He hid behind the feed bag — the same feed bag he had hidden in during the Incident — and refused to come out for three hours.

Pluck tried to console him. "It's just a pillow, Squawk."

"IT HAS SPOTS!" Squawk shrieked. "SPOTS LIKE THE CLUCKTOPUS! THE CLUCKTOPUS IS EVERYWHERE!"

Bessie, passing by, paused and looked at the pillow. "The spots are just the universe's way of saying hello," she said.

Squawk fainted.

## Post-Credit Scene

*The Barnyard Market. Late afternoon.*

A small stall stood at the edge of the market, adorned with a hand-painted sign:

> **CLUCKTOPUS MILK**
> *Limited Edition*
> *Now with 100% More Existential Dread!*

Behind the stall, Mr. Ducky adjusted his bow tie and smiled a smile that did not reach his eyes. A line of farm animals stretched around the corner. A goat was buying three bottles. A sheep was buying five.

"Thank you for your patronage," Mr. Ducky said, accepting a fistful of grass-clippings (the local currency) from a very earnest chicken. "Remember: the dread is *artisanal*."

In the distance, Sir Whiskerton watched from a fence post. His tail flicked once. Twice.

He did not intervene.

Some battles, he had learned, were not worth fighting.

Some battles were better left to the ducks.
