# Sir Whiskerton and the Snotty Surprise

*A Tale of Separation, Sympathy, and the Glue That Binds Us*

*From the Collection 1 archive — Story 106*

---

Ah, dear reader. Come closer, but perhaps not too close. The story I bring you today is a tale of... well, let us call it unrefined effluence. It is a story of a Great Unpleasantness that descended upon the farm, threatening not just hygiene, but harmony. It is, in essence, a comedy of manners where the manners are the first casualty.

## I. The Peace, Profound and Fragile

Morning on the farm was a masterpiece of quiet interconnection. Sir Whiskerton held his sunbeam court atop the barn roof, a sovereign surveying a kingdom at peace. Below, the rhythm of care prevailed: The Farmer, having successfully located both boots (a red-letter day), was murmuring to the scarecrow about the "patient ambition of root vegetables." Beatrice hummed at her hives, the bees weaving a soft, gold-threaded harmony into the air.

In the barn, Doris the Hen held her morning gossip tribunal, adjudicating pecking-order disputes with the gravity of a supreme court justice. Beside her, Porkchop the Pig luxuriated in a perfect, sun-warmed patch of dust, a philosopher-king of indolence. All was as it should be—a fragile, beautiful ecosystem of mutual, if sometimes begrudging, tolerance.

The peace was shattered not by a roar, but by a screech of such profound, worldview-upending horror that Sir Whiskerton's monocle nearly popped from its place.

## "BY ALL THAT IS SACRED AND SANITARY! WHAT IS THAT ABOMINATION?!"

Doris stood rigid, one wing extended like a dagger, pointing at the great, weathered plank of the barn door. There, glistening with a vile, internal luminosity in the slanting morning light, was **The Specimen**.

It was a mucus monument. A snot-sculpture. It hung, pendulous and greenish-gold, the size of a grown man's fist—a glistening, gelatinous testament to some profound internal cataclysm.

A stunned silence fell, broken only by the distant, oblivious hum of Beatrice's bees.

## II. The Investigation of the Unholy Artefact

Porkchop heaved his bulk upright and ambled over, squinting. "Hmm. Remarkable topography," he mused, his voice thick with scholarly curiosity. "The viscosity... the chromatic variation... Doris, I do believe you're overreacting. This isn't an abomination. It's a piglet."

"PIGLET!" chirped Ditto, Sir Whiskerton's ever-echoing apprentice, who had scurried to the scene. Before the word had finished leaving his mouth, his tiny, inquisitive paw shot out and poked the glistening mass.

## SQUELCH.

The sound was unspeakably intimate. Ditto froze. He tried to pull his paw back. It held fast. He tugged. The... structure... wobbled menacingly. A look of pure, existential betrayal dawned on his fuzzy face. In a panic, he did the only thing his instinct suggested: he licked it.

The effect was instantaneous. His entire body convulsed in a full-body shudder. "BITTER! BITTER! WRONG! SO WRONG!" he gagged, flailing now with both a stuck paw and a profaned tongue.

Sir Whiskerton descended from his perch with the dignified grace of a fireman answering a five-alarm call. He surveyed the scene: Doris hyperventilating into her wing, Porkchop giving an artistic critique, Ditto engaged in a sticky, tragicomic struggle.

"Doris, compose yourself. Your dramatics are adding to the particulate matter," he stated coolly. "Porkchop, your aesthetic commentary is noted and unhelpful. Ditto... cease tasting the evidence."

His detective's mind, a precision instrument of logic, engaged. A secretion of this magnitude indicated a source of significant internal distress. This was no common sniffle. This was a geophysical event of the sinuses.

## III. The Patient Zero and The Great Separation

The culprit was not difficult to trace. A trail of similar, though smaller, glistening deposits led from the barn door like vile breadcrumbs to the pond. There, floating in a haze of misery, was Wilma the Goose.

Her normally proud, serpentine neck was slumped. Her beak hung open, and from it dripped a continuous, clear stream that *plip-plip-plipped* into the water, creating tiny, forlorn ripples. Her eyes were glassy with fever.

"I'b fide," she honked miserably, her voice a waterlogged trumpet. "Jusd a liddle snuffle AAAAH—CHOOOOOO!"

The sneeze was not a sneeze. It was a cataclysm. A hurricane from a head. It erupted with the force of a shotgun blast, sending a shower of pond water, duckweed, and a very surprised frog sailing through the air in a parabolic arc of despair.

Sir Whiskerton did not flinch. He diagnosed the situation with clinical speed. "Wilma is afflicted with a profound rhinovirus. She is a fountain of contagion. Effective immediately: Quarantine Protocol."

The word "quarantine" spread through the farm like its own kind of virus. It bred not illness, but *fear*.

Doris became a zealot of sanitation. She fashioned a hazmat suit from a burlap feed sack, cutting two ragged holes for her wings. She began spraying vinegar (which she believed purified the soul) everywhere, creating a sour, pickled atmosphere. Porkchop, ever the pragmatic capitalist, saw opportunity. He declared the main barn-door specimen "The Glistening Wonder" and began charging the curious field mice and sparrows an acorn apiece for a viewing, providing a hushed, dramatic commentary on its "organic grandeur." The farm splintered. Paths were avoided. Shared troughs were abandoned. Side-eyed glances replaced morning greetings. The subtle, invisible threads of community that The Farmer nurtured and Beatrice anointed with honey were being severed by the specter of the sniffle.

And Wilma? She was exiled. Not to a warm corner of the barn, but to a draughty spot behind the woodpile, a pile of old rags her only comfort. Her honks grew weaker, not just from congestion, but from a loneliness that ached deeper than any fever.

## IV. The Unexpected Alchemists

In the midst of this germaphobic panic, two souls operated on different frequencies.

The Farmer, bless him, observed the chaos with his typical, detached compassion. He saw Wilma shivering and, without a word about germs, brought her his own red-plaid thermos lid filled with warm water infused with thyme from Beatrice's garden. "For the internal climate," he murmured to her, patting her head with his calloused hand before wandering off, forgetting the thermos lid entirely.

And then there was Chef Remy LeRaccoon, the francophone culinary entrepreneur. Where others saw pestilence, he saw... potential. He observed the adhesive quality of Ditto's trapped paw, the way the barn-door specimen defied gravity. His eyes, behind his tiny spectacles, lit with the fire of invention.

"Mais oui!" he gasped, scuttling forth with a miniature notebook. "Ze viscosity! Ze tensile strength! Zis is not sickness, mes amis—zis is nature's mortar!"

Ignoring the collective groan of horror, he procured a tiny, clean jar and, with the dedication of a sommelier at a rare vineyard, began collecting droplets from Wilma's drips (with her confused permission). His laboratory—a repurposed tea tin—soon hummed with activity. His goal: **Snot-Based Glue™**.

## V. The Sympathy That Sneezed

Sir Whiskerton watched the farm fracture. He saw the moral decay—the fear, the ostracization, the profiteering from misfortune—and found it far more virulent than Wilma's cold. A principle was at stake: the duty of care for a member of the flock.

He convened a meeting under the old oak, far from the Glistening Wonder.

"This pathogen has attacked more than Wilma's respiratory system," he intoned, his tail tip flicking like a metronome of disapproval. "It has attacked our sense of community. We are treating a sneeze as a sin. Doris, your vinegar is an assault on the nostrils. Porkchop, your tourism is a monument to poor taste."

Just then, a weak, pathetic honk carried from the woodpile. It was a sound of utter dejection.

It was Ditto, his paw finally unstuck but his spirit still scarred, who spoke the simple truth. "She's... lonely."

The word hung in the air, more potent than any germ.

Beatrice arrived then, carrying a small pot. "It's a poultice," she said softly. "Eucalyptus and lavender. For her chest. And this," she added, placing a perfect honeycomb beside it, "is for her spirit. Sweetness is a powerful medicine."

## VI. The Glue That Truly Binds

A procession formed. Not of the fearful, but of the penitent. Led by Sir Whiskerton and bearing Beatrice's remedies, they approached Wilma's exile. Doris, though still eyeing Wilma's beak with deep suspicion, had removed her feed-sack suit. Porkchop brought a warm mash from his own dinner.

They didn't cure her cold. But they cured her isolation. They sat with her, at a respectful distance, sharing quiet stories as she sipped her thyme water. The shared vulnerability became the real antidote.

And Chef Remy? His experiments reached a climax. With a flourish, he presented two Popsicle sticks, firmly bonded by a clear, odorless dab of his processed, sterilized adhesive. "Voila!" he proclaimed. "Strong enough to hold a dream together! And completely sterile!" To prove it, he licked the spoon. The animals recoiled in unified horror.

"Non, non! It is science!" he insisted, though his own gag reflex betrayed him a moment later.

## VII. The Moral, Blown Clean

Wilma recovered. The Great Barn-Door Specimen was, after much debate, ceremoniously scraped away with a shovel (which Porkchop, unable to let a market die, sold as a "Relic of the Contagion" to a very gullible crow).

But something had shifted. The fear had evaporated, leaving behind a stronger, more conscious kindness. The farm had faced the icky, the gross, the fundamentally other, and chosen compassion over contagion.

The Moral of the Story, dear reader, is this: Life is unavoidably, sometimes disgustingly, messy. We all have our seasons of being a fountain of inconvenience. True community is not a fortress against germs, but a field hospital for the afflicted. It is the willingness to bring a poultice to the sneezy, to share warmth with the contagious, and to remember that the stickiest substance in the world is not found in a goose's beak, but the bonds of sympathy that hold hearts together when everything else is coming unglued.

The End.

## Post-Credit Scene

*(Preserved exactly from the source.)*

A week later, The Farmer is trying to repair a broken picture frame—a faded photograph of smiling people he can't quite name. The store-bought glue fails. With a thoughtful hum, he reaches into his drawer of mysteries and pulls out a tiny, unlabeled jar from Chef Remy, labeled only with a **©**. He applies the clear adhesive. It holds instantly, perfectly. He smiles at the restored frame. "Good enough," he whispers.

And somewhere, a goose sneezes happily into a tissue provided by a former hazmat fanatic, and no one flinches at all.

## Key Jokes

- **"By all that is sacred and sanitary!"** — Doris's opening howl, delivered as if *hygiene* were a *deity* worth swearing by. The joke is that Doris's *theology* is *domestic*. Her highest power is *cleanliness*.
- **"It's a piglet."** — Porkchop's *entirely sincere* assessment of the snot monument, delivered in the register of an *art critic*. The joke is that he treats the abomination as a *newborn creature* — which is *almost* charming, and *deeply* wrong.
- **The SQUELCH.** — Onomatopoeia deployed at exactly the right moment. The joke is *auditory* and *physical*. The reader *feels* the sound in their body before their brain can process what happened. Pure slapstick timing.
- **Ditto licking it.** — The *inevitable* consequence of a kitten's curiosity. The joke is that Ditto, faced with an object he cannot unstick from, responds with the *one action guaranteed to make everything worse*. It is *perfect* Ditto logic. It is *exactly* what a kitten would do.
- **"Cease tasting the evidence."** — Sir Whiskerton's calm instruction, delivered to Ditto mid-lick. The joke is that Whiskerton *does not raise his voice*. He *names the behavior* with all the exasperated precision of a courtroom judge. He is *completely in control*, and everyone else is *completely out of control*, and the gap between them is the comedy.
- **Doris's hazmat feed-sack suit.** — Her response to the crisis, described in *one line* and *fully visualized*. The joke is that she *takes the crisis seriously* with such *total commitment* that the sight of her in the suit is *funnier* than the crisis itself.
- **"The Glistening Wonder."** — Porkchop's *marketing name* for the barn-door specimen, complete with a *charging fee* for viewing. The joke is that Porkchop's *capitalist instincts* are so *unshakeable* that they survive even a *public health emergency*. He monetizes the crisis. He is *entirely* himself.
- **"I'b fide. Jusd a liddle snuffle AAAAH—CHOOOOOO!"** — Wilma's *phonetically rendered* head cold, followed by the *cataclysmic sneeze*. The joke is that her *condition* is rendered *lovingly* in dialogue, then *detonated* for physical comedy. The sneeze is described as "a hurricane from a head." The *scale* of the gag is what lands.
- **"Nature's mortar!"** — Remy's *grand reframing* of snot as a construction material. The joke is that Remy *genuinely believes* what he is saying. He is not being cynical. He is not being greedy. He is *truly inspired*. And the *result* is that his glue actually *works*.
- **"Non, non! It is science!"** — Remy, defending his Snot-Based Glue™ after licking the spoon and gagging. The joke is that he *cannot* stop himself from *demonstrating* his product, and the demonstration *immediately* undermines his pitch. The *sincerity* of his commitment to science is what makes the moment land.
- **The "Relic of the Contagion."** — Porkchop's *post-crisis monetization* of the *scraped-away* snot monument. The joke is that Porkchop has *no shame* about his business model. He sold the pile of snot to a crow. He called it a "relic." The crow bought it.

## Starring

- **Sir Whiskerton** (胡子爵士) — the farm's calm center, whose diagnosis is clinical and whose moral is devastatingly clear: *"We are treating a sneeze as a sin."*
- **Wilma the Goose** (威尔玛鹅) — the *patient zero*, whose cold isolates her from the flock. Her arc is the story's emotional core — the loneliness of being contagious.
- **Doris the Hen** (八卦母鸡) — the farm's *Chief of Sanitation*, whose response to crisis is a hazmat feed-sack suit and vinegar spray. The comedy and the heart of the story both run through her.
- **Porkchop the Pig** (猪大排) — the farm's *pragmatic capitalist*, who monetizes the crisis (as "The Glistening Wonder") and then *monetizes the aftermath* (as "The Relic of the Contagion"). His *unshakeable* commercial instincts make him one of the story's funniest characters.
- **Ditto the Kitten** (复读猫) — the *chaos engine* whose reactions — the poke, the lick, the stuck paw — are the story's *slapstick heart*. But his *simple truth* ("She's... lonely") is also the story's *moral pivot*.
- **Chef Remy LeRaccoon** (疯狂厨师) — the *culinary alchemist* who sees *artistic potential* in the snot and invents *Snot-Based Glue™*. His *enthusiasm* is the story's *purest comedy*, and his *product* — as we learn in the post-credit scene — *actually works*.
- **Beatrice** (笨笨蜜姐) — the *heart-nurse* who brings Wilma a *poultice* and a *honeycomb* — "Sweetness is a powerful medicine." Her *softness* is the story's *counterweight* to the panic.
- **The Farmer** (农场主) — the *silent* presence who brings Wilma his *thermos lid* full of *thyme water* and pats her head *without a word about germs*. His *gesture* is the story's *quietest* act of compassion.
- **Bessie the Cow** (彩虹牛) — the *hippie philosopher* whose *reframing* of the crisis (implied in the background) adds to the farm's *emotional weather*.

## P.S.

The farm does not have *unclean* things. It has *uncomfortable* things. And the difference between the two is *the story we tell about them*.

A sneeze is a sneeze. A drip is a drip. A barn-door specimen — well, *that* is a barn-door specimen. But it is *not a sin*. It is *a symptom*. And symptoms, on this farm, are *not punished*. They are *tended*.

Wilma recovered. The Glistening Wonder was scraped away. Porkchop sold the shovel. Remy's glue *actually worked* — the Farmer is still using it. And Doris, who spent the whole crisis in a feed-sack hazmat suit, *happily* provided the tissue for Wilma's first *post-recovery* sneeze.

That is what the farm does. It takes the *disgusting* and *holds it gently* until it passes.

There is no clean place here. There is only a *warm* one.
