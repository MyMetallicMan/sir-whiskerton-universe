# The Illustrated Storybooks

*Twenty-to-thirty hand-built EPUBs. Each one, a labor of love.*

## About This Folder

This folder contains the illustrated EPUB editions of the Sir Whiskerton stories — each one constructed by hand, page by page, code by code, illustration by illustration.

These are not auto-generated files. They are not PDFs run through a converter. They are not templates filled in and exported.

**Each EPUB in this folder represents hours of manual work.**

Every chapter was formatted by hand. Every illustration was placed by hand. Every piece of metadata — the title pages, the copyright notices, the tables of contents, the bilingual character introductions, the ESL teaching guides, the curriculum standard alignments — was entered by hand, checked by hand, and refined by hand.

They are, in a small way, artifacts themselves.

---

## What's Inside Each EPUB

Each book is a complete, self-contained reading and teaching experience. It includes:

- **The story itself** — in Sir Whiskerton's signature voice, with the farm's full cast of characters
- **Bilingual character introductions** — English and Chinese, side by side
- **Illustrations** — original artwork created for each story
- **The ESL Teaching Guide** — vocabulary, grammar focus, discussion questions, and classroom activities
- **The 2022 Chinese English Curriculum Standards Alignment** — a table mapping each story to the specific curriculum codes, grade levels, and core competencies it addresses
- **Post-story materials** — moral, best lines, key jokes, and a post-credit scene
- **The "Jingle On" benediction** — because every story should end the way the farm ends

The result is a book that a child can enjoy, a parent can share, and a teacher can use in a real classroom.

## The Process

I want to be honest about how these were made, because that honesty matters.

### The Code

Each EPUB is hand-coded in HTML and CSS, structured according to the EPUB 3 specification, and packaged manually. This is not a "click export" process. It involves:

- Writing each chapter as clean, semantic HTML
- Building the table of contents by hand
- Writing the metadata files (`.opf`, `.ncx`) from scratch
- Testing the reading experience in multiple EPUB readers
- Fixing the small things that always break — the paragraph spacing, the chapter breaks, the illustration placement, the font fallbacks

It takes time. Sometimes a lot of it. Sometimes a single chapter takes an evening.

### The Illustrations

The artwork was generated by me, using AI image-generation tools, and curated page by page. Each illustration was:

- Prompted and re-prompted until it fit the moment in the story
- Cropped, adjusted, and placed in the layout by hand
- Paired with its scene so that the visual and the text were in conversation

The icons — used throughout the teaching guides, character introductions, and section dividers — were also generated and placed by hand.

### The Standards Alignment

Every story is aligned with the **2022 Chinese Compulsory Education English Curriculum Standards (义务教育英语课程标准)**.

This is not decorative. Each book identifies:

- The specific curriculum codes it addresses (e.g., 3.4.1, 5.3.3, 7.1.4)
- The core competency it develops (linguistic ability, cultural awareness, critical thinking, learning ability)
- The grammar focus (present simple, present continuous, modal verbs, comparatives, etc.)
- The vocabulary tier (foundational, extended, advanced)
- The theme or unit (exploration, creativity, respect, problem-solving)
- The activity type (role-play, debate, creative writing, etc.)
- The assessment focus (sentence writing, ongoing action description, persuasive statements, etc.)

A teacher can pick up any one of these books and know, at a glance, how it fits into their classroom.

## A Note on the Standards

The 2022 Chinese English Curriculum Standards are rigorous, thoughtful, and comprehensive. They ask students to do more than memorize vocabulary — they ask them to *use* language, *think* critically, and *understand* culture.

The Sir Whiskerton stories were built to serve those standards without ever *feeling* like a curriculum. The children reading them are not being taught grammar. They are being invited to laugh with a cat, cry with a scarecrow, and solve mysteries alongside a whole farm of unlikely friends. The grammar is simply *what the story sounds like*.

That is the goal.

The standards are the skeleton. The story is the body. And this folder is where the two are joined.
