# Artifact Bio: The Eucalyptus Grove

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** The Eucalyptus Grove (also known as "The Gift Grove," "The Road That Was Planted," or, in Sir Whiskerton's private notes, "The Grove We Planted For Someone We Will Never Meet")

**Type:** Living Landmark / Planted Grove / Gift

**Location:** A small rise just beyond Old Mable, where the soil is rich and the light is good and the wind carries the scent of the spring

**Status:** Living. Young. Growing slowly.

**First Documented:** Planted by the farm after the arrival of a letter from a faraway friend. *(See [TBF02 — The Eucalyptus Road](../../../stories/tales_by_firefly/TBF02_the_eucalyptus_road.md).)*

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

The grove is small. It is smaller than you would expect, if you came looking for a grove. There are perhaps a dozen trees, none of them taller than a person, their leaves silver-green and soft, their bark pale and smooth and cool to the touch.

They were planted by the farm in a single afternoon — the Farmer choosing the spot, Bessie choosing the trees, Porkchop digging the holes, the hens donating feathers for mulch, the geese supervising (unhelpfully), Doris reading the letter aloud so that the trees would know why they were being planted.

They are not yet tall enough to shade anyone. They are not yet old enough to scent the air. They might not survive the winter. The farm knows this. The farm planted them anyway.

Because that is the point.

The grove is a *promise*. It is a gift for people the farm will never meet. It is a road, planted in a circle, waiting for the day it becomes a place.

---

## THE LETTER THAT STARTED IT

The grove exists because of a letter. The letter arrived one Tuesday, carried by a sparrow who had flown a very long way. It told the story of a grandmother from Albania, in a town called Fier, who was asked what gift she would like from the man who wanted to marry her. She did not ask for jewelry, or money, or land.

She said: *"I wish the road from Fier to Vlora were lined with eucalyptus trees."*

He did it. He arranged for the trees to be planted. More than a century later, some of them are still standing.

The letter ended with a request: *"Plant something. For someone you will never meet."*

The farm did.

The letter is now kept in the barn, near Sir Whiskerton's sleeping spot. It is read aloud on the anniversary of its arrival. *(See [The Unsent Letters](the_unsent_letters.md) for the Farmer's letters to Li Hua — the Chinese pen-pal whose own letter inspired this one.)*

---

## THE TREES

The eucalyptus trees are small but stubborn. They have survived one winter already, and the farm is hopeful they will survive another.

Each tree is different. Each tree has been *noticed* by a different animal on the farm. The hens favor the two nearest the fence. The pigs favor the one with the deepest shade. Ferdinand the Duck favors the tallest one, which he claims is "a natural stage." Sir Whiskerton favors the one closest to the path from Old Mable — not because it is the best tree, but because it is the *right* tree to sit beside when he is thinking.

No one has ever harvested from the grove. No one has ever pruned it. No one has ever done anything except *visit* it — and, occasionally, leave small offerings at the base of a tree: a feather, a stone, a fallen leaf from another part of the farm.

The grove accepts these offerings. The grove does not require them.

---

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the grove report a strange sensation — not of being watched, exactly, but of being *anticipated*. As if the grove is waiting for someone. As if the trees know they were planted for a purpose, and they are patient, and they are willing.

The grove is quiet. It is not silent — the wind moves through the young leaves, and there is a soft rustle that sounds almost like conversation. But it is *quiet* in a way that feels deliberate. Like a held breath. Like a beginning.

An hour spent in the grove feels like a moment. A moment feels like a lifetime. It is easy to lose track of time here.

---

## THE MYSTERY

**Who will rest beneath these trees?** No one knows. The grove was planted for someone the farm will never meet — but who, and when, and why, no one can say.

**Will the grove survive?** No one knows. The trees are young. They may not make it through the winter. They may make it through a hundred winters.

**What does the grove *mean*?** No one knows. Everyone knows. The grove means *hope*. The grove means *continuity*. The grove means *the world will keep going, and we are part of it, and that is enough.*

---

## THE INVITATION

The grove is open to anyone. It does not ask for anything. It does not demand attention.

You are welcome to sit among the young trees. You are welcome to touch the smooth bark. You are welcome to leave a small gift — a leaf, a stone, a note — at the base of any tree.

You are welcome to add your own tree, if you must. But the grove does not require it. It already knows you were here.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

The eucalyptus grove is a **future**. It is the farm's promise that the world will continue, that the story will not end, that there will be something left behind for the people who come after.

It appears in stories as:

- **A place of hope** — characters visit it when they need to remember that the future is worth planting for
- **A place of patience** — the trees are young. The grove is a *waiting*. This is the point.
- **A place of connection** — the grove connects the farm to Fier, to Vlora, to Albania, to Li Hua, to a hundred years of trees that no one on the farm has ever seen

In the Commonplace Books, the grove might appear as:

- A small map of the rise beyond Old Mable, with the young trees marked in silver ink
- A pressed eucalyptus leaf, silver-green and soft, kept between pages
- A note from the Farmer: *"Planted for someone I will never meet. Good enough, Dad."*
- A letter from Li Hua, transcribed in careful English: *"I am looking forward to your reply."*
- **A small sketch of the grove at dusk**, drawn by Sir Whiskerton, with a single note: *"It will grow. It has to."*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

The grove is small. It is young. It may not survive.

But it was planted. And that is enough.

Come sit among the young trees. The bark is smooth. The leaves are silver. The future is coming.

You are exactly where you need to be.
