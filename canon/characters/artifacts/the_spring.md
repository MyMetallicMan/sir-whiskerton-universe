# Artifact Bio: The Spring

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** The Spring (also known as "The Cold Spring" or, less reverently, "The Farmer's Favorite Puddle")

**Type:** Natural Water Source / Cold Spring / Quiet Landmark

**Location:** In the far northeast corner of the pasture, where the land rises slightly before it falls away toward the birch wood

**Status:** Always flowing. Always cold. Always quiet.

**First Documented:** It predates the farm. It may predate the pasture. It is older than anyone can remember.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

The Spring is small.

This is the first thing you notice about it, and the first thing you forget. It does not announce itself. It does not roar. It does not steam or sparkle or glow. It simply *is* — a small, clear pool of water, perhaps four feet across, welling up from beneath a cluster of flat rocks at the base of a slight rise in the land.

The water is cold. Genuinely cold, even in the height of summer — not ice-cold, but *cold*, the kind of cold that makes you catch your breath and then feel perfectly, wonderfully awake. The temperature does not change with the seasons. It is the same in July as it is in January.

The pool is shallow — perhaps eight inches at its deepest — and its bottom is visible through the water: pale sand, small stones, a single dark stone that has been at the center of the pool for as long as anyone can remember. The surface of the pool is always *moving*, though there is no obvious current — a slow, patient welling, water rising and spilling over the edges of the pool and trickling away into the pasture grass.

The water tastes faintly of moss. Of stone. Of something else that no one has ever quite named — something clean and green and *old*, like the first breath of air after a long rain.

The Spring does not have a fountain. It does not have a sculpture. It does not have a monument. It does not have a bucket or a dipper or a wooden bench. It has only itself.

And it is enough.

---

## THE WATER

The water of the Spring is not ordinary water.

It is *clean*. Not purified, not filtered, not treated — just *clean*, in a way that water rarely is. The animals drink from it freely. No one has ever gotten sick from it. No one has ever needed to.

It is *cool*. It stays cool through the height of summer, when every other water source on the farm has gone warm and flat. On the hottest day of August, the Spring is still cold, still clear, still *itself*.

It is *quiet*. This is harder to explain. The Spring does not make noise. The trickle of water over the pool's edges is barely audible. But there is a *quality* to the water — a stillness that seems to come from it, a hush that settles on the grass around it. When you drink from the Spring, you feel quiet. When you leave the Spring, you feel that you have been somewhere else.

The Farmer has never explained the Spring. He has never needed to. He drinks from it every morning. He has done so for as long as anyone can remember.

---

## THE HISTORY

The Spring is older than the farm.

This is not a matter of speculation. It is a matter of *record* — or rather, of *memory*, which is the only record this Spring has ever needed. The Farmer's grandfather drank from the Spring. His grandfather drank from it before him. The Spring was here before the pasture was cleared. It was here before the fence was built. It was here before the birch wood took root, and it was here before the woods were cleared to make the fields.

It is older than the farm. It is older than the road. It is older, probably, than anyone who has ever walked this land.

And it has never stopped flowing.

This is the Spring's great *unspoken* quality. It is not a landmark in the sense that Old Mable is a landmark, or the Springhouse is a landmark. It is not dramatic. It is not storied. It is not mysterious in the way that the Long Road is mysterious. It is simply *here*, and it has always been here, and it will be here long after everyone who currently drinks from it has gone.

The animals treat it this way. They do not worship it. They do not make pilgrimages to it. They simply *drink* from it, when they are thirsty, and then they move on, and they do not think about it again until they are thirsty again.

This is exactly what the Spring wants.

---

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the Spring report:

- **A sense of *arrival*.** Not of being somewhere new, but of being somewhere *familiar*. As if the Spring has been waiting for you, and is pleased that you found it.
- **A cooling of the mind.** The water is cold. The feeling is colder, in a good way. Something in you settles when you drink from the Spring. Something in you quiets.
- **A slight reluctance to leave.** You drink. You sit for a moment on the flat rocks. You notice that the grass around the Spring is greener than the grass elsewhere. You notice that the air is slightly cooler. You notice that you do not want to leave — not because the Spring is holding you, but because *something in you* does not want to go.
- **A recurring dream, reported by several animals independently:** *You are drinking from a small pool of water. It is the middle of the night. There is no moon. The water is cold. You are not afraid. You wake up and you are not thirsty anymore.*

---

## THE LORE (Minor)

- It is said that the Spring's water *does not spoil* — that if you carry it away in a jar or a canteen, it stays fresh longer than water should. No one has tested this rigorously. Everyone knows someone who has.
- It is said that the Spring's water *does not freeze* — that even in the coldest winter, the surface of the pool remains liquid, though everything around it is ice. No one has ever seen the Spring frozen. This is considered *interesting* but not *urgent*.
- It is said that the Spring is connected — through the underground water table — to the spring inside the Springhouse, and to the pond where the Yodeling Fish live, and to the creek at the edge of the Long Road. No one has ever proven this. No one has ever needed to.
- It is said that the Spring's water, if carried to Old Mable and poured on her roots, causes the tree to grow a single new leaf — even in winter. The Farmer's grandfather believed this. The Farmer has never commented on it. But Old Mable has never lost all her leaves, even in the deepest cold, and no one has ever fully explained why.

---

## THE MYSTERY

**Where does the Spring come from?** No one knows. The water wells up from beneath a cluster of flat rocks, but the source is not visible, and the rocks have never been moved. Digging would disturb the Spring. Digging would be rude. No one has ever tried.

**Why is the Spring always cold?** No one knows. It is not deep enough to be truly insulated by the earth. It is not shaded enough to stay cool by shelter alone. It is simply cold, and it has always been cold, and no one has ever needed to explain it.

**Why does the Spring exist?** No one knows. It is not necessary. The farm has other water sources. The Spring is not required. It is simply *here*.

And yet — the animals drink from it. The Farmer drinks from it. The Spring has never run dry, not once, not for a single day. There is something in that. There is something in a small, cold, quiet pool of water that has never stopped giving, whether or not anyone was thirsty.

---

## THE INVITATION

The Spring does not ask for anything. It does not demand worship. It does not demand attention. It simply *is* — and it is open to anyone who finds it.

You are welcome to drink. You are welcome to sit on the flat rocks beside the pool. You are welcome to dip your paw, your hoof, your hand into the water and feel the cold. You are welcome to look at the dark stone at the bottom of the pool and wonder what it is, and to leave without ever knowing.

You are *not* welcome to move the flat rocks. You are *not* welcome to dig. You are *not* welcome to try to trace the source of the water, or to build a structure around the Spring, or to *improve* it in any way.

The Spring has been here longer than you. It will be here after you. Let it be.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

The Spring is a **quiet gift**. It is the farm's reminder that some of the most important things are the ones that ask for nothing, that do not need to be noticed, that simply *continue* — day after day, season after season, year after year — whether or not anyone is paying attention.

It appears in stories as:

- **A place of simple necessity** — characters drink from it when they are thirsty, and nothing more
- **A place of small moments** — a character sits beside the Spring and thinks, and the thinking does not need to be shared
- **A place of continuity** — the Spring has been here longer than anyone, and it will be here after everyone. It is a reminder that some things simply *continue*.

In the Commonplace Books, the Spring might appear as:

- A small, watercolor illustration of the pool and the flat rocks, with a single bird drinking at the edge
- A pressed blade of grass from the Spring's edge, greener than the grass elsewhere, labeled: *"From the Spring. It was cold."*
- A hand-drawn diagram, in Porkchop's wobbly hand, showing the Spring connected (by dotted lines) to the Springhouse, the pond, and the creek — with a note: *"Probably. Maybe. Who knows."*
- A single jar of Spring water, kept in the barn, still cold after a week, still clear, still fresh — with a small card: *"Do not open."*
- A page of notes, in the Farmer's handwriting, listing the dates and times he has drunk from the Spring. There are hundreds of entries. The earliest is decades old. The most recent is today.

---

## THE FINAL WORD

The Spring is small. It is cold. It is quiet.

It has been here for a very long time. It will be here for a very long time more.

It does not ask for anything. It does not need to be noticed. It simply *continues* — the way that water continues, the way that life continues, the way that the small, necessary things of the world always continue, whether or not anyone is watching.

Come drink. Sit for a moment. Notice that the grass is greener here. Notice that the air is cooler here. Notice that you are not thirsty anymore.

Then leave. The Spring will not call you back. It will simply be here, when you return.

And you will return. Everyone returns.
