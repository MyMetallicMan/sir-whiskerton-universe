# Artifact Bio: The Farmer's Single Boot

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** The Farmer's Single Boot (also known, in some circles, as **The Holy Shoe**)

**Type:** Lost Object / Running Gag / Sacred Relic

**Location:** Unknown. Currently missing. It has been found in the henhouse, submerged in the horse trough, serving as an unlikely planter for a determined squash vine, and — on one memorable occasion — being carried up the eucalyptus grove by a squirrel.

**Status:** Active. Missing. Worshipped.

**First Documented:** As long as anyone has known the Farmer, one of his boots has been missing. It is never the same boot twice.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

The Farmer's boots are, individually, unremarkable. They are sturdy leather work boots, brown with age, cracked at the seams, held together by laces that have been replaced so many times that they are more patch than string.

The Farmer has never owned more than one pair at a time. He has rarely worn both boots at once.

The missing boot is always *the other one* — whichever one is not currently on the Farmer's foot. It is the farm's most consistent mystery. It is also the farm's most beloved running gag.

The boot, when found, is always in a location that makes no sense. It has been discovered:

- In the henhouse, resting in a nesting box, as if it were an egg
- Submerged in the horse trough, upright and dignified
- Serving as a planter for a squash vine that has since flourished
- Being carried up Old Mable by a squirrel who refused to explain
- Resting on the platform of the tree house, as if someone had placed it there for safekeeping
- Floating in the pond, gently, as if it were a very small boat

The boot is never where it should be. The Farmer has never complained.

---

## THE HISTORY

The Farmer has been losing his boots for as long as anyone can remember. The tradition began, according to the animals, on a Tuesday. No one remembers which Tuesday.

At some point, the animals began to *notice* the boot. They began to *comment* on it. They began to *predict* where it would appear.

And then, one day — no one remembers exactly when — a small group of the farm's more impressionable creatures began to *worship* it.

The **Cult of the Holy Shoe** is not a formal organization. It has no leader. It has no doctrine. It simply *is* — a small group of chipmunks, a pair of very serious hens, and (on at least one documented occasion) a hedgehog who believed the boot had healing properties.

The boot is not, as far as anyone can tell, actually holy. It is just a boot. But it has been *treated* as holy for so long that it may have begun to absorb some of the properties of a relic.

This is not confirmed. This is simply *understood*.

---

## THE FEELING

The boot, when found, produces a strange mixture of emotions in the animals:

- **Delight.** The game continues. The mystery deepens.
- **Reverence.** For the members of the Cult of the Holy Shoe, the boot is not just a boot. It is a *sign*.
- **Relief.** The Farmer will be able to wear two boots again. At least for a few hours.

The Farmer, for his part, expresses no emotion at all when the boot is returned to him. He simply nods. He puts it on. He loses it again within the week.

The boot is the farm's most persistent joke. It is also, somehow, one of its most sacred.

---

## THE MYSTERY

Where does the boot go? How does it move? Is it moving at all, or is the Farmer simply losing it in increasingly improbable places?

No one knows. The Farmer does not ask. The boot does not say.

The Cult of the Holy Shoe believes the boot travels *on its own*, seeking out places of significance. The more rational animals believe the Farmer simply forgets where he leaves it, and the boot is *moved* — by squirrels, by hens, by the wind — to locations that the Farmer would never think to look.

Both theories are probably true. Neither theory has been proven.

---

## THE INVITATION

The boot is not for you. Do not take it. Do not worship it. Do not move it to a new location, unless you are a squirrel and you have a *reason*.

But you may *observe* the boot. You may note its location. You may comment on it to other animals.

You may also — on rare occasions — find the boot in a place where you did not expect it. If this happens, do not panic. Simply return the boot to the Farmer. He will nod. He will put it on. He will lose it again.

The game will continue. The game always continues.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

The Farmer's Single Boot is a *running gag*. It is also, in its own strange way, an *artifact* — a small, sacred thing that the farm has decided to hold with tenderness and humor in equal measure.

It appears in stories as a recurring motif — a lost boot, a search, a discovery, a small moment of mystery that no one tries to solve.

In the Commonplace Books, the boot might appear as:

- A pencil sketch of the boot, drawn from life, with a note: *"Currently in the horse trough. Again."*
- A pressed squash leaf, saved from the vine that once grew inside the boot, with a note: *"It was a good planter."*
- A page from the Cult of the Holy Shoe's unofficial doctrine, transcribed by Doris the Hen: *"The boot is not a boot. The boot is a sign."*
- A photograph of the boot, in the henhouse, in a nesting box, as if it were an egg

---

## THE FINAL WORD

The boot is lost. It will be found. It will be lost again.

The Farmer will not complain. The animals will not stop looking. The Cult of the Holy Shoe will not stop worshipping.

The boot is a joke. The boot is a relic. The boot is, in its own way, the farm's most honest artifact — a thing that does not need to be understood to be loved.

Come look for the boot. Do not find it. Do not tell anyone where it is.

Let the game continue. Let the game always continue.
