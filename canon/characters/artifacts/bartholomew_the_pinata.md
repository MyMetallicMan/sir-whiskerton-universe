# Artifact Bio: Bartholomew the Piñata

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** Bartholomew

**Type:** Fellow Vessel / Occasional Speaker / Held Soul

**Location:** Hanging mournfully in the barn, near the tool rack

**Status:** Silent. Watching. Occasionally speaking, though no one can explain how.

**First Documented:** The Farmer has addressed Bartholomew by name for years. No one remembers when the piñata arrived.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Bartholomew is a piñata. He is shaped like a burro — a small, colorful donkey, made of papier-mâché and painted in bright, fading colors. His ears are slightly bent. His tail is missing. His paint is chipped in several places, revealing the gray of the papier-mâché beneath.

He hangs from a hook in the barn, near the tool rack. He has hung there for as long as anyone can remember. He has never been filled. He has never been swung at. He has never been broken.

He contains forgotten candy and mystery. The Farmer has said so, on more than one occasion. No one has ever asked how he knows.

---

## THE HISTORY

Bartholomew was, at some point, a real piñata. He was made to be filled with candy and swung at and broken open at a party. No one knows what party. No one knows when.

But at some point, he was not broken. At some point, he was saved. At some point, someone hung him in the barn and decided not to swing.

The Farmer talks to Bartholomew as he talks to Jeremiah — as a fellow vessel, as a confidant, as someone who understands. On one occasion, years ago, the Farmer was overheard saying:

> "They forget you're hollow, old friend. They just see the bright colors and want to take a swing. I understand."

Bartholomew did not respond. He did not need to. The Farmer did not require a response.

And yet — on rare occasions — Bartholomew *has* spoken. A low, resonant voice. A single sentence. A piece of wisdom that no one could explain. When asked about this, the animals shrug. They do not know how a piñata speaks. They do not need to.

---

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the barn report a strange sense of *being watched over* by the piñata. It is not frightening. It is the feeling of a kind presence — a fellow being who understands what it is like to be seen only for the surface, and to be loved anyway.

The barn is quieter near Bartholomew. The wind does not stir him. He simply *hangs*.

An hour in the barn feels like a moment. A moment feels like an hour. Bartholomew does not shift.

---

## THE MYSTERY

How does a piñata speak? Why has it never been filled? What is the candy inside it, and who put it there?

No one knows. The Farmer does not ask. The animals do not ask.

Bartholomew hangs. That is all. Bartholomew hangs.

---

## THE INVITATION

The barn is open to anyone. Bartholomew is open to no one — or rather, he is open to *everyone*, in the way that a hanging piñata is open. He does not choose his listeners. He simply listens.

You are welcome to stand near him. You are welcome to speak to him. You are welcome to touch his faded paint, gently, without swinging.

He will not answer — usually. But he will *hang*. He will *be*. He will *hold* whatever is held inside him.

That is enough.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

Bartholomew the Piñata is a *fellow vessel*. He is the farm's reminder that some of the most important things are *hollow*, that being empty is not the same as being useless, that being seen only for your surface is not the same as being unseen.

He appears in stories as a quiet presence — a voice from the barn, a stillness near the tool rack, a reminder that even the hollow things have something to hold.

In the Commonplace Books, Bartholomew might appear as:

- A pencil sketch of the piñata, drawn with care, the paint chips marked in careful detail
- A single piece of candy, wrapped and pressed between pages, with a note: *"Found near Bartholomew. No one knows how."*
- A photograph of the barn at dusk, Bartholomew hanging in the shadows, his colors just visible
- A page of the Farmer's notes: *"They forget you're hollow, old friend."*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

Bartholomew hangs. He has hung for years. He will hang for years more.

He is hollow. He is bright. He is patient.

Come stand near him. Speak if you like. Listen if you like.

He will not swing. He will not break. He will simply *be*.

That is what he does. That is what he *is*.
