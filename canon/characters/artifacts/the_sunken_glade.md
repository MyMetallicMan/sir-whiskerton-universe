# Artifact Bio: The Sunken Glade

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** The Sunken Glade (also known as "The Quiet Pocket" or "The Rabbits' Parliament")

**Type:** Forest Clearing / Natural Sanctuary / Place of Light

**Location:** North end of the farm, where the birch wood dips into a shallow hollow

**Status:** Undisturbed, peaceful, intentionally hidden

**First Documented:** Known to the farm since its earliest days

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

The Sunken Glade is not found — it is *discovered*.

You walk through the birch wood, past trees so slender and white they seem to glow even on overcast days, and then the ground gently drops away, and you are somewhere else.

The glade is a shallow bowl in the earth, perhaps fifty paces across, cupped by the land as if the world itself wanted to hold this place safe. The birches stand at the rim like sentinels, their white trunks catching whatever light exists. On cloudy days, when the rest of the world is gray, the birches seem to generate light, reflecting some hidden brightness onto the glade below.

At the glade's center, granite boulders emerge from the earth like the backs of ancient, sleeping creatures. They are rounded by time, softened by lichen, warm to the touch even when the air is cool. Moss grows in their crevices — not the thick carpet of Old Mable's spring, but something finer, more delicate, like green velvet laid over stone.

Between the boulders, wild grasses grow in clumps and tufts, bending in the wind that rarely reaches this hollow. Blueberry bushes cluster near the edges, their leaves turning crimson in autumn, their berries appearing in summer — smaller than cultivated ones, but sweeter, tasting of sun and secret.

The floor of the glade is soft with years of fallen leaves, pine needles, and the gentle decay that feeds new growth. It smells of earth and birch and something else — something clean and open and quiet.

---

## THE LIGHT

This is the glade's secret: **on cloudy days, when the rest of the world is dim, the Sunken Glade is bright.**

No one knows why. Perhaps it is the birches reflecting what little light exists. Perhaps it is the granite, releasing stored warmth. Perhaps it is something else, something the rabbits understand but do not explain.

Whatever the reason, the glade is a place of sunshine on cloudy days. When the farm is gray and damp and the animals are low, those who know the way walk north, through the birches, down into the hollow, and find light waiting for them.

It is not strong sunlight — not the kind that warms Sir Whiskerton's porch. It is softer, gentler, more like a promise than a presence. But it is enough. On the gravest days, the glade glows.

---

## THE INHABITANTS

The Sunken Glade is home to the **isolationist rabbits**. They are not unfriendly — they are simply private. They have lived here for generations, long before the farm existed, and they have their own ways, their own customs, their own quiet society.

### The Rabbit Families

Two families, possibly three, live in warrens beneath the granite boulders. Their entrances are hidden with artful care — a tuft of grass pulled slightly aside, a stone that looks permanent but moves for those who know the secret.

The rabbits are:

- Silver-gray, the color of rain on stone
- Quick to vanish, slow to emerge
- Watchful, always watchful, with eyes that miss nothing
- Courteous to visitors who respect their ways

They do not speak much, at least not in words that humans or cats can understand. But they communicate. A certain flick of the ear. A particular hop. The way they sit facing the same direction at certain times of day.

**Doris once attempted to "organize" them.** She lasted one afternoon. The rabbits simply... waited. They did not engage. They did not resist. They just waited, with infinite patience, until she left. They were perfectly polite about it. They simply did not participate.

### The Rabbits' Parliament

Occasionally — rarely — the rabbits gather on the largest granite boulder. They sit in a rough circle, facing inward, ears forward, completely still. This is called (by those few who have witnessed it) **the Rabbits' Parliament**.

No one knows what they discuss. The bluejays claim it is territorial disputes. The woodpeckers insist it is philosophical. Porkchop, who once stumbled upon a session, reported: *"They were just... sitting. Looking at each other. It felt important. I left quietly."*

The Parliament meets at dawn on the first day of each month, and on certain other occasions — after a storm, before a hard winter, when something has changed in the wood.

---

## THE VISITORS

The glade is not secret, exactly. But it is private. Those who come here understand, without being told, that they are *guests*.

**Sir Whiskerton** visits occasionally, always alone. He sits on a low boulder, says nothing, watches the light. The rabbits have learned to ignore him. He is, they seem to have decided, *acceptable*.

**Ditto** tried to visit once. He bounced. He chirped. He chased a butterfly. The rabbits vanished. The glade felt suddenly empty, even though nothing had changed. He has not been invited back.

**Bessie** comes sometimes, on cloudy days when she needs light. She stands at the edge of the glade, not entering, just receiving. The rabbits watch her from their hidden entrances. They seem to approve.

**Poo Poo** once attempted to roll down into the glade. He got stuck on a birch root. A rabbit emerged, regarded him for a long moment, and then — with what might have been a sigh — helped push him free. The rabbit said nothing. It simply returned to its warren. Poo Poo has not returned, but he speaks of the encounter with reverence.

The **bluejays and woodpeckers** who nest at the wood's edge serve as announcers. They call out when someone approaches — not in alarm, but in notification. Their calls say: *Someone is coming. It is [name]. They seem peaceful. Proceed accordingly.*

---

## THE BLUEBERRIES

In late summer, the blueberry bushes produce their fruit. They are small, dusty blue, sweeter than any blueberry has a right to be. The rabbits harvest them systematically, working through the bushes in an order that seems predetermined but is never explained.

Some berries are *left for visitors*. This is traditional. If you come to the glade in blueberry season and sit quietly, a rabbit may emerge, approach a bush, pick a berry, and place it on a rock near you. They will not make eye contact. They will not wait for thanks. They will simply return to their work.

Eating a glade blueberry is a **commitment**. You are accepting their gift. You are agreeing, silently, to respect their ways. No one has ever violated this agreement. The consequences are not stated, but they are understood.

---

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the Sunken Glade report:

- A sense of being *watched*, but not *judged*. The watching is simply... noticing. The rabbits notice you. The trees notice you. The light itself seems to notice you.
- A feeling that time runs slower here. An hour in the glade feels like fifteen minutes outside it.
- A strange clarity after leaving. Problems that seemed tangled become simple. Worries that seemed heavy become light.
- On cloudy days, the light is enough. You do not need the sun. The glade provides.

---

## THE LORE (Minor)

- It is said that the Rabbits' Parliament can predict the weather for the entire season. They have never been wrong.
- A single white birch at the glade's northern edge is called the **"Listener."** If you press your ear to its bark, you can hear the rabbits' deliberations — faintly, like distant conversation.
- The blueberries from the glade, if saved and eaten on the winter solstice, will remind you of summer. This is considered a form of magic.
- No one has ever seen a rabbit leave the glade. They are born here, live here, and die here, somewhere in the hidden tunnels beneath the granite. The glade is their whole world. They seem to find it sufficient.

---

## THE MYSTERY

**The rabbits have no names.** At least, none that anyone outside the glade has ever learned.

They are referred to, when they must be referred to at all, by descriptions: *"the one with the torn ear,"* *"the small one who watches from the leftmost boulder,"* *"the elder who moves like smoke."*

They do not seem to mind. They do not seem to need names. They know who they are. That is enough.

**How long have they lived here?** No one knows. The Farmer's grandfather remembered them. His grandfather remembered them. They were here before the farm, before the wood, before anything. They will be here after.

---

## THE INVITATION

You are welcome in the Sunken Glade. But you must come on its terms:

- Move slowly. Move quietly. Move with intention.
- Do not approach the rabbits. Let them approach you, if they choose.
- Do not take anything except the berries they leave for you. Even then, eat them here. They do not travel well.
- If you sit long enough, the light will find you. Even on cloudy days. *Especially* on cloudy days.

The rabbits may not acknowledge you. They may not even appear. But they *know* you are there. They have noted your presence. They have filed you in their silent, collective memory.

If you come again, they will remember. They may not show it. But they will remember.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

The Sunken Glade appears in stories as:

- **A place of retreat** — characters come here when they need to think, to grieve, to make difficult decisions
- **A place of witness** — the rabbits see things and remember them, though they never speak of them
- **A place of light** — on the gravest days, when hope seems thin, the glade offers something

In the Commonplace Books, the glade might appear as:

- A double-page spread showing the glade in summer, with the birches glowing and the rabbits barely visible at their entrances
- A small, pressed blueberry leaf in a pocket, with a note: *"From the glade. Eat nothing. Remember everything."*
- A diagram of the Rabbits' Parliament seating arrangement (speculative, annotated by Porkchop)
- A page of "rabbit observations" in Doris's handwriting, mostly crossed out, with a final note: *"They do not wish to be organized. I respect this. I think."*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

The Sunken Glade is not for everyone. It is for those who need it.

And if you need it, you will find it. The birches will part. The light will find you. The rabbits will watch, and remember, and perhaps — if you are very still — one of them will place a berry on a rock near you.

Do not thank them. They do not want thanks. They want only that you sit, and receive, and leave as quietly as you came.

The glade will be here when you return. It is always here.

Waiting. Glowing. Holding its light for cloudy days.
