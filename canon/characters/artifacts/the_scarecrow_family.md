# Artifact Bio: The Scarecrow Family

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** The Scarecrow Family (collective); individually, the East Field Scarecrow, the West Field Scarecrow, the North Field Scarecrow, the South Field Scarecrow

**Type:** Standing Guard / Silent Witnesses / Family of Straw

**Location:** One in each of the farm's four fields — East, West, North, and South — plus several abandoned ones scattered across the property

**Status:** Standing. Watching. Occasionally talked to. Occasionally falling over.

**First Documented:** The Farmer's grandfather kept the family. The current Farmer inherited it. No one remembers who built the first one.

---

## ABOUT THE FAMILY

There are four working scarecrows on the farm — one for each of the four fields. They are not identical. They are not interchangeable. They are not, as some of the animals have occasionally suspected, the same scarecrow moved around at night by some unseen hand.

They are a family. Not in the biological sense (scarecrows are made of straw, wood, and old clothes) but in the sense that matters: they were *placed* together, they have *stood* together, and they have *witnessed* together for longer than anyone can remember.

Each one has a distinct personality, developed over decades of silent service. Each one has a distinct relationship with the animals and with the Farmer. Each one has, in its own way, become part of the farm's geography — not a landmark, exactly, but a *presence*. Something that is felt more than seen.

The scarecrows do not speak. With one exception — Old Stephen, who fell — they have never spoken, and they never will. But they *witness*. They watch. They hold. And in the long, quiet records of the farm, this is its own kind of speech.

---

## THE FAMILY STRUCTURE

The scarecrows are not ranked. They are not a hierarchy. They are not, as Doris has occasionally attempted to argue, a *committee*. They are simply four — each one doing the work that its field requires, in the manner that its placement and its history have taught it.

But the animals have noticed certain patterns.

### The East Field Scarecrow — Jeremiah ("The Morning One")

**Location:** The east field, where the corn grows tall and the sun arrives first.

**Appearance:** A tall, simple scarecrow — a wooden cross, a burlap sack, a red-and-black plaid shirt faded to pink-and-charcoal, and a wide-brimmed hat whose brim has softened over the years into a shape that resembles a tired, kindly bow. His trousers are patched with denim from a pair of jeans the Farmer wore in his twenties.

**Personality:** The East Field Scarecrow is the first to see the sun each morning. The animals say this has made him *patient* in a way the others are not. He is the watcher of beginnings. He does not move. He does not need to. He sees the day arrive, and he holds it, and he lets it be.

**The Farmer calls him:** *"Jerry."* No one else does. This is important.

**The animals call him:** *"The Morning One."* Not because he moves. Because he was there when the day began.

**Reintro Hook:** Something happens in the east field at dawn — a birth, a discovery, a departure. Jeremiah is the first to witness it, and the last to speak of it.

*(See [Jeremiah the Scarecrow](jeremiah_the_scarecrow.md) for the full file.)*

### The West Field Scarecrow — "The Evening One"

**Location:** The west field, where the sun sets longest and the light turns gold.

**Appearance:** A shorter, stockier scarecrow — a wooden frame that has weathered into something resembling a sleeping figure. His straw hat is a battered straw affair, its crown caved in on one side. His coat is a heavy wool jacket, gray with age, with a single brass button that has never fallen off.

**Personality:** The West Field Scarecrow sees the sun leave each evening. The animals say this has made him *reflective*. He is the watcher of endings. He does not resist the dark. He simply holds the last of the light until it is gone, and then he holds the dark, and then he holds the silence.

**The animals call him:** *"The Evening One."* Because he is the last to see the day before it goes.

**Reintro Hook:** A character sits with the Evening One at dusk, watching the sun go down, and comes away with something they did not have before.

### The North Field Scarecrow — "The Cold One"

**Location:** The north field, where the wind comes in hardest and the winter hits first.

**Appearance:** A wiry, angular scarecrow — built from a sturdier frame than the others, with a heavier coat, a scarf that the Farmer tied around his neck years ago and never removed, and a hat pulled down low against the wind. His posture is *leaning*, not from weakness but from a lifetime of bracing against the north wind.

**Personality:** The North Field Scarecrow endures. He does not complain. He does not bow. He simply stands, whatever the weather does, and does not fall. He is, by any measure, the toughest of the four. The animals respect him. They do not visit him often.

**The animals call him:** *"The Cold One."* Because he is always the first to know when winter is coming.

**Reintro Hook:** A character in crisis walks to the north field and stands beside the Cold One, and understands — without being told — that some things are survived by simply standing.

### The South Field Scarecrow — "The Quiet One"

**Location:** The south field, where the land slopes down toward the creek and the wind is mild.

**Appearance:** A small, gentle scarecrow — smaller than the others, with a softer shape. His clothes are the oldest of the four: a faded blue work shirt, brown trousers, and a felt hat that has lost its band. He does not loom. He simply *stands*, in the way that a small thing stands when it has decided not to be afraid.

**Personality:** The South Field Scarecrow is the quietest of the four. The animals say this is not because he has less to say, but because he is *listening*. He is the one who hears the creek, the one who hears the wind come through the tall grass, the one who hears the small things that the other three miss.

**The animals call him:** *"The Quiet One."* Because in a family of watchers, he is the one who *listens*.

**Reintro Hook:** A character needs to be *heard*, not advised. They walk to the south field, sit beside the Quiet One, and say what they have never said out loud.

## THE ABANDONED ONES

Beyond the four working scarecrows, there are others on the farm — retired ones, forgotten ones, and one that has become something else entirely (see [Old Stephen](old_stephen.md)).

### The Attic Scarecrow

**Location:** In the loft of the barn, folded and forgotten.

**About:** This is the scarecrow the Farmer's grandfather built — the *first* one. It is old, brittle, and no longer suitable for the fields. It was retired decades ago and placed in the barn's loft, where it has slowly gathered dust. The Farmer has never mentioned it. No one is sure if he remembers it is there.

The animals know. They do not disturb it.

### The Fence-Line Scarecrow

**Location:** Along the western fence line, half-collapsed against a post.

**About:** This scarecrow was put up years ago during a bad crow season. It was effective for one summer. The next summer, it was not. By the third summer, it had fallen over and no one had bothered to set it back up.

Now it lies against the fence, half-buried in weeds, its hat long gone. The animals pass it regularly. Some nod to it. Some do not.

### The Creek Scarecrow

**Location:** At the edge of the creek, upstream of the Long Road.

**About:** This one is barely recognizable as a scarecrow anymore. It has been weathered down to a wooden cross, a burlap sack, and a single leather boot that has somehow remained attached to the frame for over a decade. No one knows who built it. No one knows when.

It stands at the creek's edge, watching the water, saying nothing. The animals call it *"the Witness."*

### Old Stephen

**Location:** In the briar patch at the far south end of the farm.

**About:** Old Stephen is the only scarecrow who has ever *spoken*. He fell from his post during the Spring of the Great Wind, landed in a briar patch, and has been answering questions ever since. He is documented separately. See [Old Stephen](old_stephen.md).

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the fields report:

- A sense of being *seen*, but not in a way that feels judgmental. The scarecrows watch. They hold. They do not *judge*.
- A feeling that the field is *safe* — not because the scarecrow protects it, but because the scarecrow *remembers* it. The field has been watched for a long time. The watching has become part of the field.
- A slight reluctance to *leave* the field. The scarecrow does not call you back. But something in you wants to stay.
- A recurring dream, reported by several animals independently: *You are standing in a field. A scarecrow is standing in the next field over. You cannot see his face. But you know he is watching. And you know that you are safe.*

---

## THE LORE (Minor)

- The Farmer speaks to the scarecrows. He speaks to the East Field Scarecrow in the morning, the West Field Scarecrow in the evening, the North Field Scarecrow in the winter, and the South Field Scarecrow whenever he needs to think. He does not explain this. He does not need to.
- The animals do not speak to the four field scarecrows as often as they speak to Old Stephen. But they visit. They sit beside them. They say nothing. They leave.
- On the first day of each season, the Farmer visits all four scarecrows in order — East, then West, then North, then South. He carries a small basket of items to each one — a new hat, a new scarf, a repaired patch on a sleeve. He does not announce this. He does not explain it. He simply does it.
- The East Field Scarecrow and the West Field Scarecrow are said to be *in communication*, though no one has ever observed how. The Morning One and the Evening One have never spoken, but they *know* each other. This is enough.

## THE MYSTERY

**Who built the first scarecrow?** No one knows. The Attic Scarecrow is the oldest, but even it was not the first. There was something before it. There is always something before.

**Why does the Farmer talk to them?** No one knows. He has never explained. He may not know himself.

**What do the scarecrows *see*?** Everything. They see everything. They have stood in the fields for decades. They have watched every sunrise, every storm, every birth, every departure, every small thing that has happened on this farm. They remember. They do not speak. But they remember.

**Is the Creek Scarecrow *still* a scarecrow?** No one is sure. It has weathered down to almost nothing. But it stands. And something in the animals will not let them take it down.

---

## THE INVITATION

The fields are open to anyone. The scarecrows are open to no one — or rather, they are open to *everyone*, in the way that a standing post is open.

You are welcome to walk into any field. You are welcome to stand beside any scarecrow. You are welcome to sit at its base, or lean against its post, or simply stand nearby and look at the field the way it looks at the field.

The scarecrows will not answer. But they will *hold*. They will *witness*. They will *stand*.

That is enough.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

The Scarecrow Family is a **standing presence**. They are the farm's reminder that watching is a form of caring, that patience is a form of strength, and that the quietest things in a landscape are often the ones that hold it together.

They appear in stories as:

- **A place of stillness** — characters walk to a field and stand beside a scarecrow, and come away different
- **A place of counsel** — not because the scarecrows speak, but because *the standing itself* is a form of listening
- **A place of memory** — the scarecrows have been there for decades. They have seen everything. They do not say what they have seen, but their presence holds the history of the fields.

## THE FINAL WORD

The Scarecrow Family stands. They have always stood. They will stand long after the current generation of animals has moved on, long after the current Farmer has set down his tools, long after the fields have been replanted and replanted again.

They do not need to be noticed. They do not need to be thanked.

But if you walk into any field, at any hour, and stand beside the scarecrow that is there, you will feel something. You will feel *held*. Not by the straw. Not by the wood. By the *standing itself*.

The scarecrows are watching. They have always been watching.

And in a world of chaos, that is enough.
