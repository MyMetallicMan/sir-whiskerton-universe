# Artifact Bio: Old Stephen

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** Old Stephen (formerly "Steve," before replacement)

**Type:** Retired Scarecrow / Oracle of the Briars / Place of Counsel

**Location:** The far South end of the farm, where the pasture meets the overgrown ditch

**Status:** Decommissioned, decayed, unexpectedly wise

**First Documented:** The Spring of the Great Wind (year unknown)

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Old Stephen lies where he fell.

No one carried him here. No one buried him. One night, the wind came — a wind so strong it woke the animals in the barn and made the house creak on its foundation — and when morning came, Stephen was gone from his post in the cornfield. The Farmer searched for a day, then two, then gave up and built a new scarecrow. (The new one is also called Steve. It is not the same. The animals know this. They do not speak of it.)

Stephen landed in a tangle of briar bushes at the farm's southern edge, where the land slopes down toward the creek and the undergrowth grows thick and wild. The briars have grown up around him, through him, incorporating him into their thorny architecture. He is not trapped — he is *hosting*.

His body is a heap of old wood and faded cloth. The post that was his spine is cracked but still holds. The crossbars that were his arms are splintered, one pointing skyward, one pointing toward the creek. His original stuffing — straw, mostly — has long since scattered, fed the birds, become part of the earth. But enough remains to give him shape, to suggest the form he once was.

His shirt was red plaid once. Now it is the color of old rust, of dried blood, of something that has been washed by a thousand rains and faded by a thousand suns. In spring, tiny flowers push through the fabric — white and yellow and pale purple, as if the earth itself is decorating him. The briars have claimed his sleeves, using them as trellises. His pants, once denim, are now the same color as the soil, indistinguishable from it.

**His hat remains. This is the miracle.**

The straw hat, wide-brimmed and soft, still sits on what remains of his head. It is more hole than hat now, more shadow than substance. But it sits. It has not fallen. The wind that carried him here could not take his hat. The rains that have soaked him could not rot it away. The briars have grown around it, framing it, as if they too recognize that this hat is important.

In certain lights, at certain angles, the hat seems to nod. Just slightly. Just enough.

## THE JOURNEY

The animals remember the night Stephen fell.

**Rufus** was outside (he is always outside) and saw the whole thing. *"He just... lifted,"* Rufus reports. *"Like he was trying to fly. Like he'd always wanted to fly and finally figured out how. And then he was gone. Over the trees. Toward the south."*

**Doris** organized a search party at dawn. They found nothing. She filed a report. She still has it. It says: *"Subject: Stephen the Scarecrow. Status: Missing, presumed airborne. Notes: His hat is also missing. This is deeply concerning."*

**Porkchop** found him, three weeks later, while rooting for something edible in the briar patch. He did not tell the Farmer. He told the animals. And slowly, quietly, Stephen became their secret.

---

## THE ORACLE

**This is the strangest thing: Stephen speaks.**

Not with words, exactly. Not in the way you or I speak. But an animal in distress, an animal with a question, an animal who does not know which way to turn — if they come to the briar patch and sit quietly and listen, they will hear something.

A rustle of old cloth. A creak of weathered wood. A whisper that might be wind through straw or might be something else.

And then, somehow, they *know*. The answer arrives. Not as a sentence, but as a certainty. They know what to do.

- **Ditto** came once, confused about whether he should apologize to Doris for something. He sat. He listened. He left and apologized immediately. *"He didn't say anything,"* Ditto reported. *"But I knew. I just... knew."*
- **Bessie** comes sometimes, when the weight of being everyone's calm center becomes too heavy. She lies down near Stephen's briars and rests her head on the ground. After an hour, she rises and returns to the farm, lighter.
- **Poo Poo** visited once, rolling slowly down the slope. He got stuck in the briars — he is always getting stuck — and Stephen's hat seemed to tilt in his direction. A bramble moved, just slightly, creating a gap. Poo Poo rolled free. He has not returned, but he sends his gratitude through the wind.
- **Even Rufus**, who rarely needs advice because he rarely thinks ahead, came once. He sat. He listened. He left and immediately dug a hole. When asked why, he said: *"Stephen said I needed to. He didn't say it, but he said it. The hole felt right."* The hole later became a small pond during rains. The ducks use it. Rufus was proud.

## THE SEASONS

Stephen changes with the year.

**Spring:** Flowers push through his shirt. The briars bud and green. Young birds test their wings from his hat brim. He seems... expectant.

**Summer:** Thick and green, nearly hidden in the foliage. The briars bloom — small white flowers that smell faintly of honey. Stephen sleeps.

**Autumn:** The briars redden. The leaves fall. Stephen emerges from the green, more visible, more present. Animals come more often now, seeking counsel before winter.

**Winter:** Bare and cold, his old bones showing. The briars are brown and brittle. But his hat remains. Snow settles on its brim, and for a moment, in the right light, he looks almost like he did when he stood in the cornfield, keeping watch over things that did not need keeping.

---

## THE VISITORS

Those who come to Stephen know the protocol:

1. **Approach slowly.** The briars are not hostile, but they are not tame. They will part for those who come with respect. They will hold fast for those who do not.
2. **Sit within sight.** Not too close. Close enough that you can see his hat, his shirt, his weathered form. He needs to see you. He sees everything.
3. **Ask your question.** Out loud or in your heart. It does not matter. He hears both.
4. **Wait.** The answer may come quickly. It may take time. It may arrive days later, when you have forgotten you asked. But it will come.
5. **Leave something?** Not required. But some leave small things — a pretty stone, a feather, a berry. The briars accept these offerings. They disappear. Where they go, no one knows.

## THE LORE (Minor)

- The briars that hold Stephen are not ordinary briars. They are **Stephen's briars**. They grow where he directs. They part for those he approves. They hold fast for those who come with wrong intentions.
- No bird has ever nested in the briars around Stephen. They nest elsewhere in the patch, but not near him. They seem to know he is not for nesting.
- On the anniversary of the Great Wind, the briars hum. Not loudly — just a low vibration, felt more than heard. The animals who know about Stephen gather on that night, sitting in a loose circle at the edge of the patch, keeping vigil. They do not speak. They do not need to.
- The Farmer once walked near the briar patch, years ago, looking for a lost tool. He paused, looked directly at Stephen's form, frowned, and then walked away. He did not see what the animals see. Stephen's hat was still. That day, the briars were quiet. Some say Stephen was hiding. Some say he was waiting. Either way, the Farmer passed by, and Stephen remained a secret.

## THE MYSTERY

Why does Stephen give advice? He was never wise when he stood in the cornfield. He was just a scarecrow — straw and wood and old clothes, doing his job, scaring birds who were not actually scared of him.

But since he fell, since the briars took him in, since the flowers began pushing through his shirt — he has become something else.

Perhaps it is the fall itself. Perhaps being carried by the wind, lifted and thrown and abandoned, gave him a perspective he never had while standing rooted. Perhaps the briars are not just holding him but teaching him, their slow, patient wisdom seeping into his old wood.

Perhaps it is simply the flowers. They push through his shirt every spring, and each year they leave something behind — a memory of blooming, a reminder that even decay can be beautiful.

Or perhaps Stephen was always wise, and the cornfield was just too noisy for him to think. Here, in the quiet of the briars, with only the wind and the animals and the slow turning of seasons, he can finally hear his own thoughts.

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

Old Stephen appears in stories as:

- **A place of counsel** — characters come to him with problems and leave with answers
- **A place of memory** — he remembers the farm as it was, before the new Steve, before the changes
- **A place of transformation** — he was one thing, became another, and shows that endings can become beginnings

## THE FINAL WORD

Old Stephen lies in his briars, at the edge of the farm, at the edge of knowing. He is not forgotten — he is remembered differently. The animals know him. The briars hold him. The flowers decorate him. The seasons move through him.

He does not move. He does not speak. But he is present. He is patient. He is wise.

When you need to know something — something deep, something true, something you cannot ask anyone else — walk south. Past the pasture. Past the overgrown ditch. To the briar patch at the edge of everything.

Sit down where you can see the hat.

Wait.

The answer will come. Not in words, not in a voice. But it will come. Stephen will see to it.

He always does.
