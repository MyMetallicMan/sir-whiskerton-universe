# Artifact Bio: Jeremiah the Scarecrow

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** Jeremiah (also known as "Jerry")

**Type:** Confidant / Philosopher / Standing Guard

**Location:** The cornfield, where he has stood for as long as anyone can remember

**Status:** Silent. Patient. Witnessing.

**First Documented:** The Farmer has addressed him by name for decades. No one knows when the name was first given.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Jeremiah is a scarecrow. He is made of stuffed hay, old clothes, and a wooden cross. His hat is a wide-brimmed straw affair that has lost most of its brim. His coat is a faded brown work jacket. His shirt is a red-and-black plaid — the same plaid, the animals note, as the Farmer's.

He does not have a face. He has a burlap sack with two dark spots for eyes, stitched on by someone who cared. His mouth is a single line of stitching, tilted slightly upward at one end — the slightest hint of a smile.

Jeremiah has stood in the cornfield for as long as anyone can remember. He has weathered storms. He has weathered droughts. He has weathered the Farmer dressing him in various outfits and taking him on walks around the farm.

He has never fallen over. Not once.

*(This is technically untrue. He has fallen over. The animals have seen it. But they have decided not to count those instances, because the Farmer's reaction — a soft, quiet, "Oh dear" — was too tender to record.)*

---

## THE HISTORY

Jeremiah is the Farmer's primary confidant. The Farmer speaks to him as if he were a trusted council — about the weather, about the crops, about the nature of roundness, about the moon, about Martha.

Jeremiah does not respond. He does not need to. The Farmer does not require a response. He requires *attention*, and Jeremiah, standing in the field, is *always* attending.

The Farmer has, on multiple occasions, dressed Jeremiah in various outfits — a hat for the summer, a scarf for the winter, a set of silk ribbons on one memorable occasion. He has taken Jeremiah on walks around the farm. He has leaned on Jeremiah's post and talked for hours.

Jeremiah has never complained.

---

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the cornfield report a strange sense of *companionship*. As if someone is standing beside them, even when no one is there. As if the field is being *watched over*, not with suspicion, but with *care*.

The scarecrow is a still point in a moving world. He does not move. He does not speak. He simply *is*.

An hour in the cornfield feels like a moment. A moment feels like an hour. Jeremiah does not shift. He does not blink. He simply *stands*.

---

## THE MYSTERY

Does Jeremiah hear the Farmer's monologues? Does he *understand* them? Does he have any awareness at all?

No one knows. The Farmer does not ask. The animals do not ask.

Jeremiah stands. That is all. Jeremiah stands.

---

## THE INVITATION

The cornfield is open to anyone. Jeremiah is open to no one — or rather, he is open to *everyone*, in the way that a standing post is open. He does not choose his listeners. He simply listens.

You are welcome to stand beside him. You are welcome to speak to him. You are welcome to lean on his post and look out over the field.

He will not answer. But he will *hear*. He will *witness*. He will *stand*.

That is enough.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

Jeremiah the Scarecrow is a *witness*. He is the farm's reminder that attention is a form of love, that standing still is a form of presence, that the most important thing you can do for someone is to *be there* without needing to say anything at all.

He appears in stories as a still point, a quiet confidant, a place where characters go to think out loud. The Farmer speaks to Jeremiah more than he speaks to any human. The animals know this. They do not disturb it.

In the Commonplace Books, Jeremiah might appear as:

- A pencil sketch of the scarecrow, drawn in careful detail, with a note: *"He listens."*
- A pressed corn husk, saved from a harvest, with a note: *"Jeremiah's field."*
- A photograph of the cornfield at sunset, Jeremiah standing at the center, the light falling gold across his shoulders
- A page of the Farmer's notes, addressed to Jeremiah: *"You understand, don't you, Jerry?"*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

Jeremiah is silent. He is still. He is standing.

He has stood for decades. He will stand for decades more.

Come stand beside him. Speak if you like. Stay if you like. Leave if you like.

He will not notice. He will not mind. He will simply *be*.

That is what he does. That is what he *is*.
