# Artifact Bio: Jeremiah the Scarecrow

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** Jeremiah (also known as "Jerry" by the Farmer, "The Morning One" by the animals, and "Old Straw" by no one in particular)

**Type:** Standing Guard / Farmer's Confidant / Place of Counsel

**Location:** The east field, where the corn grows tall and the sun arrives first

**Status:** Standing. Listening. Quietly holding the east field together.

**First Documented:** The Farmer's grandfather placed him in the east field decades ago. He has stood there ever since.

---

## RECONCILIATION NOTE

Jeremiah is one of the four field scarecrows of the Scarecrow Family (see [The Scarecrow Family](the_scarecrow_family.md)) — specifically, the **East Field Scarecrow**, whom the animals call *"The Morning One."*

He is the *oldest* of the four working scarecrows. He is the *tallest*. He is the *first to see the sun each morning*. And, more than any of the others, he is the one the Farmer talks to *most often*.

The Farmer calls him *"Jerry."* No one else does. This is important.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Jeremiah is a tall, simple scarecrow.

He is built from a wooden cross — the *original* cross, still holding, still straight after decades of weather. His body is a burlap sack, packed with straw that has been replaced, over the years, more times than anyone can remember. His head is a smaller burlap sack, tied at the neck, with two dark buttons for eyes and a single line of stitching for a mouth — tilted slightly, at one end, into what might be a smile.

His clothes are the clothes of the Farmer's father — a red-and-black plaid shirt, faded now to something closer to pink-and-charcoal, and a pair of denim overalls that have been patched so many times they are no longer denim, exactly. His hat is a wide-brimmed straw affair, the brim softened over the years into a shape that resembles a tired, kindly bow.

He has stood in the east field for as long as anyone can remember. His straw has been refreshed. His clothes have been mended. His hat has been replaced once — the Farmer did it himself, one spring, without ceremony — but it is the *same kind of hat*, and no one has commented on the replacement.

Jeremiah has never fallen over. He has *leaned*, once or twice, in a bad storm, but he has never fallen. The Farmer has always set him back up. Always.

---

## ROLE ON THE FARM

Jeremiah serves three roles on the farm. They are, in order of importance:

1. **The Farmer's confidant.** The Farmer talks to him. Often. Not as a *scarecrow*, but as a *listener* — a fellow being who stands in the field and hears what the Farmer has to say.
2. **The morning witness.** Jeremiah is the first creature on the farm to see the sun each day. The animals say this has made him *patient* in a way the others are not. He *watches* the day arrive, and holds it, and lets it be.
3. **A standing guard.** Jeremiah scares the crows. He is *good* at this. He has always been good at this. The crows have learned to respect him.

The first role is the one that matters most.

---

## THE FARMER'S CONFIDANT

The Farmer talks to Jeremiah more than he talks to any other being on the farm — more than Sir Whiskerton, more than Bartholomew, more than the moon.

He talks to Jeremiah about:

- **The weather.** The Farmer narrates the weather to Jeremiah the way some people narrate their day. *"Coming in dry, Jerry. Don't know if we'll get rain. Thought about watering the far row, but I want to see what the sky does first."*
- **The crops.** Jeremiah knows about every row, every seed, every harvest. The Farmer tells him. The Farmer tells him everything.
- **His thoughts.** The Farmer thinks out loud when he is with Jeremiah. He thinks about Martha. He thinks about his father. He thinks about the letters he never sends. He thinks about the moon, and the melons, and the way the light falls in the afternoon.
- **The small things.** Jeremiah is the one who hears about the ladybug on the fence post, the way the wheat has grown, the way the Spring has been particularly cold this year, the way the eucalyptus grove has put out a new leaf. The Farmer notices these things. He tells Jeremiah about them. He does not tell anyone else.

The Farmer has never explained why he talks to Jeremiah. He has never needed to. Jeremiah is *there*. Jeremiah *listens*. That is enough.

---

## JEREMIAH'S ROLE IN THE SCARECROW FAMILY

Among the four field scarecrows, Jeremiah is the *eldest*. The Farmer's grandfather placed him in the east field first, and the others followed — West, then North, then South — over the years.

The animals say that Jeremiah *knows* this. They say he *holds* the family, in a way that is quiet but real. He is not a *leader*, exactly — the four scarecrows do not lead each other — but he is the *anchor*. He is the one the others are *placed* in relation to.

In the seasonal visits — the Farmer's visits to all four scarecrows at the turn of each season — Jeremiah is always visited *first*. The Farmer brings him something small each time: a new patch for his shirt, a small bundle of dried corn, a smooth stone from the Spring. He does not explain these gifts. He does not need to.

Jeremiah keeps the gifts. The animals have seen them, tucked into his clothing, resting at his feet. He does not *use* them. He *holds* them.

---

## THE ANIMALS AND JEREMIAH

The animals do not talk to Jeremiah as often as the Farmer does. But they *visit* him. They *sit* with him. They say nothing. They leave.

Sir Whiskerton has been observed, on several occasions, sitting in the east field at dawn, watching the sun come up beside Jeremiah. He has never commented on this. No one has ever asked.

Doris the Hen has, on at least one occasion, delivered a *formal report* to Jeremiah — reading it aloud, in the field, to the scarecrow, and then waiting as if for a response. The response did not come. Doris filed the report anyway. She considers Jeremiah *a trusted official*.

Ditto the Kitten has tried, more than once, to climb Jeremiah. He has never succeeded. He has never stopped trying.

Porkchop the Pig once brought Jeremiah a small piece of cornbread — a gift, carefully wrapped in a leaf. The cornbread was gone the next day. No one knows who took it. Porkchop considers the matter *resolved in Jeremiah's favor*.

---

## THE FEELING

Visitors to the east field report:

- A sense of being *seen*, but not judged. Jeremiah watches. He holds. He does not *evaluate*. He simply *witnesses*.
- A feeling that the field is *safe* — not because Jeremiah protects it, but because Jeremiah *remembers* it. The field has been watched for a long time. The watching has become part of the field.
- A slight reluctance to *leave* the field. Jeremiah does not call you back. But something in you wants to stay.
- A recurring dream, reported by several animals independently: *You are standing in the east field. Jeremiah is standing beside you. You are both watching the sun rise. Neither of you speaks. You wake up feeling that you have been seen.*

---

## THE MYSTERY

**Does Jeremiah *hear* the Farmer's monologues?** No one knows. The Farmer does not ask. The Farmer does not need to. The Farmer simply *talks*, and Jeremiah *stands*, and the talking is *enough*.

**Does Jeremiah *understand*?** No one knows. He is a scarecrow. He is *not* a scarecrow. Both of these are true. The farm has decided not to know.

**Why does the Farmer call him "Jerry"?** No one knows. The Farmer has never explained. The name is *his*, not the farm's, not the animals', not anyone else's. It is a *private name*, kept private.

**Why does the Farmer visit Jeremiah first, every season?** No one knows. The Farmer has never explained. The animals suspect — but they do not know.

---

## THE INVITATION

The east field is open to anyone. Jeremiah is open to no one — or rather, he is open to *everyone*, in the way that a standing post is open.

You are welcome to walk into the field. You are welcome to stand beside Jeremiah. You are welcome to sit at his base, or lean against his post, or simply stand nearby and look at the field the way he looks at the field.

He will not answer. But he will *hold*. He will *witness*. He will *stand*.

That is enough.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

Jeremiah is a **witness**. He is the farm's reminder that attention is a form of love, that standing still is a form of presence, and that the most important thing you can do for someone is to *be there* without needing to say anything at all.

He appears in stories as:

- **A quiet confidant** — characters go to Jeremiah when they need to think out loud
- **A morning witness** — Jeremiah sees the sun rise first, and holds the day for the farm
- **A family anchor** — among the four field scarecrows, Jeremiah is the eldest, the first, the one the others are placed in relation to

In the Commonplace Books, Jeremiah might appear as:

- A pencil sketch of Jeremiah in the east field at dawn, the sun just rising behind him, his shadow stretching across the corn
- A pressed corn husk, saved from the east field, with a note: *"Jerry's field."*
- A photograph of Jeremiah at sunset, his buttons catching the last light
- **A page of the Farmer's notes**, addressed to Jeremiah, in the Farmer's looping hand: *"Good talk today, Jerry. I don't know what I'd do without you."*
- A single dried corn kernel, tucked into the back of the Commonplace Book, with a note: *"He kept it. I don't know why. It was just a kernel."*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

Jeremiah stands. He has always stood. He will stand long after the current Farmer has set down his tools, long after the current generation of animals has moved on, long after the east field has been replanted and replanted again.

He does not need to be noticed. He does not need to be thanked.

But if you walk into the east field, at any hour, and stand beside him, you will feel something. You will feel *held*. Not by the straw. Not by the wood. By the *standing itself*.

The Farmer calls him Jerry. No one else does.

And that is enough.
