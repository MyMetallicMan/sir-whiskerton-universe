# Artifact Bio: The Chipped Ceramic Bowl

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** The Chipped Ceramic Bowl

**Type:** Altar / Held Space / Absence

**Location:** On the porch, by the kitchen door

**Status:** Always clean. Never used. Waiting.

**First Documented:** As long as anyone can remember. Which is to say, no one remembers a time without it.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

It is a small ceramic bowl — no wider than a saucer, no deeper than a teacup. The glaze is a soft, faded blue, cracked in places with fine lines that look like veins. There is a chip on the rim, on the side that faces the door, as if someone once tipped it and never quite forgave themselves.

The bowl is always clean. It has never held food. It has never held water. It has never held anything at all, as far as anyone has ever seen.

It sits on a small wooden stool on the porch, just to the left of the kitchen door. It is in the way. It has been in the way for decades.

The Farmer has never moved it. No one has asked him to.

---

## THE HISTORY

No one knows who made the bowl. No one knows who chipped it. No one knows why the Farmer keeps it.

The bowl has been on the porch for as long as anyone can remember. The Farmer has never spoken of it. He has never filled it. He has never moved it. He simply *keeps* it.

Occasionally, when he sits on the porch in the evening, he will glance at the bowl. His expression changes — barely, softly. Then he looks away.

The animals know not to drink from it. They know not to knock it over. They know, without being told, that the bowl is not for them.

---

## THE FEELING

The bowl has no feeling. It is a bowl. But the porch, near the bowl, feels different.

It feels *held*. As if someone is thinking of someone else. As if a small ritual is being performed, every day, without ceremony.

Visitors who sit on the porch — rarely — report a sensation of *presence*. Not of being watched, but of being near something that is being *kept*.

The bowl is not sad. It is not happy. It is simply *there*. Waiting for something that may never come.

---

## THE MYSTERY

What is the bowl for? Why is it empty? Who is the Farmer waiting for?

No one knows. No one asks. The bowl is a *private thing*, kept in a public place.

The farm has decided not to know. Some altars are meant to stay uncrowded.

---

## THE INVITATION

The bowl is not for you. Do not fill it. Do not move it. Do not ask about it.

But you may sit on the porch. You may be near it. You may allow the feeling of the porch — the held-ness, the waiting — to settle around you.

The bowl does not require anything. It simply is.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

The Chipped Ceramic Bowl is an *altar to absence*. It is the farm's reminder that waiting is a form of love, that some things are held for people who may never come, that *space can be a gift*.

It appears in stories as a quiet motif — a bowl that is always clean, a porch that is always patient, a Farmer who glances at something no one else sees.

In the Commonplace Books, the bowl might appear as:

- A pencil sketch of the bowl, with the chip in the rim drawn carefully, and a single line of text beneath: *"For someone."*
- A pressed flower, found near the bowl one morning, with a note: *"No one knows who left this. No one asked."*
- A photograph of the porch at dusk, the bowl catching the last light
- A recipe for a soup that no one has ever made, in the Farmer's handwriting, titled *"For the bowl, if the guest ever comes."*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

The bowl is empty. It will remain empty. It is waiting for something that may never arrive.

But it is *there*. On the porch. In the light. Always clean.

Come sit nearby. Do not fill it. Do not ask.

Just let it be. Just let the Farmer be.

The waiting is the point.
