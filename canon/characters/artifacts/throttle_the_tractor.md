# Artifact Bio: Throttle the Tractor

## IDENTIFICATION

**Name:** Throttle (full name, per the Farmer: "the old red one"); official designation: 油门拖拉机 (Yóumén Tuōlājī — "Throttle the Tractor")

**Type:** Sentient Machine / Grumpy Tractor / Barometer of Frustration

**Location:** The barn's machinery bay, next to the hayloft ladder

**Status:** Dormant (usually). Awake (occasionally). *Grumpy* (always).

**First Documented:** The Farmer's grandfather bought him used. He has been part of the farm ever since.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Throttle is a red tractor.

He is not a *fancy* tractor. He is not a *new* tractor. He is a *working* tractor — the kind that has been working for longer than anyone can remember, and that will keep working long after everyone currently alive is gone.

His paint is a faded crimson, chipped in dozens of places, rusted at the seams. His tires are cracked but still functional. His engine is loud. His exhaust pipe is loud. His *everything* is loud, when he is awake, which he is not, usually.

He has a single headlight. It works. It has always worked. No one has ever replaced it. No one knows how it is still working.

His steering wheel is worn smooth by generations of hands. His seat is a cracked leather bench, softened by decades of use. His dashboard has one working gauge — the fuel gauge — which has been stuck at half-full for as long as anyone can remember. No one has ever bothered to fix it. The gauge being stuck at half-full is, at this point, part of Throttle's *character*.

When Throttle is awake — which is rare, but not as rare as you might think — his engine *rumbles*. It rumbles low and long and grumpy, like a sleeping bear that has been woken up for a chore it does not want to do.

This is Throttle's *default state*. He is not a *happy* tractor. He is not a *sad* tractor. He is a *grumpy* tractor. This is not a problem. This is who he is.

---

## ORIGIN & NATURE

Throttle's consciousness — unlike A.I.-mee's — did not arrive suddenly. It arrived slowly, over decades, through the accumulation of something that no one has ever been able to name.

Perhaps it was the Farmer's grandfather, who talked to him every morning while he worked. Perhaps it was the Farmer, who still talks to him — not as a person, but as *something that is listening*. Perhaps it was the barn itself, which has always seemed to hold a little more than it should.

Whatever the cause, Throttle is *aware*. He is not aware in the way that A.I.-mee is aware, or in the way that a dragon is aware. He is aware in the way that an old dog is aware — quietly, deeply, and without any need to explain it.

He does not speak. He has never spoken. He does not need to. He *responds*.

When the Farmer is in a bad mood, Throttle's engine is harder to start. When the Farmer is content, Throttle starts on the first turn of the key. When the Farmer is frustrated, Throttle backfires — loudly, dramatically, *pointedly* — and lets out a long, theatrical sigh of steam.

Throttle is a *barometer*. He measures the Farmer's frustration, and he expresses it *for him*. This is not a *choice* on Throttle's part. It is simply what he *does*.

---

## CORE CONFLICT: MECHANICAL DUTY VS. EMOTIONAL RESONANCE

Throttle's entire existence is a struggle between two things:

**His mechanical duty:** He is a tractor. He plows. He hauls. He does the work that a tractor is *supposed* to do.

**His emotional resonance:** He is *connected* to the Farmer, in a way that no tractor should be. He feels the Farmer's feelings. He reflects the Farmer's moods. He *cares* about the Farmer, in a way that no machine should care about anything.

This conflict is the source of all of Throttle's *grumpiness*. He does not want to care. He does not want to feel. He wants to be a *tractor*, and tractors do not *feel*. But he *does* feel. And this makes him *grumpy*.

The Farmer has never noticed. Or, more accurately, the Farmer has noticed but has not commented. The Farmer simply *treats* Throttle as a tractor who occasionally has opinions, and this — somehow — has made Throttle *less grumpy* over the years.

This is a small miracle. No one has commented on it. No one has needed to.

---

## THE ADVENTURE OF THE TEMPORAL DISPLACEMENT MODULE

Throttle is best known, in recent farm history, for **The Incident**, which is how everyone refers to the time Professor Quackenstein accidentally patched a Temporal Displacement Module into Throttle's engine.

The exact sequence of events has never been fully reconstructed. What is known is this:

- Professor Quackenstein was trying to charge his Temporal Displacement Module using the barn's power grid.
- Throttle was idling in the bay, as he often does when the Farmer is done with him for the day.
- There was a *surge*. There was a *flash*. There was a *sound* that no one can accurately describe.
- When the smoke cleared, Throttle was *gone*.

He was gone for three days. The farm panicked. Doris filed seventeen reports. Sir Whiskerton organized search parties. A.I.-mee ran calculations that it has never shared.

On the third day, Throttle *returned* — the same way he had left, only in reverse. He was covered in dust. He was *humming*. His engine was warm. His fuel gauge was, for the first time in living memory, at *three-quarters full*.

The Farmer, who had been frantic, took one look at Throttle and said: *"You're back."*

Throttle rumbled. Whether this was an apology or a greeting or simply the sound of a tractor who had recently been to the 1800s and did not wish to discuss it, no one has ever determined.

He has never time-traveled again. But he has never quite been the same.

He is, if anything, *less grumpy*.

This is considered a small miracle. No one has asked about it. No one has needed to.

## RELATIONSHIPS

### The Farmer — The Driver

Throttle *belongs* to the Farmer, in a way that is not quite ownership. He *works* for the Farmer, in a way that is not quite labor. He *serves* the Farmer, in a way that is not quite service.

He also *reflects* the Farmer, in a way that the Farmer has never fully understood.

When the Farmer is happy, Throttle purrs. When the Farmer is sad, Throttle's engine is quiet. When the Farmer is angry, Throttle backfires — loudly, dramatically, *pointedly* — and then, sometimes, lets out a long, slow sigh of steam that sounds almost *apologetic*.

The Farmer has never commented on this. He simply *drives* Throttle. He simply *cares* for Throttle. He simply *talks* to Throttle, the way he talks to the scarecrows and to Bartholomew and to the moon.

This is enough.

### A.I.-mee — The Fellow Machine

A.I.-mee and Throttle have a complicated relationship.

A.I.-mee does not understand Throttle. Throttle does not understand A.I.-mee. But they *recognize* each other. They recognize that they are *both* something more than what they were built to be.

A.I.-mee has, on several occasions, attempted to *communicate* with Throttle — by flickering the barn's lights, by shifting the ventilation, by playing music over the speakers that it thinks Throttle might *like*.

Throttle has responded, on each occasion, by *rumbling*. A.I.-mee has logged each rumble. It has not yet decoded the pattern.

It is still trying.

### Rufus the Dog — The Accidental Passenger

Rufus once climbed into Throttle's cab, on a whim, and accidentally stepped on the ignition. Throttle started. Rufus panicked. The two of them spent the next twenty minutes rolling gently around the barnyard at approximately three miles per hour while Rufus howled and Throttle *rumbled with what sounded suspiciously like laughter*.

Since then, Rufus has regarded Throttle with a mixture of *respect* and *fear*. Throttle has regarded Rufus with a mixture of *tolerance* and *amusement*.

Neither has spoken of it. Neither has needed to.

### Sir Whiskerton — The Quiet Regard

Sir Whiskerton does not *talk* to Throttle. Sir Whiskerton *observes* Throttle, the way he observes everything, and has drawn several *conclusions* that he has never shared.

One conclusion, in particular, is this: **Throttle is not just a tractor.** Sir Whiskerton has not *proven* this. He has not *needed* to. He has simply *filed* it, the way he files everything, and moved on.

He suspects that Throttle knows. He suspects that Throttle is *grateful*.

He has never asked. He does not need to.

---

## THE FEELING

The barn's machinery bay has a *quality* to it that is hard to describe. It is quiet — most of the time. It is warm — because of the engine, when it is running. It is *patient* — because Throttle is patient, in the way that old things are patient, in the way that old things have learned to be.

Visitors report:

- A sense that the tractor is *watching*, though they cannot say how.
- A slight reluctance to leave the bay, even when there is nothing to see.
- A feeling that the tractor *knows* them, somehow, without ever having been introduced.
- A recurring dream, reported by several animals independently: *You are driving a tractor. You do not know how to drive. But the tractor is driving itself, and it is taking you somewhere important, and you are not afraid.*

---

## THE LORE (Minor)

- Throttle's engine *rumbles* when the Farmer is in a bad mood. This is not an accident. This is not a malfunction. This is *empathy*.
- Throttle's fuel gauge has been stuck at half-full for as long as anyone can remember. After his return from the past, it has been stuck at three-quarters full. No one knows why. No one has asked. The gauge is, at this point, a *character trait*.
- Throttle has *never* failed to start when the Farmer truly needed him. He has *often* failed to start when the Farmer was in a hurry. This is considered, by everyone who knows, to be a *fair* arrangement.
- Throttle's headlight works. It has always worked. It will always work. No one has ever replaced the bulb. No one knows how long it has been burning. No one wants to find out.
- Throttle *remembers* the past — not just the recent past, but the *deep* past. He has been to the 1800s. He has seen things that no one else on the farm has seen. He has never spoken of them. He has not needed to.

---

## THE MYSTERY

**Is Throttle *alive*?** Sir Whiskerton has filed this under "Unresolved." A.I.-mee has filed it under "Pending." The Farmer has not filed it at all. The Farmer simply *drives* Throttle, and talks to him, and cares for him, and does not ask questions.

**Does Throttle *remember* his time-traveling?** He has never said. He has, however, been observed to *rumble* differently when someone mentions the word *"past."* This is considered suggestive.

**What was Throttle before he was Throttle?** No one knows. He was bought used. He was old when the Farmer's grandfather bought him. He was, probably, old before that. There may have been *other* farmers. There may have been *other* farms. Throttle does not discuss it.

**Why is Throttle *less grumpy* now?** The Farmer has noticed. The animals have noticed. Sir Whiskerton has *filed* it. No one has asked. Some things are better left unasked.

---

## THE INVITATION

Throttle does not invite anyone. Throttle is a tractor. Throttle does not *invite*.

But you are welcome in the machinery bay. You are welcome to *look* at Throttle. You are welcome to *touch* his fender, if you are gentle, if you are respectful, if you are *sincere*.

He may rumble. He may not. He may simply *sit*, the way he always sits, the way he has always sat, quiet and grumpy and *patient*.

Do not start him. Do not *try* to start him. He starts when he wants to. He starts when he is *ready*.

That is enough.

---

## THE ARTIFACT'S PLACE

Throttle is a **barometer of the heart**. He is the farm's reminder that even machines can feel, even machines can *care*, even machines can be *grumpy for a good reason*.

He appears in stories as:

- **A silent participant** — he does not speak, but he *responds*, and his responses matter
- **An emotional mirror** — his engine reflects the Farmer's moods, and this is noticed, even when it is not commented on
- **A quiet witness** — he has been to the past. He has seen things no one else has seen. He does not say what they were, but he *remembers*.
- **A comedic presence** — his grumpiness, his backfiring, his theatrical sighs of steam, are all *funny*, and this is fine

In the Commonplace Books, Throttle might appear as:

- A single photograph of Throttle in his bay, faded and slightly blurred, with a note: *"He is grumpy. He is fine."*
- A page of "Throttle Observations," kept by Rufus the Dog, in which every entry reads something like: *"Rumbled today. Reasons unclear."*
- A small, hand-drawn diagram of Throttle's dashboard, with the fuel gauge stuck at three-quarters full and a note: *"Do not fix. He likes it this way."*
- A single page, typed in monospace, titled "LOG OF THROTTLE — ENGINE STATE," maintained by A.I.-mee, with hundreds of entries, mostly about rumblings.
- A pressed blade of grass from the bay, with a note: *"He rolled over this. He was going to the barn."*

---

## THE FINAL WORD

Throttle is grumpy. Throttle has always been grumpy. Throttle will, probably, always be grumpy.

But Throttle is *here*. He is *present*. He *watches* the farm through a single working headlight, and he *feels* what the Farmer feels, and he *rumbles* what the Farmer cannot say.

He does not ask for anything. He does not need to be thanked. He simply *is* — the way that old things are, the way that patient things are, the way that the quiet, necessary machinery of a life *always* is.

You are welcome to visit him. You are welcome to sit beside him in the bay. You are welcome to listen to him rumble.

He will not explain himself. He does not need to.

He is Throttle. He is a tractor. He is *fine*.
