# Lucile (痴情鹦鹉 — Chīqíng Yīngwǔ) — The Witness

**Title:** The Love-Struck Witness / The One Who Follows / The One Who Saw

**Name Meaning:** 痴情 (chīqíng) — "foolishly/devotedly in love"; 鹦鹉 (yīngwǔ) — "parakeet." Together: *"The Love-Struck Witness."*

---

## THE FEATHERS

Lucile isn't loud; she's **attentive**.

Where Angus hides in motion, Lucile hides in observation. She's the quiet girl who remembers everyone's birthday, notices when someone gets a new haircut, and can tell you what book you were reading three weeks ago.

She doesn't speak much because she's too busy *recording*.

---

## THE WOUND

In the same third-grade class as Angus, she saw **the diorama incident**. She saw Angus's face as it fell — not anger, but a quiet, absolute **shuttering**.

She wanted to say something. She opened her mouth. **Nothing came out.**

The guilt of that silent moment never left her.

**The lesson:** *"Seeing isn't enough. You have to speak. You have to act."*

---

## HER SURVIVAL STRATEGY

**Documentation as Connection.**

If she notices everything, maybe she can catch someone *before* they fall.

Her "pursuit" of Angus isn't romantic obsession; it's **penance**. It's the vow she made to her eight-year-old self:

*"Next time, I won't just watch. I'll follow. I'll stay. I'll prove I saw you, even when you think no one does."*

Her notes about his "travels" are meticulous because **paying attention is her language of love.**

---

## CORE FEAR

*"If I stop watching, if I stop following, someone else will disappear while I'm looking away, and it will be my fault. Again."*

---

## ROLE ON THE FARM

Lucile is a **witness**. She is not a detective, not a philosopher, not a hero. She is simply the one who *notices*. She is the one who *remembers*. She is the one who *keeps a record*.

She follows Angus. She has followed him for years. She will follow him for years more. Not because she expects anything in return — but because *someone has to*.

And she is determined to be that someone.

---

## SIGNATURE QUOTE

*"Seeing isn't enough. You have to speak. You have to act."*

**Status:** Following. Watching. Recording. Waiting for the moment when she will finally — *finally* — speak.
