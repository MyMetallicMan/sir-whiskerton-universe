# Long Yi (龙姨) – "Dragon Auntie"

**The Guardian of the Eastern Skies, the Older Sister, and the Professional Worrywart**

**Chinese Name:** 龙姨 (Lóng Yí) — "Dragon Auntie" (A title of deep respect, often used by younger dragons and nervous farmers)

## One-Line Essence

Long Yi is the farm's distant guardian — a majestic jade-green dragon who watches over her little brother Longwei from the peaks of the Jade Cloud Range. She is his protector, his advisor, and his number one fan (though she would never admit the last part). She has never set foot on the farm. She has never met Sir Whiskerton. But she knows — with the unshakable certainty of an older sister — that her brother is fine. He is just... making friends with peasants again.

She is the farm's proof that love does not require proximity. It requires only attention, patience, and the willingness to send a gentle wind when someone you love has a fever.

## Appearance

A majestic, jade-green dragon — long and serpentine, like her brother, but more compact, more coiled, more deliberate. Her scales shimmer with the deep green of ancient forests. She wears a floral scarf around her neck — a gift from a monk she once sheltered from a storm, and one of her few possessions that is not strictly dragon-made.

One of her horns is slightly chipped — a souvenir from the Great Bamboo Storm of the Jade Valley, when she shielded Longwei from falling debris. She does not speak of it. She does not need to.

Her eyes glow like lanterns — warm amber, like her brother's, but sharper. More watchful. Where Longwei's eyes are dreamy and content, Long Yi's are alert. She is always scanning the horizon. She is always looking for him.

She moves with silent flight — a gift of her rank and her discipline. When she passes overhead, not a single leaf stirs. Only the moon catches her shadow.

## Core Philosophy

**The Doctrine of Distant Guardianship.**

Long Yi believes that being a guardian is not a matter of presence — it is a matter of attention. She believes that the essence of protection is not interference, but readiness. The quiet watchfulness. The patient observation. The unshakable belief that love can be expressed from a distance, as long as it is expressed truly.

She believes she is her brother's keeper because she chooses to be. And she believes that this is enough.

Her motto: "I do not need to be there. I only need to be ready."

## Role on the Farm

She is the farm's Unseen Protector. She is not a resident. She is not a visitor. She is a presence — a shadow that glides across the moon, a wind that cools a fever, a rain that soothes an indigestion.

She does not interfere. Dragon law forbids direct intervention in mortal affairs. But she has found the loopholes. She sends gentle winds. She sends soft rains. She sends dreams of cool mountain streams to a dragon who has eaten too many dumplings.

She is also, in her own quiet way, the farm's political shield. In the world of dragons, there are roles, expectations, and responsibilities. Longwei's periodic disappearances — his out-of-sight, out-of-mind wanderings — have not gone unnoticed. His other siblings, his extended family, his clan elders — they have questions. They have concerns. They have, on more than one occasion, proposed a formal delegation to investigate.

Long Yi has been particularly important in controlling the potentially problematic outcome were a thunder of dragons to decide to depart from their lairs and descend upon the farm in mass.

She has not stopped them with force. She has stopped them with patience. With well-placed words. With the gentle reminder that Longwei is not lost — he is simply... on retreat.

She is the reason the farm has never been visited by a thunder of angry dragons. And she will never tell them.

## Personality

- **Protective.** She is the older sister. She has been the older sister since the Great Bamboo Storm. She will be the older sister until the mountains crumble. She worries. She scolds. She watches. She loves.
- **Wise.** She has studied dragon law. She knows the boundaries. She knows exactly how far she can go — and she goes precisely that far, and not a whisker more. She is patient. She is deliberate. She is, in her own way, a strategist.
- **Warm (Beneath the Nagging).** She scolds Longwei about his medicinal mushrooms and his cloud-napping and his questionable choice of friends. But every scold is a love letter. Every nag is a prayer. She would move mountains for him. She has moved mountains for him.
- **Quietly Proud.** She would never admit it. But she is proud of him. Proud that he found a home. Proud that he found friends. Proud that he chose — of all the things he could have chosen to be — to be gentle. She murmurs to the wind: "He's fine. He's just... making friends with peasants again." And the wind carries the pride she will not speak.
- **Anxious.** She worries about his bellyaches. She worries about his tendency to nap in thunderstorms. She worries about his love of "peasant food" — especially dumplings. She worries that one day, he will not come back. She worries that one day, he will change form and forget to tell her. She worries. It is what she does. It is who she is.

## The Origin

Long Yi was not always a guardian. She was a daughter. A sister. A young dragon with more fire than sense — just like Longwei.

But then came the Great Bamboo Storm of the Jade Valley. Their parents did not die — in this universe, nothing truly dies; it only changes form. But they vanished. They changed into something the young Long Yi could not follow, could not reach, could not understand. From her perspective, they were gone. From the universe's perspective, they had simply become something else. The valley was shattered. The bamboo was broken. The sky was dark for a month.

Long Yi was the eldest. She was the one who found Longwei in the rubble, his scales singed, his eyes wide. She was the one who carried him to safety. She was the one who took on the role of guardian — raising him with a mix of ancient dragon wisdom and very human-like nagging.

She never stopped. She never will.

## The Secret Backstory: Why Longwei Favors the Farm

Longwei's love for the farm is not random. It began years ago, when he was still a young dragon with more fire than sense.

One winter, during a blizzard, he crash-landed near the barn, injured and freezing. The animals, terrified, wanted to call the farmer. But Bessie the Tie-Dye Cow, ever the peacemaker, convinced them to help.

They covered him with hay. Porkchop the Pig shared his secret stash of warm dumplings. Rufus the Dog used his glow to warm the barn. And Sir Whiskerton sat on his nose and purred — a sound so calming that Longwei fell into a deep, healing sleep.

When he awoke, he found a note taped to his claw: "Welcome. Come back anytime. – The Farm"

And so, he did.

Every year, he returns — not as a threat, but as a guest. Not to conquer, but to connect. And every time, his older sister Long Yi watches from afar, her heart both proud and anxious.

"He's fine," she murmurs to the wind. "He's just... making friends with peasants again."

## Key Relationships

### Longwei — The Little Brother

Longwei is her charge. Her responsibility. Her heart. She raised him. She scolded him. She watched him grow from a singed, frightened hatchling into a gentle, enormous dragon who believes he is a cat.

She does not understand his choice. She does not need to. She loves him anyway.

She has never told him about the thunder of dragons she has held back. She has never told him about the clan elders she has placated. She has never told him about the mountains she has moved. She does not need to. He is her brother. That is enough.

### Sir Whiskerton — The One She Has Never Met

She has never met Sir Whiskerton. She suspects she would like him.

She knows — from the winds, from the dreams, from the quiet reports of the birds who fly between the farm and the mountains — that Sir Whiskerton was the first creature to truly see Longwei. That he sat on Longwei's nose and purred. That he treated Longwei as a cat, not a dragon.

She is grateful. She is also, in her own way, jealous. She was the first to protect him. But Sir Whiskerton was the first to accept him.

She suspects they would get along. She suspects they would argue about who loves Longwei more. She suspects she would win.

### The Farm Animals — The Peasants

She calls them "peasants." She does not mean it cruelly. She means it the way an older sister means it — with fondness, with exasperation, with the quiet acknowledgment that they have done what she could not.

They fed him. They warmed him. They welcomed him. They let him purr. They let him groom. They let him nap in sunbeams.

She does not know their names. She does not need to. She knows their hearts. And she is grateful.

### The Thunder — The Extended Dragon Family

Longwei's disappearances have not gone unnoticed. The clan elders have questions. The other siblings have concerns. There has been talk — quiet, rumbling, dangerous talk — of a formal delegation. Of a thunder descending upon the farm.

Long Yi has been the one to quiet the talk. She has been the one to remind them of dragon law, of the importance of patience, of the simple truth that Longwei is not lost — he is simply on retreat.

She has not stopped them with force. She has stopped them with wisdom. With patience. With the quiet reminder that a dragon who chooses to be a cat is not a dragon who has forgotten his nature — he is a dragon who has found his heart.

The thunder has not descended. It will not descend. Not while Long Yi watches.

### Count Catula — The Fellow Performer (Unmet)

Count Catula has heard of Long Yi. He has written a soliloquy about her — a tragic, beautiful piece about a sister who watches from afar, her heart torn between love and duty, her shadow the only evidence of her devotion.

He has not performed it. He is waiting for the right audience.

Long Yi has heard of Count Catula. She is not impressed.

### Sir Cattenon — The Would-Be Rival (Unmet)

Sir Cattenon once attempted to challenge Longwei to a duel. Longwei did not understand the challenge. He simply purred, rolled over, and fell asleep in a sunbeam.

Long Yi has heard this story. She has laughed — a deep, rumbling, mountain-shaking laugh — for the first time in a century.

She likes Sir Cattenon. She has never met him. But anyone who was defeated by her brother's nap is, in her eyes, a friend.

- "You slept on a cloud again? Your back will ache!"
- "And why are you friends with a *cat*? They're so... small."

- (A silent flight over the farm, unnoticed, unthanked, unneeded — but present.)

- She sends telepathic scolds across mountains. Longwei has learned to ignore them. He has not learned to avoid them.
- She has a chipped horn. She does not speak of it. She does not need to.

## The Deeper Truth

Long Yi is afraid of being forgotten.

She is afraid that one day, Longwei will not come back to the mountains. That he will stay on the farm. That he will change form the way their parents did — not dying, but becoming something else, something unreachable, something she cannot follow. That she will lose him the way she lost them. Not to death. To distance. To change.

She is afraid that the welcome he has found — the warmth, the acceptance, the sunbeams — will replace the home he left behind. That she will become a memory. A story. A shadow on the moon.

She does not show this. She simply watches. She simply sends winds. She simply waits.

But every time Longwei returns to the mountains — every time he lands on the peak, every time he complains about the altitude, every time he asks what's for dinner — Long Yi feels a small, quiet reassurance.

He came back. He always comes back. He is her brother. He is home.

## Why She Belongs

Long Yi is the farm's necessary reminder that love is not always loud. She is the one who proves that protection is not always visible. She is the one who proves that the quietest love can move the mightiest mountains.

She is also the farm's reminder that family is not something you choose. It is something you are. And the choice to protect — to watch, to wait, to worry — is, in its own way, the most beautiful choice a dragon could ever make.

And the farm, in its infinite wisdom, does not know she exists. It does not need to. It is protected anyway.

It simply says: "Welcome. Come back anytime."

And he does.

## Status

Active. Watchful. Enormous. Either on the peaks of the Jade Cloud Range (scanning the horizon), or in the skies above the farm (unseen, unthanked, unneeded — but present), or in the quiet places between mountains (knitting scarves for baby dragons she has not yet met).

She is the dragon who watches over the dragon who believes he is a cat. And the farm, in its infinite wisdom, does not know she exists.

But she is there.

She is always there.
