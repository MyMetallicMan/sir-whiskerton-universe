# Sir Cattenon

*The Arrogant Meow Knight, the Duke of Delusion, and the Little Brother Still Waiting for the Bagpipes to Return. The Vanity.*

**Chinese Name:** 傲喵爵士 (Ào Miāo Juéshì) — "Arrogant Meow Knight"

---

## One-Line Essence

Sir Cattenon is the farm's vanity made visible. He is Sir Whiskerton's estranged brother — the one who heard the same bagpipes and learned the opposite lesson. He is pompous, proud, insufferable, and deeply, achingly *lonely.*

He is the farm's proof that pride is not a strength — it is a wound.

---

## Appearance

A sleek, well-fed cat with fur the color of polished copper — warm, shining, and always, always immaculate. His eyes are a sharp, pale blue — the color of winter sky, and just as cold.

He does not merely walk — he *proceeds.* He does not merely speak — he *orates.* He does not merely enter a room — he *arrives.*

He wears a tiny monocle of his own design — a bottle cap, carefully polished — and a small velvet cape (actually a scrap of old curtain) draped over one shoulder. He has never explained why. He believes that the mystery is part of his *presence.*

He is not dressed to be seen. He is dressed to be *worshipped.*

---

## Core Philosophy

**The Doctrine of the Noble Bloodline.**

Sir Cattenon believes that he is *royalty.* He believes that his father, MacTavish, was a *chieftain* — not a barn cat. He believes that his brother, Sir Whiskerton, is *pretending* to be a noble — when the real noble is *him.*

He believes that the world owes him *deference.* He believes that his presence is a *gift.* He believes — with every fiber of his being — that if he simply performs his nobility *well enough,* the world will finally recognize it.

His motto: *"If you cannot be the rock they build upon, be the glittering weathervane they cannot help but look at."*

---

## Role in the Universe

He is the farm's **Estranged Brother and Occasional Visitor.** He does not live on the farm. He visits — always unannounced, always inconvenient — and spends his time trying to outshine his brother, court attention from the farm animals, and generally create the impression that he is *above* this place.

He never quite succeeds.

He is the farm's reminder that the pompous cat is often the *lonely* cat — the one who is so busy performing that he has forgotten how to be.

---

## Personality

- **Pompous.** He does not ask for attention. He *demands* it. He does not enter a room. He *arrives.* He does not speak. He *proclaims.*
- **Insecure.** He is not arrogant *despite* his common origins. He is arrogant *because* of them. His pomp is a fortress against the fear of being unremarkable.
- **Secretly Admiring.** He admires Sir Whiskerton desperately. Whiskerton's quiet, earned authority is the one thing his performance cannot replicate. This admiration curdles into rivalry because to acknowledge it would be to admit his own title is made of tin.
- **Deeply Lonely.** He has no friends. He has no court. He has no place. He has only his performance — and the desperate hope that one day, someone will applaud.

---

## The Origin

Sir Cattenon and Sir Whiskerton share a single, unshakable foundation: the ghost of bagpipes. Their father, a massive, ginger Scottish Fold named **MacTavish,** was a barn cat with the soul of a Highland chieftain. He wore no collar, but in his mind, he wore a kilt. He didn't hunt mice; he "patrolled the borders." And every evening at dusk, he would sit on the stone wall and emit a low, rhythmic, buzzing purr that sounded, to his kittens, exactly like the drone of bagpipes carrying over misty hills.

This was the inheritance: a sense of profound, theatrical dignity about one's place in the world.

But the two brothers heard the same bagpipes and learned opposite lessons.

**Whiskerton** heard the steady drone — the constant, grounding note of duty, watchfulness, and quiet honor. It taught him to be the still center, the observer, the keeper of covenants.

**Cattenon** heard only the skirling, attention-seeking melody — the part that soars above the drone, demanding to be heard, celebrated, admired. It taught him that to be noble, one must be *seen* being noble.

The pivotal moment came when they were young cats. Their father, MacTavish, went on a "diplomatic mission" (chasing a particularly bold raccoon) and never returned. The bagpipes fell silent.

Whiskerton, the elder, felt the silence as a vacuum of responsibility. He became quieter, more watchful, adopting the farmer's porch as his new castle wall.

Cattenon, the younger, felt the silence as a void of audience. With no bagpipes to compete with, he decided he must *become* the bagpipes — louder, prouder, more magnificent. He didn't inherit a duty. He inherited a *role to play.*

---

## Key Relationships

### Sir Whiskerton — The Estranged Brother

Their rivalry is one of the deepest threads in Sir Whiskerton's story. Sir Whiskerton does not hate his brother. He *pities* him. Because he sees, beneath the bluster, a little brother still standing on the stone wall, trying to summon the ghosts of bagpipes and a father's approval.

Sir Cattenon, for his part, cannot admit — even to himself — that the brother he mocks is the brother he most wants to *be.*

### Count Catula — The Fellow Performer

Two theatrical cats. Two armor-clad souls. They do not get along — but they *recognize* each other. They see, in each other's performances, the same thing: the terror of being ordinary.

### The Farm Animals — The Unwilling Audience

Sir Cattenon has spent his entire life trying to impress the farm animals. He has failed. Repeatedly. Spectacularly. He has tried to duel geese, impress hens, outwit ducks, and challenge his brother. He has lost every single time.

He does not know how to stop.

### Gertrude the Goose — The Nemesis

Gertrude the Goose has, on multiple occasions, humiliated Sir Cattenon in front of the entire farm. She is unimpressed by his tinfoil armor. She is unimpressed by his twig scepter. She is unimpressed by his cape, which she once described as *"a napkin that has seen better days."*

Sir Cattenon both fears and *respects* Gertrude. She is, in his mind, the only creature on the farm who truly *sees* him — and refuses to play along.

## Catchphrases & Quirks

- *"Behold, peasants! I have arrived to grace you with my magnificence. Again."*
- *"Humility? A word invented by the unremarkable to cope with their dullness."*
- *"Bow before my glorious fluffiness!"*
- *"I would never wear a mustache that inelegant."*
- *(A theatrical pose, immediately before a spectacular failure.)*

He wears a bottle-cap monocle. He wears a napkin cape. He quotes Shakespeare, incorrectly. He attempts heroic deeds, unsuccessfully. He declares duels, and loses them. He arrives, always, at the wrong moment.

And yet — somehow — he always comes back.

---

## The Deeper Truth

Sir Cattenon is afraid of being *common.*

This is the deepest truth of his character. He has spent his entire life constructing an identity out of scraps and showmanship because the alternative — being *ordinary,* being *unremarkable,* being *just another cat* — is unbearable to him.

He is not a villain. He is not even a real rival. He is a *tragedy* wrapped in a cape. He is the little brother still standing on the stone wall, puffing out his chest and making a buzzing purr, trying to summon the ghosts of bagpipes and a father's approval, terrified that if he stops performing, no one will see him at all.

He is the farm's reminder that the loudest cats are often the loneliest ones.

---

## Why He Belongs

Sir Cattenon is the farm's necessary mirror of *vanity.* He reflects back, in exaggerated form, the pride of Doris, the drama of the Valley Chicks, even the performative cool of Jazzpurr. By being the *most* pompous, he makes everyone else's pretensions seem charming.

He is also the farm's reminder that even the most insufferable cats deserve a place. Because beneath the bluster, beneath the tinfoil, beneath the tragedy — there is a *brother.* A brother who is still trying to earn his father's love.

And the farm, in its infinite wisdom, does not reject him. It simply *watches.* It lets him perform. It lets him fail. And it waits — patiently, patiently — for the day he realizes that he does not need to perform to belong.

He simply needs to *be.*

---

## Signature Quote

*"Humility? A word invented by the unremarkable to cope with their dullness."*

*(And the unspoken, hidden counter-truth: "I would not know humility if I had it. I have never had the chance to learn.")*

Active. Pompous. Somewhere on the farm — strutting, monologuing, failing, and trying, always trying, to be seen.

He is the little brother still waiting for the bagpipes to return.

Jingle on.
