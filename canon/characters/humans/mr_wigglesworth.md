# Mr. Wigglesworth

**Title:** The Eccentric Farmhand-for-Hire / The Man with the Toolbox Full of Everything Except What You Need

**Core Philosophy:** "Trust me, I've been doing this for years!"

*(Spoiler: He hasn't.)*

---

## Role on the Farm

Mr. Wigglesworth is a farmhand-for-hire who arrives at the farm with a toolbox, a grin, and a confidence that is entirely unjustified by his actual abilities. He is a man who has, by his own count, worked on forty-seven farms. This number is suspect. He has also, by his own count, solved forty-seven farm problems. This number is *definitely* suspect.

He does not solve problems. He *transforms* them. A broken fence becomes an abstract sculpture. A clogged drain becomes a water feature. A collapsed shed becomes an open-air pavilion. Mr. Wigglesworth does not fix things. He *reimagines* them.

The animals have learned to recognize him from a distance. They have learned to hide. They have learned that the arrival of Mr. Wigglesworth is not a solution. It is a *plot twist*.

---

## Appearance

Mr. Wigglesworth is a wiry man with a scruffy beard, mismatched suspenders (one leather, one canvas, one occasionally a rope), and a hat that has seen better days. Possibly better decades. The hat is a battered brown fedora that may once have been elegant. It is no longer elegant. It is now *characterful*.

His toolbox is a legend. It is a battered green metal box, held together by rust and hope. It contains everything except what he actually needs at any given moment. It contains:

- Three hammers (none of them the right kind)
- A screwdriver with a handle made of twine
- A wrench that may or may not be a wrench
- A jar of something that he insists is "wood polish" but no one has ever seen him use
- A single glove
- A photograph of a dog no one has ever met
- A key that does not open anything on the farm
- A small, perfect, unexplained feather

No one has ever asked him about the contents. He has never offered to explain them.

---

## Personality

**Charmingly Scatterbrained.** Mr. Wigglesworth operates on a level of distraction that is almost impressive. He will begin a sentence about fencing and end it about the migratory patterns of geese. He will begin a project and end it... somewhere else. He does not notice this. He believes he is *focused*.

**Overly Confident.** He is confident in the way that a man who has never been wrong is confident. He is not a man who has never been wrong. He is a man who has never *noticed* he was wrong.

**Perpetually Optimistic.** Every failure is a *learning opportunity*. Every disaster is a *happy accident*. Every catastrophe is *exactly what was supposed to happen*. Mr. Wigglesworth does not have bad days. He has days that did not go according to plan, which is to say, *all days*.

**Speaks in Riddles.** He does not answer questions directly. He answers them *obliquely*. Asked what he is doing, he will say, "The question is not *what* I am doing, but *why* the doing is necessary." Asked if he has fixed the fence, he will say, "The fence is not broken. It is *waiting*. It is waiting to be *understood*." This is not helpful.

**Offers Bizarre Trades.** He does not work for money. He works for *trades*. He will fix your fence for three chickens and a dream. He will unclog your drain for a jar of honey and a story. He will rebuild your shed for a single, perfect apple and the promise that you will remember him. The animals have learned that his trades are always worse than they sound. They have also learned that refusing them is somehow impolite.

---

## His Work

Mr. Wigglesworth's work is not *bad*. It is *unexpected*. He does not do what you ask. He does what he believes you *meant* to ask. This is not the same thing.

His greatest hits include:

- **The Chicken Coop That Became a Gazebo.** He was asked to repair the roof. He removed the walls. "It has more *light* now," he explained. The chickens disagreed.
