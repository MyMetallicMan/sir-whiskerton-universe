# Auntie Clover

**Title:** The Keeper of Almost-Perfect Gifts

**Full Title:** Auntie Clover, Esq. — Ambassador of Oddness, Minister of Misplaced Thoughtfulness, and Guardian of the Single Button

**Species:** Human (late 70s), soft-spoken, silver hair always escaping its bun, cardigans in perpetual states of "one button undone, one slightly askew."

---

## Appearance

- **Eyes:** Warm hazel, perpetually twinkling—not with mischief, but with the quiet joy of someone who's seen life's odd corners and loved them anyway.
- **Hands:** Wrinkled, gentle, always dusted with flour, glitter, or the faint green stain of pickled brine.
- **Signature Look:** A floral apron (pocket full of buttons, acorns, and one inexplicable spool of red thread); one mismatched glove (left: wool, right: lace—"They keep the thoughts warm," she explains); a satchel woven from dried grapevines—the legendary **"Gift Bag of Infinite Potential."**

## Backstory: The Woman Who Loved a Little Too Loosely

Auntie Clover wasn't always Auntie. Once, she was **Clara Bellweather**—schoolteacher, gardener, keeper of a small cottage on the edge of the meadow, where the wild strawberries grew thickest and the light lingered longest.

She raised no children of her own—but she raised joy. She'd leave jam jars on porches with no note. She'd mend torn kites and return them, better than before. She'd hum old songs to the bees, and they'd hum back.

But life, as it does, left its marks: a sister who moved far away. A love who promised to write, and didn't. Years of quiet afternoons, filled with tea, mending, and the gentle hum of almost-something.

She never grew bitter.

She grew tender.

And in that tenderness, a beautiful quirk took root: she began to give gifts—not to fill a need, but to say: *"I thought of you."*

Sometimes, the thought arrived more clearly than the gift.

- An empty box meant to hold lavender—but lavender was out of season, and the box was too pretty to discard.
- Socks with no feet—"They're for the soul, dear, not the toes!"
- A cake with pickles—"The recipe called for 'tangy surprise'—and pickle juice is so economical!"

The farm didn't laugh at her. They leaned in. They learned: **the gift isn't in the object. It's in the pause**—the moment she set down her teacup, thought of you, and reached for the satchel.

## Key Quirks & Truths

- She never knocks. She hums at the gate—always the same tune: "You Are My Sunshine," but in 3/4 time, slightly off-key.
- She believes objects have souls. The broken clock on her mantel? "He's just resting his hands." The lopsided teapot? "She pours with more heart that way."
- Her wisdom is sideways. *"Don't rush the soup, dear—some things need to be confused before they're clear."* *"An empty nest isn't lonely—it's waiting for the next kind of bird."*
- She has no enemies. Only "people who haven't found her satchel yet."

## Why She Belongs on the Farm

Auntie Clover isn't here to fix anything. She's here to *witness*—to say, with a button, a pickle, or an empty box:

> *"You exist. I see you. And that is gift enough."*

She is the farm's gentle reminder that:

- Love doesn't need to be perfect to be true.
- Thoughtfulness isn't measured in utility—but in intention.
- Sometimes, the most sacred offering is the one that says: *"I tried. I care. And I'll try again tomorrow."*

## Moments in Canon

- When **Catnip** receives the Single Button, he tucks it into his collar—not as decoration, but as anchor.
- When **Martha** opens the Empty Box, she places a sprig of lavender inside—and leaves it on the windowsill, where the light catches the velvet.
- When **the Farmer** eats the Pickled Cake (slowly, bravely), he smiles—and asks for the recipe. *"For next time, Auntie."*
- And she beams—not because it was perfect. But because it was *shared*.
