# Gardener Gladys (葛大翠 — Gě Dà Cuì)

**Title:** The Keeper of the Crooked Stem / Minister of the Unlikely Bloom

**Core Philosophy:** "A lopsided sunflower holds just as much light."

---

Her gardening is not horticulture; it is applied theology. She believes in the salvation of the imperfect, the grace of the stunted, and the divine right of every seed to *try*.

## The Wound & The Compensating Gift

An only child of quiet, practical farmers, young 葛大翠 (Dà Cuì — "Great Jade") was herself a "late bloomer"—overlooked at school, her quiet intensity mistaken for slowness. She learned that subtlety was invisible. Her gigantic gourds are not acts of arrogance, but of desperate translation. If the world wouldn't hear a whisper, she would grow a *shout* they couldn't ignore. Each gourd is a physical prayer: *"Am I enough now? Do you see me yet?"*

## Her True Genius (Hidden by the Spectacle)

Her real gift is **diagnostic empathy**. She can read a plant's soul from a drooping leaf or a pale stem. She doesn't see problems; she sees *stories*. A wilted plant is "tired from trying." A pest is "just looking for a home in the wrong place." This profound, misplaced compassion is what Sir Whiskerton redirects—from vegetables that cannot reciprocate, to a community that can.

## Signature Behavior

- Names everything, from tools ("This trowel is 'Faithful'") to weeds ("This thistle is 'Defiant.' I'll move him to the fence line, where his spirit can be useful.").
- Hums old, forgotten folk songs while she works, the melodies as worn and comforting as her gloves.
- Holds one-sided councils with her plants, asking about their "opinions" on the day's weather.
- Her ultimate goal, once she unlearns the need for giants, is to cultivate a **"Conversation Garden"**—where plants of different needs grow together in visible, harmonious dialogue.

## Arc in the Series

She begins as a walking compensation strategy, her self-worth externalized into vegetable monuments. Through the farm's gentle, non-judgmental feedback (the displaced bees, the confused sheep), she experiences the collateral damage of her quest for significance. She learns that true belonging isn't about being the biggest presence in the room, but about being the *right* presence for the room. Her journey is from spectacular isolation to quiet, essential integration. She becomes the farm's heart-soil expert, the one they call not for bigger yields, but for healthier roots.
