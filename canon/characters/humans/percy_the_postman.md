# Percy the Postman (帕哆咪 — "Pa Duo Mi")

**Title:** The Trembling Courier / The Anxious Deliverer

**Core Philosophy:** "A letter is a promise. And promises must be kept — even if you're not entirely sure who made them, or to whom, or why."

---

## Role on the Farm

Percy is the farm's postman — a wiry, bespectacled man with trembling hands and a voice that quivers like a leaf in a high wind. He is dedicated to his job with an intensity that borders on the devotional. He believes — genuinely, deeply, without a shred of doubt — that the postal service is the *connective tissue of civilization*. Without it, he believes, we would all be alone.

This is a beautiful philosophy. It is also, unfortunately, frequently undermined by Percy's chronic nervousness.

Percy has misdelivered packages. He has misdelivered *letters*. He has, on one occasion, misdelivered a *sheep*. (This is not technically his job. This is not something he has ever explained.) He has dropped mail in puddles, in hedges, and — once, memorably — into the open mouth of a surprised duck.

But he keeps going. He keeps walking. He keeps delivering.

Because a letter is a promise. And Percy keeps his promises.

---

## Appearance

Percy is a wiry man with a permanently worried expression. He wears spectacles that are perpetually sliding down his nose, and a postal uniform that is always slightly too large for him. His hands tremble. His voice quivers. His knees have been known to knock.

He carries his mailbag as if it were a sacred object — which, to him, it is. It contains letters. It contains *lives*. It contains the small, fragile connections that hold the world together. He does not take this lightly.

He never takes this lightly.

---

## Personality

**Nervous.** Percy is nervous in the way that a rabbit is nervous — constantly, thoroughly, and with a strong inclination to flee. He jumps at loud noises. He flinches at sudden movements. He has been startled by his own reflection on at least three documented occasions.

**Dedicated.** He is the most dedicated postal worker in the county. Possibly the state. Possibly the country. He has never missed a delivery day. He has never left a letter undelivered. He has never once abandoned his route, no matter how lost he became, no matter how many wrong turns he took, no matter how many times he found himself in fields he had never seen, delivering letters to farms that may or may not exist.

**Anxious.** He worries. He worries about the letters. He worries about the recipients. He worries about whether he has the right address, whether the stamp is sufficient, whether the letter might contain bad news, whether the sender meant to send it, whether the recipient wants it. He worries about *everything*. It is exhausting. It is also, in its own way, beautiful.

**Secretly Brave.** Beneath the trembling hands and the quivering voice is a core of genuine, unshakeable courage. Percy has walked through storms. He has walked through the dark. He has walked through fields he was told were dangerous, because *someone had to deliver the mail*. He does not think of himself as brave. He simply does what needs to be done.

---

## His Route

Percy's route is, by any reasonable measure, impossible. It winds through the county in a way that defies cartography. It passes through fields that do not appear on any map. It visits farms that may or may not exist. It begins and ends at the same point, but no one — including Percy — is entirely sure where that point is.

He has been known to arrive at the farm from directions that do not make sense. He has been known to arrive before he left. He has been known to deliver the same letter twice, once before it was sent.

The farm has accepted this. The farm has accepted a lot about Percy.

---

## Key Relationships

**Millie the Milkmaid.** Percy and Millie are, in many ways, kindred spirits. They are both lost. They are both cheerful (or, in Percy's case, cheerfully anxious). They are both dedicated to their respective callings. They have passed each other on the road many times, each going in a direction the other was sure was wrong. They have never once questioned this. They simply wave. They simply smile. They simply continue.

**The Farmer.** The Farmer is one of Percy's most reliable recipients. This is not because Percy delivers to him reliably — it is because the Farmer does not care when the letters arrive. The Farmer leaves out a cup of tea for Percy on delivery days. Percy does not know if this is standard. He has never asked.

**Sir Whiskerton.** Sir Whiskerton has, on several occasions, intercepted Percy before he could misdeliver an important letter. Sir Whiskerton does not say anything. He simply sits on the letter until Percy notices. Percy does not question this. He has learned that sometimes the universe (or a cat) intervenes.

**Martha.** Martha writes letters. Percy delivers them. Martha is one of the few recipients Percy has never misdelivered to. This is not because he is more careful with her mail — it is because Martha is patient, and Martha does not mind. She once thanked Percy for a letter he had delivered to the wrong farm. She said it was the loveliest wrong delivery she had ever received. Percy cried.

---

## Catchphrases & Quirks

- "A letter is a promise. And promises must be kept."
- "I'm terribly sorry — I'm not sure I'm going the right way." (He is not. He never is. He always arrives anyway.)
- "The mail must go through!" (This is his mantra. He says it like a prayer.)
- He apologizes to the letters when he drops them.
- He has been known to deliver mail to the wrong farm, on the wrong day, in the wrong weather, and to be thanked for it anyway.
- He always knocks twice. He always waits. He always says "I'll just leave this here" even when he has not been asked to.
- He has never once failed to deliver a letter. This is technically true. It is also technically impossible.

---

## The Deeper Truth

Percy is afraid of failing. He is afraid of letting someone down. He is afraid that one day, he will deliver a letter too late, or too wrong, and someone will be hurt because of it.

He does not show this. He shows nervousness. He shows trembling hands. He shows a quivering voice and a perpetually worried expression.

But beneath all of it is a simple, unshakeable truth: Percy believes in what he does. He believes in the postal service. He believes in the connection between people. He believes that a letter is a promise — and that promises must be kept.

He has kept every promise he has ever made. Even the ones he did not know he was making.

---

## Why He Belongs

Percy is the farm's reminder that connection is a fragile thing, and that the people who hold it together are often the ones we overlook. He is the one who proves that dedication matters more than skill. He is the one who proves that a trembling hand can still deliver a perfect letter.

He is also the farm's reminder that the most important journeys are the ones we do not know we are taking — and that the most important deliveries are the ones that arrive exactly when they are needed, even if no one knows they were expected.

And the farm, in its infinite wisdom, does not tell Percy he is lost.

It simply waits for him to arrive.
