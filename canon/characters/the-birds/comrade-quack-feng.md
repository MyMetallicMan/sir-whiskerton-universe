# Comrade Quack Feng

*The Revolutionary of Progress. Auditor of Inefficiency. Visionary of Utopian Ducks. The Farm's Unheeded Prophet.*

**Chinese Name:** (Not yet assigned in source material — awaiting official translation)

---

## One-Line Essence

Comrade Quack Feng is the farm's *revolutionary*. He believes the farm is a "pre-revolutionary lethargy" to be shaken off. He files reports on "suboptimal emotional resource allocation." He is forever one missed quota away from realizing that the paradise he seeks is already here.

---

## Appearance

A determined-looking duck, perpetually upright and alert. He is never seen without his iconic **red armband** — a symbol of his self-appointed revolutionary duty — and a weathered **clipboard** clutched tightly under one wing.

His gaze is intense, analytical, and perpetually disappointed by the unoptimized world around him. He walks with purpose. He speaks with conviction. He believes, with every fiber of his being, that the farm can be **better** — if only everyone would follow the plan.

He does not smile. But occasionally, when an efficiency drive succeeds, the corner of his beak twitches in what might be approval.

---

## Core Philosophy

> *"Progress Demands Sacrifice. Joy Is a Distraction Until the Paradise Is Built."*

Comrade Quack Feng is a true revolutionary, driven by a utopian vision. He believes that through strict unity, radical order, and relentless planning, the farm can transcend its animal nature and achieve a state of **perfected, harmonious efficiency** — a paradise on earth.

He sees frivolity, spontaneity, and individual quirks not as charms, but as **fatal inefficiencies to be eradicated**.

His tragedy is that his vision is so rigidly perfect that it has no room for the imperfect, beautiful reality of the farm. He mistakes love for laxity and compassion for counter-revolutionary sentiment.

---

## Skills & Specializations

### The Auditor of Reality

He constantly measures the farm's activities against his own detailed, unrealistic standards. His clipboard contains metrics for everything from **"Seed-Scatter Efficacy"** to **"Emotional Labor Output."**

### The Questioner

He believes no tradition is sacred if it doesn't serve The Goal. He will interrogate the purpose of a purr, the productivity of a sunbeam nap, and the ROI of a shared biscuit.

### The Five-Year Plan

He has drafted multiple five-year plans for the farm's development. None of them have been implemented. He has drafted revisions. The revisions are longer.

### The Efficiency Sprint

A recurring event he organizes. The animals attend out of politeness, then immediately go take a nap in the exact way he just denounced.

### Corrective Citation Tickets

He issues tickets for observed inefficiencies. They are usually used as nesting material or to patch holes in the hayloft.

---

## Origin

No one is sure when Quack Feng arrived or what ignited his fervor. Some whisper he read a single, water-damaged political tract and never recovered. He appointed himself the farm's revolutionary conscience, declaring that its gentle chaos was not peace, but a **"pre-revolutionary lethargy"** to be shaken off.

He has been trying to shake it off ever since. The farm has been gently declining to be shaken.

---

## Key Relationships

### The Farmer & Martha — The Ultimate Symbols

The Farmer and Martha are the ultimate symbols of the "unplanned leadership" he seeks to reform. He files lengthy reports on their "suboptimal emotional resource allocation." Martha saves him the crustiest bread. The Farmer tips his hat.

### Sir Whiskerton — The Outdated Aristocrat

Quack Feng views Sir Whiskerton as a figure of outdated, aristocratic leisure. Sir Whiskerton treats Quack Feng's manifestos with the same solemn respect he gives Lucifer's — a containment strategy.

### Doris the Hen — The Natural Ally

Doris is a natural, if dramatic, ally in bureaucracy. However, even she finds his lack of flair for the dramatic in paperwork to be lacking.

### Mr. Waddle — The Fellow Bureaucrat

Quack Feng and Mr. Waddle share a passion for systems. They disagree on methods—Quack Feng is a revolutionary, Mr. Waddle is an inspector—but they understand each other's need to measure, categorize, and improve.

### The General Population — The Tolerant Audience

The general population of the farm sees him as a mildly amusing, mostly harmless part of the scenery. A duck shouting at the sky to form a committee.

They humor his five-year plans and productivity drives, not out of belief, but out of a deep, unspoken understanding: **his fierce, misguided care is still a form of belonging**. They attend his "Efficiency Sprints" and then immediately go take a nap in the exact way he just denounced. He is a living stress test for the farm's principle of Belonging Without Earning — they let him stay, clipboard and all, even though he hasn't earned an ounce of their compliance.

## Catchphrases & Quirks

- *"You are not a community! You are a disorganized collective of biomass with a snack problem! We must have a plan!"*
- *"Progress demands sacrifice!"*
- *"The paradise is not yet built! And you are eating a biscuit!"*
- *"Efficiency! Order! The Plan!"*

He has a favorite pen — a small, red-tipped quill — that he uses for signing reports. He has a favorite chair — a specific stump near the barn — where he sits to observe "productivity." He has a favorite time of day — dawn — when he believes the day's potential is at its highest.

He refers to naps as *"unauthorized downtime."* He refers to snacks as *"unplanned caloric events."* He refers to joy as *"the opium of the animals."*

He keeps a journal of every inefficiency he has observed. The journal is now four volumes long. Volume Five is in progress.

---

## The Tender Truth

Comrade Quack Feng is not a villain. He is not even truly unreasonable. He is, in his own way, **a revolutionary of love**.

He wants the farm to be **better**. He wants the animals to be **happier**. He wants the world to be **perfect**. His tragedy is not that he is wrong about what he wants. It is that he cannot see that the paradise he seeks is **already here** — operating on an economy of joy he refuses to audit.

He is the farm's unheeded prophet. And like all prophets, he is both ridiculous and necessary. He reminds the farm that **there is always more to do**. That perfection is a horizon, not a destination. That the pursuit of a better world, however flawed, is still a form of hope.

And the farm, in its infinite wisdom, does not silence him. It lets him shout at the sky. It lets him draft his five-year plans. It lets him issue his corrective citation tickets. Because the farm understands that **even the prophets need a place to belong**.

---

## Why He Belongs

Comrade Quack Feng is the farm's **necessary friction**. He is the one who reminds everyone that **order is not the enemy of joy**. That structure can be a form of care. That the desire to make things better is not, in itself, a flaw.

He also reminds the farm that **efficiency without wisdom is just noise**. And that sometimes, the most productive act is a quiet plan.

And in the end — someday, perhaps — Quack Feng will realize that the paradise he seeks is already here. And on that day, he will put down his clipboard. He will sit on his favorite stump. He will watch the sun set over the farm. And he will smile.

Not because the farm has finally become perfect. But because he has finally **seen** that it was perfect all along.
